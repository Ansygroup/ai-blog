Anthropic's model naming used to be simple: one Claude, then a number. In late 2026 the lineup reads like a family tree: the Claude 4 generation that defined the company's breakout year, the 5.5 tier that took over the frontier slot, and the brand-new Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 released on November 9, 2026, weeks before Anthropic's IPO. So what is "Claude 4" now? It is the settled, mid-priced workhorse generation, and after a year of using it daily, we have a clear picture of what it is actually good at.

## What it is

Claude 4 is the model family that turned Anthropic from a safety-research lab with a chatbot into the default model for serious work: Sonnet for speed and price, Opus for the heavy reasoning, Haiku for cheap volume. It powered the first wave of genuinely useful coding agents, and it is why "Claude" became a verb in developer circles. In November 2026 you reach it through claude.ai, the API, and half the tools you already use, from Copilot's model picker to Perplexity's model table.

## What it is genuinely good at

Long-form reasoning with restraint. Claude 4's signature trait is that it says "I don't know" and "that premise is wrong" more readily than its rivals, which makes it the model you want reviewing a contract, auditing an argument, or reading a 200-page document for the one clause that matters. Coding is the other standout: its multi-file reasoning and its willingness to ask before making sweeping changes made it the backbone of agentic coding tools, a position it still holds even after newer models shipped.

Writing quality is the quieter win. Claude 4's prose is the least "AI-flavored" of the major models: fewer filler transitions, less reflexive hedging, better rhythm. People who edit AI text for a living consistently rank its first drafts as needing the least de-roboticizing.

## A year of daily use: three specific memories

Concrete examples say more than adjectives. First: a contract review where we pasted a 40-page vendor agreement and asked for the clauses that could hurt us. Claude 4 found the auto-renewal trap and the liability cap, quoted both, and flagged that the indemnification language was asymmetric. A rival model summarized the contract cheerfully and missed all three. Second: a refactoring session where we asked it to modernize a legacy module. It refused to touch two files until we confirmed their behavior was intentional, which felt pedantic until we realized one of them contained a workaround production depended on. Third: a weekly newsletter draft where its rewrites needed almost no voice correction, while competing models kept producing sentences we would never say out loud.

The pattern across all three: Claude 4 behaves less like an eager intern and more like a senior colleague who has been burned before. That personality is the product.

## Where it fails

Speed and spectacle. Claude 4 is not the fastest model to respond, it does not generate images or video, and its consumer app has historically lagged rivals on novelty features. Its usage limits are the most common complaint: the free tier is genuinely tight, and even paid users hit the rolling five-hour and weekly caps during heavy agent sessions. And while it is excellent at saying what it cannot do, that conservatism occasionally becomes refusal theater on harmless requests.

The other failure is shelf life. With Opus 5.5 holding the frontier slot and Fable 5.2 cutting prices at the top end, Claude 4 is no longer the model you buy for bragging rights or for the hardest reasoning tasks. It is the model you buy for reliability at a sane price.

## Pricing in plain terms

As of November 2026: Free at $0 with limited usage, Pro at $20 a month or $17 billed annually ($200 up front), and Max at $100 or $200 a month, which now bundles $100 or $200 of monthly API credits respectively, a genuinely good deal if you also build on the Claude Platform. Access to the newest Fable model is metered through usage credits on paid plans rather than being flat-out included. Team and Enterprise tiers add collaboration, search, and compliance features. The hidden cost to watch is the usage cap shape: limits reset every five hours and weekly, so a Max plan buys headroom, not infinity.

## Who should skip it

Skip Claude 4 if you need one subscription that also does images, video, and voice; ChatGPT and Gemini are broader consumer packages. Skip it if your usage is occasional and shallow, because the free tiers of its rivals answer casual questions just as well. And skip the older 4-series API models for new agent builds, where the 5-series successors now win on both capability and, after the November price moves, cost.
