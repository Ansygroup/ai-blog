One of these tools fixes your commas. The other will happily rewrite your paragraph, your argument, and, if you let it, your [voice](/posts/ai-voice-search-seo-keyword-clustering-boost-rankings-2026). Grammarly and Claude both get pitched as "AI for better writing," but after editing the same pile of drafts through both, the honest framing is different: Grammarly is a proofreader that lives where you type, and Claude is a ghostwriter on call. Choosing wrong means either under-using a subscription or quietly publishing prose that no longer sounds like you.

| Scenario | Winner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleaning typos and [grammar](/posts/grammarly-review-2026-test-pricing-alternatives) everywhere you type | Grammarly | Works inside [email](/posts/best-ai-powered-influencer-outreach-email-templates-2026), [docs](/posts/ai-code-doc-generators-python-javascript-2026), browsers, and apps |
| Reworking a weak draft's structure | Claude | Reasons about argument, not just sentences |
| Keeping your own voice intact | Grammarly | Suggestions, not rewrites, by default |
| Matching a specific [style](/posts/review-best-ai-powered-brand-voice-checkers-2026) guide | Claude | Paste the guide, get edits against it |
| Team rollout with admin controls | Grammarly | Enterprise [tier](/posts/copilot-free-tier-limits-2026) with DLP and BYOK |
| Budget under $20 a month | Grammarly | Pro at $12 vs Claude Pro at $20 |

## The proofreader

Grammarly in late 2026 is simpler than it used to be: Free at $0 with 100 AI [prompts](/posts/copy-ai-prompting-guide-2026) a month, Pro at $12 a month with 2,000 prompts per member, and Enterprise, now delivered through Superhuman Go after the two companies merged, with unlimited prompts, BYOK encryption, and [data](/posts/ai-for-generating-schema-markup-for-seo-2026-tutorial) loss [prevention](/posts/ai-agents-aggressively-hack-us-canada-2026-guide). Its strength has never been raw intelligence. It is coverage: the suggestions appear in your email client, your browser, your docs, your phone, at the moment you make the mistake. Tone detection, full-sentence rewrites, plagiarism and AI-detection checks round it out. It will not rescue a bad argument, but it will catch the embarrassing stuff every single time, which is what most professional writing actually needs.

## The ghostwriter

Claude is a general reasoning [model](/posts/auto-ml-tutorial), and editing is one of the things it is quietly best at. Paste a draft and a style guide and it will restructure sections, flag logical gaps, cut filler, and propose three alternative openings, with explanations you can argue with. Claude Pro costs $20 a month or $17 billed annually as of November 2026, and the free tier covers light use. The catch is that Claude works in its own window. There is no red underline following you around the internet; you have to bring the text to it.

## Where each one fails

Grammarly fails at [depth](/posts/gamma-review-2026-ai-presentation-tool-tested-in-depth). Its rewrites optimize for correctness and tone, and on anything ambitious (a persuasive essay, an opinion piece with real texture) its suggestions sand off exactly the edges that make the writing interesting. Its AI detection and plagiarism [features](/posts/cursor-api-cost-comparison-2026) also produce false positives often enough that you should treat them as flags, not verdicts.

Claude fails at discipline, yours. Ask it to "improve" a draft and it will rewrite more than you asked for, in a smooth, slightly generic register that creeps into everything it touches. Writers who use it daily learn to constrain the ask: "flag problems, do not rewrite" produces an [editor](/posts/cursor-editor-deep-dive-2026); "make this better" produces a co-author. It also has no persistent memory of your style guide between sessions unless you set up a Project, so occasional users re-explain their rules a lot.

## A week of identical drafts through both

We ran the same five drafts through both tools: a client proposal, a [performance review](/posts/suno-vs-chatgpt-2026), a newsletter issue, a technical explainer, and an apology email. Grammarly caught 100 percent of the mechanical [errors](/posts/fixing-undefined-errors-ai-apps) in all five within seconds, and its tone warnings correctly flagged the apology email as sounding defensive. It contributed nothing to the proposal's weak pricing justification, because that is not a sentence problem. Claude found the weak justification in one pass, proposed a restructure of the explainer that cut 300 words without losing [content](/posts/ai-powered-content-gap-analysis-niche-blog-topics-2026), and suggested a stronger opening for the newsletter. It also missed two genuine typos, because that is not what it is for. Neither result was surprising, but seeing it quantified on identical text makes the division of labor obvious.

One more practical [note](/posts/top-notebooklm-alternatives-2026-best-ai-note-taking-tools): Grammarly's suggestions are one-click accepts, which makes it fast and also makes it easy to accept changes you should have rejected. Claude's suggestions require you to retype or paste them, which is friction that accidentally protects your voice.

## The combination that actually works

The strongest writing [workflow](/posts/best-ai-copy-tools-for-translators-2026) we tested uses both, in sequence: draft with whatever you like, run Claude for the structural pass ("what is weak in this argument, do not rewrite yet"), revise yourself, then let Grammarly do the final mechanical sweep where you actually publish. Total cost, $32 a month. That sounds like a lot until you price one embarrassed client email. ## Which should you pick

If you write competently and want an always-on safety net, Grammarly Pro at $12 is the easiest subscription to justify in the category. If your drafts fail higher up the stack (structure, logic, persuasion), Claude Pro is the better spend, with the standing rule that you stay the author and it stays the critic. If you can only have one, pick based on where your drafts actually die: sentences, or thinking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Grammarly or Claude better for editing?

Grammarly is better for mechanical correctness everywhere you type. Claude is better at structural and argumentative feedback on drafts you paste into it. Many [writers](/posts/best-ai-tools-non-native-english-writers-2026) use both in sequence.

### How much does Grammarly cost in 2026?

As of November 2026, Grammarly has a Free tier, Pro at $12 a month with 2,000 AI prompts per member, and an Enterprise tier delivered through Superhuman Go.

### How much does Claude Pro cost?

Claude Pro is $20 a month or $17 a month billed annually, as of November 2026, with a free tier for light use.

### Will Claude change my writing voice?

It can. Unconstrained improve requests tend to flatten prose into a generic register. Asking Claude to flag problems without rewriting keeps you the author.
