OpenAI's help center describes a Meetings plugin for ChatGPT that takes notes for you and saves summaries with action items. This article summarizes that help page, linked at the end, which was marked "Updated: yesterday" when we read it. We have not used the plugin, so everything below is OpenAI's description of a beta feature that may change.

## What does the Meetings plugin do?

According to OpenAI, Meetings takes notes for online calls or in-person conversations "without a bot joining the call." It saves a personalized summary and suggested action items in ChatGPT Space. You can add your own notes to the same page, ask ChatGPT questions about the meeting, share the page, or start suggested action items with one click, such as drafting an email or updating a project plan.

Summaries use the transcript, your notes and context from connected apps you have authorized. They are generated with ChatGPT Work.

## Who can use it?

- **Plans:** beta in the ChatGPT desktop app on macOS for all Pro plans and all Business plans.
- **Other platforms:** iOS, Android and Windows support is "coming soon", per the page.
- **Enterprise:** not generally available. OpenAI is testing it with a limited group through an alpha program and tells customers to ask their account manager.
- **Business workspaces:** ChatGPT Space and the Meetings plugin must both be enabled by the workspace.

OpenAI notes that Meetings is separate from the Record feature in the older ChatGPT desktop app.

## How do you set it up?

Open the Mac app, open Plugins in the sidebar, install Meetings, then go through onboarding and allow microphone and system audio access. A calendar connection is optional. It adds reminders and a list of upcoming meetings through the Google Calendar or Outlook Calendar plugin, and you can always start notes manually.

## What about consent?

This is the part to read carefully. OpenAI says to tell everyone in the meeting that you are taking notes and get their consent before you start. The reminder in the app "is visible to you" and "does not notify other participants or obtain their consent for you." Because no bot joins, other participants will not see anything unless you tell them. Recording rules differ by place and by employer, so check yours.

## What happens to the audio and the notes?

- Meetings streams microphone and system audio from your Mac to OpenAI to generate notes.
- Once notes are ready, OpenAI says the audio is deleted from your Mac and its servers and cannot be replayed. If you are offline or processing is incomplete, audio may stay on your Mac temporarily until it uploads.
- Meeting pages are private by default. If you share one, others can see the notes but not the transcript.
- OpenAI advises reviewing important details before relying on or sharing the notes.

## When does it start and stop?

The plugin can remind you to start notes for calendar meetings, and can detect that you are in a call and offer to take notes even if it is not on your calendar. It reminds you to stop if no audio is detected for a few minutes. It also stops on its own when you close your laptop lid, when there is no audio for an extended period, or when a session reaches four hours. Notes appear a few minutes after you stop, once the audio finishes uploading.

For missing transcript content, OpenAI says both permissions are needed: the microphone for your voice and people near your Mac, and system audio for other participants on an online call.

## Our take

The useful comparison is with note-taking bots that announce themselves. Meetings is quieter, which is convenient and also puts the whole consent burden on you. The audio-deletion statement and private-by-default pages are reassuring, but they are OpenAI's own claims. A practical habit is to say at the start of each call that you are taking notes, and to check the summary before you send out any action item it suggests.
