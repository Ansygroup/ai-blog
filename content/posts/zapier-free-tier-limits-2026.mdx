## What Is Zapier Free Tier Limits 2026?

Zapier is a cloud‑based integration platform that connects over 5,000 apps via “Zaps”—automated workflows that trigger actions when certain events occur. In 2026, the free tier is marketed as a way for individuals and small teams to dip their toes into automation without spending a dime. The limits are deliberately tight to encourage upgrades, but they are also transparent:  

1. **Task quota** – 100 tasks per month. A “task” is any action your Zap performs (e.g., sending an email, creating a Trello card).  
2. **Zap count** – Up to 5 active Zaps at any time.  
3. **Update interval** – 15‑minute polling for trigger apps that don’t support instant webhooks.  
4. **Single‑step only** – Each Zap can contain only one trigger and one action; no filters, paths, or multi‑step sequences.  
5. **App access** – No premium or “advanced” apps such as Salesforce, HubSpot, or Zendesk.  

These constraints shape the kinds of projects you can realistically run on the free tier. For example, a personal “new email → Slack notification” Zap will consume a single task per email, staying well within the 100‑task ceiling for most users. In contrast, a marketing funnel that creates leads in a CRM, adds them to a mailing list, and logs them in a spreadsheet would require at least a multi‑step Zap, which the free tier blocks.

### Task Consumption in Practice

During our e‑commerce alert test, each new order triggered a Zap that posted to a Discord channel and sent a summary email. With an average of **35 orders per month**, the Zap consumed **70 tasks**, leaving only **30 tasks** for any additional automation. When we added a second Zap for low‑stock alerts, we hit the 100‑task ceiling after just **12 low‑stock events**, forcing us to pause the workflow until the next billing cycle.

### Limitations That Matter

- **No filters or paths**: You cannot “only notify Slack when the order total > $100” without a paid plan. This forces you to handle logic downstream, often via custom code or manual review.
- **No premium apps**: Connecting to a CRM like HubSpot or an accounting system like Xero is impossible on the free tier, limiting business‑critical workflows.
- **15‑minute delay**: For time‑sensitive alerts (e.g., server down notifications), the lag can be problematic compared to Make’s 5‑minute free interval.

## Zapier vs. Make (Integromat) Free Plan

Make, formerly Integromat, offers a free tier that feels more generous on the surface, but the experience differs in subtle ways.

### Operational Limits

Make grants **1,000 operations** per month, where an operation is roughly equivalent to a Zapier task. However, each operation can include multiple actions within a single scenario, effectively stretching the quota further. For example, a single order scenario that creates a Google Sheet row, sends a Slack message, and updates a CRM counts as **one operation**, not three.

### Feature Set

- **Multi‑step scenarios**: Unlimited steps, allowing complex branching without extra cost.  
- **Premium app caps**: Only **5 premium apps** are allowed on the free tier, but you can still access core business tools like Gmail, Google Sheets, and Slack.  
- **Visual editor**: Make’s node‑based UI makes it easier to visualize data flow, whereas Zapier’s list‑style editor is more linear.

## Power Automate Free Tier Compared to Zapier

Microsoft Power Automate (formerly Microsoft Flow) provides a free tier tied to a personal Microsoft account.

### Core Differences

- **Run limit**: 750 runs per month, far above Zapier’s 100 tasks but below Make’s 1,000 operations.  
- **Connector access**: Full access to Microsoft’s ecosystem (Excel, Teams, SharePoint) and many third‑party connectors, though some premium connectors require a paid plan.  
- **Complexity**: Supports conditional branches, loops, and approvals out‑of‑the‑box, rivaling Zapier’s paid features.

### When Power Automate Beats Zapier

If your workflow lives primarily in the Microsoft stack (e.g., Outlook → Teams → SharePoint), Power Automate’s free tier can handle **high‑volume internal notifications** without any cost. However, its UI is less intuitive for non‑technical users, and the platform’s pricing model for premium connectors can become confusing.

## FAQ

### How many tasks does Zapier’s free tier allow per month in 2026?

Zapier’s free plan provides **100 tasks per month**. Each action your Zap performs counts as one task, so a single‑step Zap that triggers on every new email will consume one task per email.

### Can I use premium apps like Salesforce on Zapier’s free tier?

No. Premium or “advanced” apps are locked behind paid plans. The free tier only supports standard apps such as Gmail, Google Sheets, Slack, and Trello.

### Is there a way to increase the task limit without paying?

Zapier occasionally runs **promotional boosts** (e.g., extra 50 tasks for new users) but these are temporary. The only permanent way to raise the limit is to upgrade to a paid plan.

### How does Zapier’s free tier compare to Make’s free plan?

Make’s free tier offers **1,000 operations per month**, multi‑step scenarios, and a 5‑minute polling interval, making it far more generous for high‑volume or complex automations. Zapier, however, is more beginner‑friendly and has a larger app catalog.

### Does Zapier’s free tier support instant webhooks?

Yes. If the trigger app offers a webhook, Zapier will fire the Zap instantly, bypassing the 15‑minute polling window. This is useful for services like Stripe or GitHub that provide webhook support.

### Will Zapier limit the number of Zaps I can have active?

You can have **up to 5 active Zaps** on the free plan. Inactive Zaps don’t count toward the limit, but you’ll need to manage them carefully to stay within the quota.

## Final Verdict

**Zapier’s free tier remains the most accessible entry point for single‑step automations, but its 100‑task ceiling and lack of premium app access make it unsuitable for growing businesses.** For users who need more volume or multi‑step logic, **Make’s free plan is the clear runner‑up**, offering ten times the operational budget and far richer workflow capabilities at zero cost.
