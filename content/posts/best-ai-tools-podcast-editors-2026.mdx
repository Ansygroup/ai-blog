This list is for people who edit podcasts for other people, not for hosts editing their own show. That changes the math. A host cares about one episode a week. A freelance editor with eight clients cares about turnaround, consistent loudness across shows, and how many hours of tedious cleanup are left after the tool has finished. AI helps by removing the first three hours of every project.

Prices below were read on vendor pages on November 27, 2026. Last tested: November 27, 2026. This list is written for client editors and is distinct from the host-focused [best AI tools for podcast editing](/posts/best-ai-tools-for-podcast-editing).

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Editing talk shows from recorded files | Descript | Edit by transcript, plans at $16, $24 and $50 |
| Clients who record remote interviews | Riverside | Local recording, free tier, Pro $29 monthly or $24 annual |
| Fixing a flubbed line without a re-record | ElevenLabs | Plans from $6 monthly; only with client consent |
| Show notes, titles and chapters | Claude Pro | $17 annual or $20 monthly |

## Descript: the default for talk shows

Descript's paid tiers were $16, $24 and $50 on the page read. Its core idea is that deleting words in a transcript deletes the audio. For an interview-heavy show, that cuts the first pass from an hour to minutes. Filler word removal is a click, though a human ear should still decide which ums to keep, since removing all of them makes speech sound robotic.

Where it fails: overlapping speech. When two people talk at once, the transcript is wrong and the cut is rough. Budget manual time for lively conversations. Read the [Descript review](/posts/descript-review-2026-top-all-in-one-audio-video-editor) for more on the full tool, or compare it with its rivals in [Descript vs Riverside vs Zencastr](/posts/descript-vs-riverside-vs-zencastr-best-ai-podcast-editing-tool).

## Riverside: fix the source file

Riverside's page lists a free tier at $0, Pro at $29 monthly or $24 annual, Grow at $39 monthly or $34 annual, and a Webinar tier at $99 monthly or $79 annual. Its value to an editor is upstream: when a client records locally on each side, you receive clean separate tracks instead of a compressed call recording.

The best AI for an editor is often a better recording. If you can persuade clients to switch to local recording, your editing hours per episode fall more than any plug-in will achieve.

Where it fails: you do not control the client's setup. Include a one-page recording guide in your onboarding to keep microphones and rooms consistent.

## ElevenLabs: the repair tool with strict rules

ElevenLabs listed $6, $22, $99 and $299 monthly, or $60, $220, $990 and $2,990 annually. Voice cloning can patch a mispronounced word or a missing line. That is useful and risky. Get written permission from the host before cloning a voice, and disclose any synthetic patch to the client. Never use it on a guest's voice.

## Claude for show notes and chapters

Claude Pro was $17 annual or $20 monthly. Feed in the transcript and ask for chapter markers with timestamps, five title options and a 120-word summary. Always check the timestamps against the audio, since a model can place a chapter marker a minute off.

## What the vendor pages do not say

A freelance editor's real constraint is not software cost. It is revision rounds. Clients who send vague feedback ("make it punchier") can burn unpaid hours. Put the number of revisions in your contract, charge for extra rounds, and send a transcript with timestamps so feedback points at specific moments.

Pricing also deserves a look. If your tool saves you 3 hours per episode, do not cut your per-episode price by that amount. Price on the outcome, which is a finished episode, not on the hours.

## A quality check before every delivery

Before you send a file, run the same five checks every time: loudness matches the client's previous episodes, no clipped peaks, the intro and outro are the right versions, the first and last 10 seconds are clean, and the show notes name every guest correctly. Misspelled guest names are the most common complaint an editor gets, and the cheapest to prevent. A short checklist in your delivery template turns this into a habit rather than a memory test, and it is the part of the job no tool does for you.

## Who should skip this list

If you edit one show in a single DAW and already work fast, adding a transcript editor may only change your workflow without saving time. Test on one episode before moving a client.

## What to buy first

Pick Descript for one month and edit two real episodes with it, tracking time before and after. Move clients who record remotely onto Riverside's free or Pro tier. Add ElevenLabs only when a client asks for repairs and consents in writing. Claude Pro can wait until show notes become the slowest step.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which tool saves a podcast editor the most time?

Descript for transcript editing, and Riverside for cleaner source files. Better recordings often save more hours than any editing feature.

### Is it acceptable to patch a host's voice with AI?

Only with the host's written permission and disclosure to the client. Never do it on a guest's voice.

### How should an editor price work when tools save time?

Price on the finished episode, not on hours, and limit revision rounds in the contract.
