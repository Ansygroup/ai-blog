About one in four American employer businesses says it uses AI. The number is more interesting than it sounds, because of how it moved: from 17.3 percent in late November 2025 to 24.2 percent in the survey period covering early October 2026, according to an analysis of the Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey files published on 11 November 2026.

That is a real shift in a national survey of employer businesses, not a vendor poll. It is also the number most small business owners should be looking at when they decide whether they are early or late.

## What the survey asked

The Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS) asks a national sample of employer businesses whether they used AI in any business function. The core question was revised in November 2025 to ask about use "in any business function", and a second AI supplement began on 17 November 2025, according to the analysis.

For the period collected from 19 October to 1 November 2026, referring to the two weeks from 5 to 18 October, the results were:

| Response | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Yes, used AI in any business function | 24.2% |
| No | 61.9% |
| Do not know | 13.9% |

The standard error on the 24.2 percent figure is 0.61 percentage points. The previous period, 5 to 18 October, showed 24.6 percent yes and 9.6 percent "do not know".

## The trend line

- November 2025: 17.3 percent yes, under the revised question.
- December 2025 to May 2026: "hovered between 17% and 20%", per a Census Bureau story of 26 May 2026, with 19.8 percent as of 3 May 2026.
- October 2026: 24.2 percent.

Two things could explain part of the jump: genuine adoption, and the survey's own wording. The analysis notes that the questionnaire now labels the technology "Super Intelligence" rather than "Artificial Intelligence", following the September 2026 executive order directing federal agencies to use that term, and that the files do not say when the wording changed or whether estimates before and after it are comparable.

That caveat matters. A business owner who does not think of their tools as "AI" may answer differently depending on the label on the question.

## What businesses say they use it for

The October 2026 file carries three additional questions for the first time:

- 26.8 percent said their employees had used generative AI to assist with work-related tasks.
- Of businesses answering the headcount question, 6.6 percent said AI use had increased total headcount in the last six months, 2.5 percent said it had decreased it, and 87.3 percent reported no change.
- For new hires, 2.5 percent reported an increase, 3.3 percent a decrease, and 90.2 percent no change.

Read those three together and the picture is not a jobs story. It is a tools story. Roughly a quarter of employer businesses have someone using generative AI at work, and the overwhelming majority report no change in headcount either way.

## What the number does not tell you

The survey counts businesses that report use. It does not measure how many workers use AI, how often, or whether it works. It also does not separate a two-person landscaping firm using a writing tool from a manufacturer running models in production.

That distinction is the one that matters for anyone making a budget decision. A 24 percent adoption rate is consistent with both "most of this is casual" and "a meaningful minority has rebuilt a process". The survey cannot tell you which.

## Why it matters if you run a small business

Three practical readings:

1. Not adopting is now the majority position, so you are not late. 61.9 percent said no.
2. The 27.6 percent who said they expect to use AI in the next six months means your competitors are mostly planning rather than doing.
3. The "do not know" share, 13.9 percent, is larger than the entire change since 2025. Some of the growth in "yes" is probably clarity, not adoption.

For a business deciding where to start, the survey supports the boring answer: pick one repeated task, use a tool on it for a month, and measure whether the task got faster. The national numbers will not tell you whether your version worked.

## What to watch

The next two BTOS releases will show whether 24 percent is a new level or a spike. If the share holds through the winter, the interesting question becomes the gap between businesses that report use and businesses that report a change in how they work. The survey's new headcount questions are the first attempt to measure that gap, and one period is not a trend.

The underlying files are published on the [Census Bureau's BTOS data page](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/data). The analysis of the October 2026 period, including the wording change, is published by [theaio.org](https://theaio.org/assets/papers/aio-s0022.c695576f.pdf).

## The judgment call

Treat 24 percent as a floor on experimentation and a ceiling on nothing. The useful question for a small business is not whether peers have adopted AI, it is which single process in your week is repetitive enough to test. The survey says most businesses have not answered that question yet, which is either a warning or an opening, depending on how fast you move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What share of US businesses use AI?

24.2 percent of employer businesses reported using AI in any business function in the survey period covering early October 2026, with 61.9 percent saying no and 13.9 percent saying they do not know.

### Is AI adoption among small businesses growing?

Yes on the survey measure: 17.3 percent in late November 2025, 19.8 percent as of 3 May 2026, and 24.2 percent in October 2026. Some of the movement may reflect a change in how the survey words the question.

### Did AI use change hiring at small businesses?

Mostly not. Of businesses answering, 87.3 percent reported no change in total headcount over the last six months, with 6.6 percent reporting an increase and 2.5 percent a decrease.
