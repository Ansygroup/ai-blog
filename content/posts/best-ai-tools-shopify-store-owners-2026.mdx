Shopify's genius is that it lets you install your way into a $600 a month software bill before your store has made a thousand dollars. Every AI app on the store promises to write descriptions, answer tickets, and recover carts, and most of them charge by seat, by ticket, or by credit on top of the platform fee. The question that matters is not which app is best. It is which three apps are worth the subscription tax.

Prices were read on vendor pricing pages on November 27, 2026. App pricing on Shopify changes often and varies by store size, so confirm inside your own admin.

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform fee | Shopify Grow | The tier most stores land on after Basic |
| Email and SMS | Klaviyo | One tool for flows and campaigns |
| Support that knows orders | Gorgias | Ticket pricing tied to volume |
| Product copy | A model, not an app | Cheaper than a subscription per 100 words |
| Creative | Canva | Fast variants without a designer |

## The app stack is the real cost

Shopify lists Basic at $29 a month, Grow at $79, and Advanced at $299, with POS Pro at $89 a month per location.

Those numbers are almost irrelevant next to the app bill. A typical store runs a reviews app, an email app, a support app, an upsell app, and a subscription app, and each one has a monthly floor. Add AI features to each and the floor rises.

The discipline that works: every app must map to one number it improves, whether that is conversion rate, support resolution time, or repeat purchase rate. If you cannot name the number, the app is a hobby.

## Klaviyo: the one app almost every store keeps

Klaviyo's marketing plans are listed from $20 a month, with a $1 first-month offer for up to 5,000 active profiles.

The reason it survives every app purge is that email and SMS are the only channels you own. Flows that welcome, recover a cart, and bring back a lapsed buyer do the work that paid ads cannot afford to do at small scale.

The trap is profile count. Pricing scales with active profiles, so a list inflated by a giveaway costs money every month and converts at a fraction of the rate.

## Gorgias: support priced by volume

Gorgias prices by tickets: its entry plan includes 50 tickets a month with $0.40 overage, a mid tier includes 2,000 tickets at $0.36 overage, and AI Agent interactions are $0.90 each on annual plans or $1.00 monthly, each also counting as a ticket.

For a store doing real volume, that is a predictable cost that scales with success. For a store with 40 orders a week, the free tier or a shared inbox is enough, and paying for a support platform is premature.

## Product copy: an app is usually the wrong purchase

This is where store owners overspend most. A Shopify app that generates descriptions typically charges a monthly fee for a fixed number of credits, and the output is about as good as a well-prompted general model.

Use a model subscription with a saved prompt that includes your brand voice, your product specifications, and two examples of descriptions you wrote yourself. Check every claim against the supplier's spec sheet, because invented materials are a returns problem.

If you run thousands of SKUs in several languages, an app with templates and a translation workflow earns its fee. Below that, it is a tax on impatience.

## Canva: the creative layer

Canva does not need a price quoted to justify itself. The value is that one person can produce twenty ad variants and a set of collection banners in an afternoon, and testing creative is how small stores find what works.

The failure is brand drift: five people making assets in five styles. Lock the fonts and colors in a brand kit and give editors a template rather than a blank canvas.

## Where Shopify stores get this wrong

The first mistake is app sprawl. Every app adds scripts to the storefront, and page speed is a conversion lever you can measure in revenue.

The second is AI support without an escape hatch. If a customer cannot reach a human in two clicks, the bot is generating refunds and bad reviews.

The third is trusting an AI-written product page for a product you have not held. Descriptions that oversell generate returns, and returns are the most expensive error in ecommerce.

## Who should skip this list

Skip the support platform until you have a backlog. Skip the copy apps entirely if you write your own product pages. And skip the Advanced plan until you need the reporting and the lower card rates, because the jump from Grow is $220 a month.

## What to buy first

Shopify on the tier that matches your volume, Klaviyo for email, and Canva for creative. Add support tooling when the inbox is genuinely a bottleneck. That is under $120 a month before apps, and it covers the three things that actually move revenue.

### How much does Shopify cost per month?

Shopify lists Basic at $29, Grow at $79 and Advanced at $299 a month, with POS Pro at $89 per location. The app stack usually costs more than the platform fee.

### Do Shopify stores need an AI copywriting app?

Usually not. A general model subscription with a saved brand-voice prompt does the same job for less, unless you are producing thousands of SKUs across several languages where templates and translation workflows matter.
