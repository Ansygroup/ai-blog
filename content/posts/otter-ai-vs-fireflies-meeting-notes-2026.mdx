The promise of AI meeting notes is that you stop typing and start listening. The reality, after a month of running both Otter.ai and Fireflies through the same calendar, is that both deliver the promise and differ on everything around it: how the bot behaves in the room, what the transcript costs, and whether the notes read like a colleague wrote them or like a robot filed a report.

| Scenario | Winner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live transcription you watch in real time | Otter | In-app live capture is its original strength |
| Automated bot that joins every call | Fireflies | More aggressive auto-join and recap sharing |
| Budget per seat | Fireflies | Pro at $10 a seat annually vs Otter's $8.49 intro offer |
| Multilingual calls | Otter | English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Chinese |
| CRM and workflow integrations | Fireflies | Deeper app and CRM connectivity on paid tiers |
| Free tier usefulness | Otter | 300 transcription minutes a month |

## The in-meeting experience

Otter's identity is the live transcript. You watch words appear as people speak, highlight passages mid-call, and assign action items before the meeting ends. Its bot can auto-join from your calendar, but the product still feels centered on the transcript itself. Transcription now covers English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, and Chinese.

Fireflies is built around the bot. It joins, records, transcribes, and then pushes recaps outward: to Slack, to your CRM, to shared channels. Paid tiers add video recording, analytics, private channels, and team-pooled API limits, and you can even pay to rename the bot so it joins as something less conspicuous than "Fireflies Notetaker." If your goal is that nobody on the team ever thinks about notes again, Fireflies is designed for exactly that.

## Pricing in plain terms

Otter, as of November 2026: Basic is free with 300 monthly transcription minutes and 30-minute caps per conversation. Pro is $16.99 a month, or an effective $8.49 a month on the annual first-time offer, with 1,200 in-app recording minutes. Business is $30 a month or $24 annually, and Enterprise sits above that.

Fireflies: Free tier, Pro at $18 per seat a month or $10 billed annually, Business at $29 ($19 annually) adding team features and video recording, and Enterprise at $39 with HIPAA readiness and private storage. On annual billing, Fireflies Business at $19 undercuts Otter Business at $24, while Otter's Pro intro pricing undercuts Fireflies Pro for solo users in year one.

## Where the robot feel comes from

Both tools produce summaries that occasionally miss the point of a meeting in the same way: they promote whatever was said most often, not what mattered most. In our testing, both handled status meetings well and both stumbled on delicate negotiations, where the action item was a tone shift, not a sentence. Otter's summaries read more like structured notes; Fireflies' recaps read more like a report, with topics and bullet sections. Neither reliably caught decisions phrased as "let's just go with what Priya said," which is how half of real decisions are phrased. ## Accuracy, accents, and the search test

On raw transcription accuracy with clear audio, both are good enough that you stop correcting them, which is the real bar. Differences appear with crosstalk, heavy accents, and jargon. In our calls, Otter handled overlapping speakers slightly better, which tracks with its live-first design, while Fireflies was more consistent at labeling who said what across longer meetings. Both let you train a custom vocabulary for company and product names, and both improve noticeably if you bother.

Search is where a year of notes pays off. Otter's search across transcripts is fast and transcript-centric. Fireflies adds topic trackers and smart search filters (questions, dates, action items) that make it easier to answer "what did we agree about pricing across all Q3 calls" without opening six meetings. If your archive is the product, Fireflies' retrieval tooling is the stronger of the two.

## Where each one fails

Otter fails at scale and price creep: minute caps on Pro bite heavy meeting schedules, and the jump from Pro to Business is steep. Fireflies fails at subtlety: the bot-forward approach means more "why is there a bot in this call" moments with external guests, and the free tier's limits push you to paid fast.

## Which should you pick

Solo users and small teams who want to see and search the live record should start with Otter's free tier and only upgrade when the 300 minutes run out. Teams that want notes to flow automatically into Slack and the CRM, with admin controls and cheaper per-seat annual pricing, should pick Fireflies Pro or Business. Either way, keep one habit: skim the transcript for the two decisions that matter. No AI notetaker in 2026 reliably knows which two those are.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Otter or Fireflies cheaper?

For solo users, Otter Pro's annual intro offer works out to $8.49 a month. For teams on annual billing, Fireflies Business at $19 a seat undercuts Otter Business at $24, as of November 2026.

### Does Otter have a free plan?

Yes. Otter Basic includes 300 transcription minutes a month with 30-minute caps per conversation.

### What languages does Otter support?

As of November 2026, Otter transcribes English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, and Chinese.

### Can Fireflies rename its meeting bot?

Yes, on Pro and Business plans you can purchase an add-on to customize the name the Fireflies Notetaker bot uses when joining meetings.
