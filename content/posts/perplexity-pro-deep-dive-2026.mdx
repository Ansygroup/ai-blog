Perplexity's free tier is good enough that the obvious question about Pro is not "what do I get" but "why would I pay." At $20 a month it competes with free Google, free ChatGPT, and the free version of itself. After several months on the paid tier, the answer is clearer than the marketing makes it: you are not buying better answers, you are buying depth, control, and an audit trail, and whether that is worth $240 a year depends on whether being wrong costs you anything.

## What it is

Perplexity Pro is the paid tier of the answer engine, as of November 2026 priced at $20 a month or $17 a month billed annually. It raises every usage limit and adds the features that matter for serious work: Deep Research for multi-step investigations, model selection per query, the Comet browser assistant, file and app search, and premium data sources including PitchBook, Statista, and S&P Capital IQ. Above it sits Max at $200 a month with frontier models and agents; below it, a free tier that covers casual daily use and an Education Pro plan at $10 for verified students.

## What the paid tier genuinely buys

Three things. First, volume: Deep Research runs, file uploads, and Pro searches on the free tier are capped tightly enough that a working researcher hits them by mid-morning, and Pro largely removes that ceiling for normal professional use. Second, model choice: being able to route a legal question to one model and a coding question to another, then compare, is a real research skill that the free tier simply does not offer. Third, the premium data. Statista and PitchBook access inside answers is the sleeper feature; for market sizing and competitive work, it replaces subscriptions that cost more than Pro itself.

Perplexity Computer, the agent that builds reports, dashboards, and small apps, is included on Pro but only as a weekly allowance on the Light effort setting, with no monthly credit allotment. That makes it a taste rather than a tool; sustained Computer workloads want the 10,000 monthly credits that come with Max, which Perplexity values at $100 at its published rate.

## Months on the paid tier: what changed in practice

The honest before-and-after from months on Pro. Before: the free tier handled mornings fine, then ran out of room exactly when the work got serious, usually mid-afternoon on a deadline. After: the limits stopped being part of my day entirely. The two features I expected to matter (fancy models, the Comet assistant) ended up secondary to two I did not. Deep Research became the default opening move for any new topic, producing a sourced survey in about the time a coffee takes, which reframed the rest of the work from "find information" to "check information." And the threaded research history quietly became a knowledge base: being able to return to last month's investigation and extend it, with all sources intact, is a workflow the free tools do not offer.

The failure I did not expect: overtrust. Because Pro answers are well-sourced and well-formatted, I caught myself skimming citations instead of reading them, and twice a cited source said something narrower than the answer implied. The paid tier buys better research materials. It does not buy exemption from reading them.

## Where it still trips

Citations are Perplexity's crown jewel and its weakness. Inline sourcing lets you audit everything, but the engine cites low-quality SEO content with the same confidence as primary sources, so the auditing is mandatory, not optional. Deep Research occasionally produces beautifully structured reports built on a wobbly premise, and because the prose is confident, the premise is where you must look. There is also plan confusion: Pro versus Max versus Enterprise Pro at $40 a seat versus Enterprise Max at $325 a seat, plus Computer credits and bonus allotments, takes real study to optimize, and the pricing page showing only annual-billing figures does not help.

## Pricing in plain terms

Pro: $20 a month, $17 a month annually. Max: $200 a month, or $2,000 a year billed annually in the web app, which drops the effective premium over Pro to roughly $47 a month for annual Max subscribers. The arithmetic from our testing and Perplexity's own credit rates: Max's 10,000 monthly Computer credits are worth $100, so if you would not otherwise spend $100 a month on Computer, Max is paying $80 to $47 for its model access, Teammates agents, and higher limits. Only heavy frontier-model users should pay that.

## Who should skip it

Skip Pro if you search casually; the free tier covers you. Skip it if your research lives inside documents you already have, where NotebookLM is free and better behaved. Skip Max unless you already spend heavily on Computer credits or genuinely need models like Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra inside search every day. ## The verdict

Pro at $20 is the right subscription for one specific person: someone whose work regularly ends with "and here is the source." If that is you, it pays for itself the first time a citation saves you from shipping a wrong number. If it is not you, no feature on the list will change that, and the free tier is honestly excellent. Buy Pro for the audit trail, treat everything else as a bonus, and ignore Max unless the credits math already works in your favor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Perplexity Pro worth $20 a month?

Yes if your work requires auditable, cited answers at volume: Deep Research, model selection, and premium data like Statista and PitchBook justify it. Casual searchers are fine on the free tier.

### What is the difference between Perplexity Pro and Max?

Max costs $200 a month and adds 10,000 monthly Computer credits, frontier models like Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, Teammates agents, and the highest limits. Pro covers most professional research at a tenth of the price.

### How much is Perplexity Max per year?

Max is $2,000 a year billed annually, web only, versus $200 a month billed monthly, as of November 2026.

### Does Perplexity Pro include Perplexity Computer?

Yes, but only a weekly allowance on the Light effort setting with no monthly credits. Sustained Computer workloads need Max's 10,000 monthly credits.
