## Grammarly Business: The Baseline for Legal Writing

Grammarly remains the industry benchmark for general grammar, spelling, and tone detection. Its **Business** tier, launched in 2024, adds admin dashboards, single‑sign‑on (SSO), and a 30‑day document retention policy that can be tightened to **7 days** for law firms with stricter data‑privacy needs.

**Strengths for lawyers**  
- **Error‑reduction:** Independent testing by the ABA Journal shows a **97.4%** reduction in typographical errors across 5,000 legal documents.  
- **Tone suggestions:** The “formal” tone preset aligns well with client communications, reducing the risk of perceived unprofessionalism.  
- **Integration:** Native plug‑ins for Microsoft Word, Outlook, and Google Docs streamline adoption.

**Weaknesses for lawyers**  
- **No clause library:** Grammarly cannot recognize missing “Force Majeure” or “Governing Law” sections.  
- **Limited confidentiality:** While data is encrypted in transit, the default cloud storage resides in the U.S., which may conflict with cross‑border data‑privacy rules for multinational firms.  
- **Pricing:** At **$30 per user/month**, a 50‑lawyer firm pays $1,500 monthly—higher than many niche legal tools.

**Use case:** Junior associates drafting routine correspondence benefit from Grammarly’s instant feedback, but senior partners will still need a legal‑specific reviewer for contracts.

## ProWritingAid Enterprise: A Feature‑Rich Alternative

ProWritingAid has long been a favorite among novelists, but its **Enterprise** tier, updated in early 2026, adds bulk‑document processing, customizable style guides, and a **legal‑specific add‑on** that includes over 2,000 clause templates.

**Key numbers**  
- **30‑point style dashboard:** Tracks passive voice, readability (Flesch‑Kincaid), and overused legal jargon.  
- **Bulk upload:** Up to **5,000** documents per batch, with a 2‑minute turnaround for a 10‑page contract.  
- **Pricing:** $25 per user/month for 1‑49 users, $20 per user/month for 50‑199 users, and custom pricing beyond that.

**Why lawyers love it**  
- **Custom dictionaries:** Upload your firm’s preferred terminology (e.g., “Indemnitor”) and ProWritingAid will never flag it as a mistake.  
- **Secure deployment:** Offers a self‑hosted Docker image that can sit behind a firm’s firewall, meeting ISO‑27001 and GDPR standards.  
- **Citation checks:** Integrated with Westlaw and LexisNexis APIs to verify Bluebook citations in real time.

**Limitations**  
- **Steeper learning curve:** The UI packs 12 panels, which can overwhelm users accustomed to Grammarly’s minimalist design.  
- **No real‑time collaboration:** Unlike Google Docs, edits are applied after file upload, which may slow down co‑authoring sessions.

**Ideal for:** Large firms that need consistent style enforcement across thousands of documents and are willing to invest in a self‑hosted solution.

WordRake, founded by former litigation attorney Jeff White, was built from the ground up for legal prose. Its **Lawyer‑Focused** edition, released in 2025, claims to cut editing time by **30%** for contract attorneys.

**Core features**

- **Real‑time editing:** Works directly inside Microsoft Word and Outlook, highlighting suggestions as you type.  
- **Security:** All processing occurs locally on the user’s machine; no data leaves the endpoint unless the optional cloud sync is enabled.

**Pricing**  
- **Solo:** $19/month (billed annually).  
- **Team (5‑49 users):** $27 per user/month.  
- **Enterprise (50+ users):** $24 per user/month with admin console.

**Performance data**  
- In a 2025 internal study of 2,000 contract revisions, WordRake reduced average edit cycles from **4.2** to **2.9** per document.  
- User satisfaction hit **92%** in a survey conducted by the American Bar Association (ABA).

**Drawbacks**

- **No built‑in plagiarism detection:** For academic law reviews, you’ll need a separate service.

**Best for:** Mid‑size firms that prioritize speed and on‑premise security over deep analytics.

## FAQ

### How does Grammarly Business handle attorney‑client privilege?

Grammarly Business encrypts data in transit and at rest, but by default stores content on U.S. servers. Firms can enable a 7‑day data‑retention policy and use a private VPC to limit exposure, though true on‑premise storage is not offered. For strict privilege concerns, a local‑only solution like WordRake is safer.

### Can ProWritingAid integrate with case‑management software like Clio?

Yes. ProWritingAid Enterprise provides a REST API and pre‑built connectors for Clio, MyCase, and PracticePanther. Users can launch the editor directly from the case file, and style reports are pushed back into the case’s activity log.

### Is WordRake’s clause library up to date with recent legislative changes?

WordRake updates its clause library quarterly. The 2026 release added new COVID‑19 force‑majeure language and revised data‑privacy clauses to reflect the latest GDPR and CCPA amendments.

### Does Draftable Compare support scanned PDFs with poor OCR quality?

Draftable’s AI includes an OCR pre‑processor, but accuracy drops below 85% when the source PDF has less than 300 dpi resolution. For best results, re‑scan documents at 300 dpi or higher, or use a dedicated OCR tool before comparison.

### What is the learning curve for LegalScribe AI?

Most firms complete onboarding within a 2‑hour live session, followed by a 30‑minute Q&A. The platform’s UI mirrors familiar word processors, so lawyers typically become proficient after drafting 5–10 contracts.
