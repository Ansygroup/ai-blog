Insurance is a paperwork business with a sales pitch attached, and the paperwork is regulated. That combination makes it a strange fit for AI: the drafting is easy to automate, and the compliance review is exactly the part that cannot be. The tools below are judged on how much of the routine work they remove without touching the parts that carry professional liability.

Prices were read on vendor pricing pages on November 27, 2026. Agency management systems and carrier portals usually quote privately, so most figures here come from CRM and productivity tools.

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo agent or small agency | Wealthbox | CRM built for insurance and advisory work, published pricing |
| Agency that wants a general CRM with marketing | HubSpot | Free tier, then $7 to $20 per seat |
| Renewal reminders and follow-ups | Your AMS plus a scheduler | The data is already there |
| Client letters and coverage explanations | ChatGPT or Claude | Plain-language drafts, always reviewed |
| Professional email and proposals | Grammarly | Cheap, and catches the errors that look sloppy |

## What AI should never do in an agency

Three things, and it is worth being blunt about them. It should not determine coverage, quote a premium, or state a policy term. Every one of those is a regulated act, and a confident wrong answer is an errors and omissions claim waiting to happen.

What it can do is explain a coverage the client already bought, in language they understand, and draft the follow-up sequence that keeps a renewal from slipping. That split, drafting versus determining, is the line to hold.

## Wealthbox: the CRM with a price list

Wealthbox lists plans at $65, $85, and $119 per user a month, with lower add-on tiers listed at $49 and $29. It is built for relationship-driven financial work: contact records, activity streams, workflows, and the ability to see every touchpoint with a household before a call.

For an insurance agent, the value is the workflow engine. A renewal that starts ninety days out needs six touches, and a CRM that reminds you is worth more than one that writes clever emails.

The limitation is that it is not an agency management system. Policy data, carrier downloads, and commission accounting live elsewhere, and no CRM will replace that.

## Redtail: the cheaper incumbent

Redtail's plans are listed at $39 per user a month billed yearly, or $45 billed monthly, with a higher tier at $59 yearly and $65 monthly. It is the workhorse many agencies already run, and the reason to stay is migration cost rather than features.

If you are choosing fresh, Wealthbox is easier to look at and Redtail is easier to afford. Neither will transform the business.

## HubSpot: when marketing is the problem

HubSpot's CRM is free, with Starter at $7 per seat a month and the next tier at $20 per seat a month. For an agency that wants landing pages, email sequences, and a pipeline in one place, that is a lot of capability for the price.

The catch is that HubSpot is a marketing platform, not a compliance system. Anything client-facing still needs review, and the email builder makes it very easy to send something at scale that should have been checked first.

## Grammarly: the cheapest compliance aid

Grammarly Pro is listed at $12 a month. In an agency, its job is narrower than in other businesses: it catches the sloppy sentence in a client email that makes an accurate answer look careless. Insurance clients judge competence by how the paperwork reads.

It will not catch a wrong coverage limit. Nobody should expect it to.

## ChatGPT and Claude: the explanation layer

The best use in an agency is turning a policy into a client letter that a normal person can follow: what is covered, what is not, what to do when something happens. Models are good at that translation, and clients genuinely appreciate it.

The failure mode is a fabricated detail. A model will produce a deductible, an exclusion, or a waiting period that sounds authoritative and is not in the policy. The rule that works: the model may rewrite what you paste in, and may not add a fact you did not give it.

Meeting notes are the second useful application. Recording a client review and turning it into a summary with action items saves an hour a week, provided the client knows and the recording is stored somewhere compliant.

## Where agencies get this wrong

The first mistake is pasting client data into a consumer chat tool. Names, dates of birth, and policy numbers are personal data, and the retention terms of a free tool are not built for a regulated business.

The second is letting AI draft a coverage recommendation. Even with a disclaimer, the client reads it as advice from the agency.

The third is automating follow-up without a human check on the list. A renewal reminder sent to a client who cancelled last month is a small embarrassment that costs a referral.

## Who should skip this list

Skip a new CRM if you have fewer than 200 clients and your current system works. Skip AI drafting entirely if your state or regulator requires specific language in client communications, unless you have a review step. And skip any tool that wants to send email on your behalf before you have watched it draft twenty of them.

## What to buy first

A CRM you will actually update, a scheduler for renewals, Grammarly for the writing, and one model subscription used for explanation rather than determination. Total cost for a solo agent sits comfortably under $150 a month, which is one small policy's commission.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can AI determine whether a client is covered?

No. Coverage determination is a regulated act and a confident wrong answer is an errors and omissions risk. Use AI to rewrite policy language you have already verified, never to add or infer a term.

### How much does a CRM cost for an insurance agency?

Wealthbox lists plans at $65, $85 and $119 per user a month. Redtail lists $39 per user a month billed yearly, or $45 monthly, with a higher tier at $59 and $65. HubSpot's CRM is free with paid seats from $7 a month.

### Is it safe to record client meetings with AI?

Only with consent and a compliant storage path. Transcripts contain personal data, so the recording should live in a system your agency controls, with a retention period you can defend.
