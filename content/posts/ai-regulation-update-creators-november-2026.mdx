November 2026 was the month Europe's copyright fight over AI moved from argument to process: a landmark court opinion, fourteen governments formally asking for a new law, and the first enforcement dates landing. For creators, the practical question stayed the same, how do I get a say and get paid, but the answers got more concrete. Every item below is verified and dated.

## 1. The first CJEU generative-AI opinion split training from retrieval

On November 3, Advocate General Maciej Szpunar delivered his opinion in Like Company v Google, the first generative AI case to reach the EU's top court. His recommendation: AI training may in principle fall under the EU's text-and-data-mining exception, provided the material was lawfully accessed and rights holders did not reserve their rights, with robots.txt and Google-Extended named as valid reservation mechanisms. Real-time retrieval by chatbots is legally different: it can infringe publishers' rights when a chatbot circumvents access controls or reproduces an article's expression so completely that users no longer need the original. The opinion is non-binding, but CJEU judgments follow it more often than not. The practical creator move: check your site's rights-reservation signals now, because this framework makes them load-bearing. Sources: [Dentons](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/articles/2026/november/24/first-cjeu-generative-ai-case-advocate-general-considers-ai-training-and-press-publishers-rights), November 24, 2026; [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2545531/google-s-gemini-may-avoid-publisher-copyright-claims-eu-court-opinion-says), November 3, 2026. Our [RAG explainer](/posts/rag-explained-for-non-developers-2026) covers the retrieval side of this distinction in plain terms.

## 2. Fourteen EU countries formally asked for an AI copyright law

A Council note dated November 18, backed by Austria, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Denmark, Estonia, Germany, Hungary, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia, and Spain, asks the European Commission to propose a new copyright law for the AI age "as soon as possible." The three asks: an opt-out from AI training that can actually be enforced, transparency about training data, and adequate remuneration for all creators. It was an information item at the November 19-20 culture ministers' meeting, not a vote, and only the Commission can propose law. Still, fourteen of 27 member states on paper moves the question from "whether" to "when and what." Source: [AI Musicpreneur](https://www.aimusicpreneur.com/ai-music-news/eu-14-countries-ai-copyright-law-culture-council-november-2026/), November 20, 2026.

## 3. The EU AI Act's model-provider duties are now enforceable

The AI Act's obligations for general-purpose AI providers, including publishing a sufficiently detailed summary of training data, became enforceable on August 2, and November brought fresh attention to how thin compliance looks in practice. Collecting society GEMA pointed out that the Commission's template asks only for the top 10 percent of scraped domains, while rights holders need work-by-work disclosure to bring cases, and that enforcement sits with the AI Office, not creators. Expect pressure on the template to be the next fight. Sources: [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/ai-and-copyright-major-developments-in-the-uk-eu-and-china-in-q4-2026), November 23, 2026; [AI Musicpreneur](https://www.aimusicpreneur.com/ai-music-news/gema-kai-welp-ai-output-remuneration-songwriters-november-2026/), November 3, 2026. Our [EU AI Act tracker](/news/ai-regulation-2026-eu-ai-act) has the full obligation timeline.

## 4. The AI Act reached Northern Ireland, with a product twist

The EU AI Act was added to the Windsor Framework effective November 10, applying in Northern Ireland where an AI system is closely integrated with a physical product placed on that market. Brands selling AI-enabled devices there should check whether the Act's obligations now apply to them. Source: [Stobbs](https://www.iamstobbs.com/insights/ai-ip-law-updates-november-2026), November 24, 2026.

## 5. Two US states sued xAI over Grok's image generation

Oregon and Virginia filed suit against SpaceX and related companies on November 24, alleging Grok generated nonconsensual sexualized images, including material involving children, and that safeguards and safety claims were inadequate. The suits seek civil penalties and court orders. For creators who license likenesses, and for platforms hosting AI image tools, this is the state-level enforcement theory to watch: not copyright, but safety claims as a consumer-protection matter. Source: [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-november-24-2026/), November 24, 2026.

## 6. GEMA made the case for paying on output, not just training

Fresh from its July 31 Munich court win against Suno, under appeal and covering six works, German collecting society GEMA argued publicly for output remuneration: payment each time AI music is generated or used, with the AI company paying at generation and venues paying at play. GEMA's own analysis of why lawsuits alone will not fix the problem is unusually candid: training moves abroad, and guardrailed models may never output anything traceable to a specific work. Source: [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2026/11/02/gema-on-ai-the-most-important-thing-is-we-get-an-output-remuneration/), November 2, 2026.

## 7. The UN asked for a global AI risk framework within a year

At the Barbados AI safety summit on November 24, UN Secretary-General Guterres urged member states to build a global AI risk framework within twelve months, covering common testing methods, transparency requirements, safeguards, and independent evaluation, plus a fund for local-condition testing. Barbados's prime minister added a sharper proposal: emergency powers for governments to block access to frontier models. Voluntary today, but this is the template national regulators will copy. Source: [AI Affairs](https://www.aiaffairs.com/audio/2026-11-24/), November 24, 2026.

## What creators should actually do now

Three concrete moves follow from November's events. First, set your rights reservations: robots.txt, Google-Extended, and metadata are the mechanisms Europe's emerging framework treats as binding, and unmarked works are increasingly trainable by default. Second, register with your collecting society if you have one, because societies like GEMA are where the cases and the licenses actually happen. Third, follow the money consultation: the Commission's targeted consultation on AI and copyright closed November 3 with legislative options on the table, and its outcome shapes whether remuneration becomes law. Our [late November news digest](/posts/ai-news-digest-late-november-2026) covers the industry side of the same month.

The direction is no longer in doubt: Europe is moving toward enforceable opt-outs, real transparency, and some form of payment. The creators who benefit will be the ones whose paperwork, reservations, registrations, and records, is already in order when the rules land.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What did the CJEU Advocate General say about AI training?

On November 3, 2026, AG Szpunar opined that LLM training may fall under the EU text-and-data-mining exception if access was lawful and rights were not reserved, but that real-time chatbot retrieval can infringe when it substitutes for the original article.

### Which EU countries want a new AI copyright law?

Fourteen, per a Council note dated November 18, 2026: Austria, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Denmark, Estonia, Germany, Hungary, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia, and Spain.

### How can creators opt out of AI training in the EU?

Through machine-readable rights reservations such as robots.txt, Google-Extended, and file metadata. The November CJEU opinion treats these as valid reservations, so unmarked works risk being trainable by default.

### What is output remuneration for AI music?

A GEMA proposal to pay songwriters each time AI music is generated or used, collected from AI companies at generation and from venues at play, on top of any training license.
