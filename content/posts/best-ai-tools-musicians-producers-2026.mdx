A producer's week is mostly not music. It is file management, metadata, revision notes, and the fifteen messages it takes to get a track onto a platform with the right credits attached. AI tools have become genuinely useful at that layer, and still unreliable at the layer musicians care about most, which is whether the thing sounds good.

Prices were read on vendor pricing pages on November 27, 2026. Music AI pricing moves with usage, so check the current credit terms before committing to a year.

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Getting tracks onto streaming | DistroKid | Flat annual fee, unlimited releases on paid tiers |
| Voice work, narration, character lines | ElevenLabs | Best quality per dollar in the category |
| Editing podcasts and session talk | Descript | Text-based editing that saves hours |

| Lyrics, bios, and pitch copy | ChatGPT or Claude | Good at the writing around the music |

## The rights question comes before the tools

Every tool below has terms about who owns the output and what you can do with it. Two things matter for a working musician: whether the service claims any rights in what you generate, and whether the output can be registered and monetized.

Read those terms yourself rather than trusting a summary, including this one. They differ by tier and they change. If a track is going on a release, the platform's terms and your distributor's rules both apply, and distributors have become stricter about AI-generated material and disclosure.

## DistroKid: distribution with a flat fee

DistroKid lists Musician at $24.99 billed annually, Musician Plus at $44.99, and Ultimate at $89.99. That is roughly $2.08 to $7.50 a month, and the key difference from percentage-based distributors is that you keep the royalties.

For a producer releasing several singles a year, the flat fee is the right shape. The trap is the extras: leave a release date, add a feature, or need a specific store, and the add-ons accumulate. Read the tier comparison before you assume the cheapest tier covers your release plan.

## ElevenLabs: the voice layer

ElevenLabs lists Starter at $6 a month, Creator at $22, Pro at $99, and Scale at $299, with annual pricing at $60, $220, $990 and $2,990. Character allowances drive the tier you need, and a three-minute narration uses far fewer characters than people expect.

For musicians, the uses are practical: guide vocals for a demo, spoken intros, character voices for a concept record, and narration for the video that accompanies a release. The quality is good enough that listeners do not flinch, which was not true two years ago.

The ethical line is consent. Cloning a voice you do not have permission to clone is a legal problem and, increasingly, a platform ban. Use your own voice, or a voice with a license that says what you think it says.

## Descript: the editing shortcut

Descript lists plans at $16, $24, and $50 a month. It transcribes audio and lets you edit by deleting text, which turns a two-hour podcast cleanup into a twenty-minute job.

For producers, the value is in the session talk: interviews, studio chatter, the behind-the-scenes content that promotes a release. It is not a mastering tool and it will not fix a bad take, but it removes the tedium that stops people from publishing.

## Suno and the sketch problem

Text-to-music tools are useful in exactly one role: generating a reference. A rough sketch of a groove, a chord mood, a style reference to send a collaborator. Used that way, they save an afternoon.

Used as a release, they run into three walls. The output is not mixable in the way a session is, the stems are limited, and the rights position is murkier than a human performance. a second argument: a listener can often tell, and the listeners who care about your genre are the least forgiving.

## ChatGPT and Claude: the writing around the music

Bios, press releases, pitch notes for playlists, lyric edits, and the email to a sync library. Models are good at all of it, especially when you give them three examples of your own writing.

The failure mode is generic enthusiasm. A bio that says "genre-defying" tells a booker nothing. Ask for specifics, then rewrite the first line yourself, because that is the line people read.

## Where musicians lose money with AI

The first leak is mastering by algorithm without a reference. Tools that claim to master a track will happily make it loud and lifeless. Compare against a commercial release in your genre before you approve.

The second is metadata. A release with wrong credits or a misspelled featured artist cannot be fixed after delivery on some platforms, and fixing it can mean losing the playlist adds.

The third is subscription stacking. A producer paying for a distributor, a voice tool, an editing suite, a music generator, and two model subscriptions is spending more than the release earns.

## Who should skip this list

Skip the generative music tools if your audience is other musicians, who will notice. Skip voice cloning entirely unless you own the voice. And skip the annual distributor plan in your first year if you are not sure you will release more than one project.

## What to buy first

A distributor, one voice or editing tool depending on whether you publish audio or video, and one model subscription for the writing. Add the generative tools last, as sketchpads rather than instruments.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does DistroKid cost?

DistroKid lists Musician at $24.99 billed annually, Musician Plus at $44.99 and Ultimate at $89.99, which works out to roughly $2.08 to $7.50 a month. Add-ons for release dates and extra stores cost more.

### Can I release AI-generated music commercially?

It depends on the tool's terms and your distributor's policy, and both have become stricter about disclosure. Check the service's ownership terms and your distributor's rules before a release, not after.

### Is ElevenLabs good enough for professional voice work?

For narration, intros and character lines, yes. Plans start at $6 a month for Starter and $22 for Creator. Only clone a voice you have permission to use.
