The AI music wars ended not with a winner but with lawyers. Both Suno and Udio spent 2025 settling with the major labels, and the settlements rewrote what you are actually buying. Suno emerged with a Warner Music partnership, new licensed models, and full downloads on paid plans. Udio emerged with higher credit limits and a disabled download button. As of late November 2026, comparing them means comparing a tool that lets you own your output against one that, for many practical purposes, does not.

## What the settlements changed

In November 2025, Warner Music Group settled its copyright case against Suno and struck a licensing partnership; by September 2026, Suno's v6 generation was trained on licensed Warner material, and the pricing page now lists commercial rights as included on all paid tiers. Suno's plans: Free with the v6-mini model, Pro at $10 a month ($8 annually), Premier at $30 ($24 annually) adding the Suno Studio workspace, and a Platinum tier at $100 a month, all as of November 2026.

Udio settled with Universal Music Group on October 29, 2025. Standard jumped to 2,400 credits a month and Pro to 6,000, at $10 ($8 annually) and $30 ($24 annually) respectively, but Udio's own help article, still current as of February 2026, states that downloading audio, video, and stems is disabled, and no paid plan restores it. Songs you make in Udio stay in Udio.

## Sound quality and creation tools

On pure output, this is closer than the business story. Suno's v6 and v6-wild models produce vocals that pass casual listening tests, with coherent song structure and genre control through style tags, plus features like saving a consistent vocalist across songs. Udio's engine remains excellent at texture and arrangement, and its editing loop (extend, remix, inpaint, section replacement) is arguably the better creative instrument: every action generates two variants, so you compose by curation.

The difference is what happens next. In Suno, a finished song becomes a file you own with commercial rights, stems you can pull into a DAW, and an upload slot for further work. In Udio, a finished song becomes a link. For hobbyists sharing in-app, fine. For anyone whose deliverable is an audio file, that is the whole comparison.

## The credits math, honestly

Both services meter creation through credits, and the marketing math flatters them. Udio's free tier gives 10 credits a day plus a 100-credit monthly pool, but full-length songs (about two minutes ten seconds) cost two credits each and are capped at three per day on free accounts, so the real free allowance is much thinner than "10 credits daily" suggests. Standard's 2,400 monthly credits could theoretically yield 1,200 full songs, but extend, remix, inpaint, and edit actions all draw from the same pool, and each produces two variants whether you keep them or not. Add-on packs (100 credits for $3, 1,000 for $25) never expire, but subscription credits vanish monthly with no rollover.

Suno's system is more generous in practice because paid tiers also unlock the features that save credits: better first-pass quality means fewer regeneration loops, and tools like saving a consistent vocalist cut the trial and error of recurring characters in your songs. Concurrent generation limits matter too: Suno's paid plans run 10 generations at once in a priority queue, while Udio Standard runs 3 sets and Pro runs 5.

## Where each one fails

Suno fails at editing depth relative to Udio and at transparency about what "licensed" means for edge uses; read the commercial terms before syncing a track to an ad. Its free tier is also deliberately limited, with no commercial rights and the weaker mini model.

Udio fails at the exit door. No downloads means no stems, no masters, no archiving your catalog outside the service, and no clean answer on commercial use from the pricing page alone. Subscription credits also do not roll over, so a quiet month is money evaporated. ## Which should you pick

If you need music you can export, publish, or sell, Suno is the only answer here, and Pro at $10 a month covers it. If you want a creative instrument for exploring ideas inside the app and never need the files, Udio's editing tools are genuinely lovely and its Standard plan is fairly priced for what it does. But choose with eyes open: in 2026 you are not picking the better model, you are picking whether you get to keep the song.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you download songs from Udio in 2026?

No. Since Udio's October 29, 2025 agreement with Universal Music Group, downloading audio, video, and stems is disabled, and Udio's help article confirms no paid plan restores it.

### Is Suno legal for commercial use?

Paid Suno plans (Pro from $10 a month) include commercial rights, and its v6 models were trained on licensed Warner Music material following the November 2025 settlement and partnership.

### How much do Suno and Udio cost?

As of November 2026, Suno runs Free, Pro at $10 a month ($8 annually), Premier at $30 ($24 annually), and Platinum at $100. Udio runs Free, Standard at $10 ($8 annually), and Pro at $30 ($24 annually).

### Which sounds better, Suno or Udio?

Both are strong. Suno's v6 line leads on vocal polish and consistency; Udio's editing tools (extend, remix, inpaint) make it the better in-app creative instrument.
