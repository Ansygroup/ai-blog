## What Is n8n alternatives 2026?

The phrase “n8n alternatives 2026” refers to workflow‑automation platforms that can replace or supplement n8n’s open‑source, self‑hosted engine. n8n (pronounced “n-eight‑n”) is prized for its node‑based visual editor, JavaScript code steps, and a community‑driven marketplace of over 350 integrations. However, as automation demands grow, users often seek tools with larger app ecosystems, stronger SLA guarantees, or built‑in AI capabilities.

In 2026 the market is crowded with options ranging from pure‑code platforms like **Huginn** to SaaS powerhouses such as **Zapier**, **Make**, and **Tray.io**. Each alternative balances three core dimensions: **integration breadth**, **pricing flexibility**, and **deployment model** (cloud vs. self‑hosted). Selecting the right one depends on your team’s technical skill, volume of tasks, and regulatory constraints.

## Make (Integromat): The Visual Powerhouse

Make, formerly Integromat, has evolved into a visual automation studio that rivals n8n’s node‑based approach while offering a far larger library of pre‑built connectors. As of 2026, Make lists **1,250+ native apps**, including niche services like **Airtable**, **Notion**, and **Miro**. Its **scenario editor** lets users branch logic with routers, iterators, and error handlers without writing code.

### Pricing & Limits

| Plan | Monthly Price | Operations | Data Transfer |
|------|---------------|------------|---------------|
| Free | $0 | 1,000 ops | 100 MB |
| Core | $9.99 | 10,000 ops | 1 GB |
| Pro | $29.99 | 100,000 ops | 10 GB |
| Teams | $79.99 | 500,000 ops | 50 GB |

Make’s free tier already exceeds n8n Cloud’s free limit (500 ops) and includes **2‑step error handling** out of the box. Execution latency averages **1.2 seconds** per node, comparable to n8n’s self‑hosted performance.

### Real‑World Use‑Case

A mid‑size SaaS startup used Make to sync **HubSpot → Intercom → Google Analytics**. The workflow processed **45,000 leads/month**, staying within the Pro plan’s 100k operation cap and costing $29.99/mo, a 70 % reduction compared with their previous n8n self‑hosted server that required $80/mo for a 2 vCPU VM.

### Pros & Cons

- **Pros:** Massive app library, built‑in data mapping UI, robust error handling.  
- **Cons:** Advanced features (custom apps, private modules) require the Teams plan, which can be pricey for small teams.

### Pricing & Limits

| Free | $0 | 100 | No |
| Starter | $29.99 | 750 | Yes |
| Professional | $73.99 | 2,000 | Yes |

Zapier’s **Starter** plan is the cheapest tier that unlocks premium apps like **Salesforce** and **Workday**. The platform’s **task history** spans 30 days (vs. 7 days on Make), offering better auditability for compliance teams.

### Real‑World Use‑Case

A digital marketing agency migrated 12 client Zaps from n8n to Zapier to leverage the **Zapier AI** “Create workflow from plain English” feature. The agency saved **15 hours/month** in workflow creation and paid $299.99/mo for the Team plan, covering 50k tasks—enough for their combined client volume.

### Pros & Cons

- **Pros:** Largest integration catalog, intuitive UI, strong brand trust.  
- **Cons:** Higher per‑task cost; limited conditional logic compared with Make’s routers.

Pipedream blends the simplicity of no‑code with the power of serverless functions. Users can write **JavaScript**, **Python**, or **Go** steps that run on a **pay‑as‑you‑go** model. The platform boasts **2 million free events/month**, making it attractive for developers who need custom logic without provisioning servers.

### Pricing & Limits

| Tier | Monthly Price | Free Events | Event Cost |
|------|---------------|------------|-----------|
| Free | $0 | 2 M | $0.0008/event |
| Pro | $29 | 10 M | $0.0005/event |
| Business | $199 | 100 M | $0.0003/event |

Pipedream’s **event source catalog** includes **GitHub**, **Stripe**, **Twilio**, and **AWS S3**. Each step runs in an isolated container, providing **sandboxed security**.

### Real‑World Use‑Case

A SaaS monitoring service used Pipedream to ingest **webhook alerts from 30 services**, transform them with a Python script, and push to **Datadog**. The workflow processed 1.5 M events/month, costing only $120 on the Business tier—far less than the $400 they spent on a comparable n8n self‑hosted VM.

### Pros & Cons

- **Pros:** Unlimited custom code, generous free tier, serverless scaling.  
- **Cons:** Requires programming knowledge; UI is less visual than Make.

## Huginn: The Self‑Hosted Open‑Source Cousin

Huginn is a **Ruby on Rails**‑based, self‑hosted automation platform often compared to n8n for its “agents” concept. It’s free, community‑driven, and can run on any VPS or Docker container. Huginn’s **agent library** includes **RSS**, **Twitter**, **Weather**, and **custom HTTP** agents.

### Pricing & Limits

| Deployment | Approx. Cost (monthly) |
|------------|------------------------|
| DIY VPS (1 vCPU, 2 GB RAM) | $5‑$10 |
| Huginn Cloud (managed) | $15‑$20 |

Because Huginn is self‑hosted, **there’s no per‑task charge**; you only pay for the underlying compute. However, you must handle **updates, scaling, and security** yourself.

### Real‑World Use‑Case

A hobbyist blogger set up Huginn agents to monitor **new WordPress posts**, tweet them, and archive to **GitHub**. The entire stack runs on a $5 DigitalOcean droplet, costing $60/year—an attractive low‑budget alternative to cloud services.

### Pros & Cons

- **Pros:** Zero per‑task cost, full data ownership, highly extensible via Ruby.  
- **Cons:** No official support; requires sysadmin skills; limited pre‑built integrations.

## FAQ

### What are the main differences between n8n and Make?

Make offers a larger native app library (1,250+ vs. n8n’s 350), built‑in error handling, and a more polished visual editor. n8n, however, provides unlimited self‑hosting flexibility and a stronger community marketplace for custom nodes.

### Can I migrate existing n8n workflows to Zapier or Make automatically?

Both Zapier and Make provide **import tools** for common formats like JSON or CSV, but complex n8n workflows with custom JavaScript often require manual recreation. Exporting the workflow definition from n8n and using Make’s “Import Scenario” can speed up the process for simple pipelines.

### Is there a truly free, open‑source alternative to n8n?

Yes. **Huginn** is a free, self‑hosted automation platform that runs on a modest VPS. While it lacks a marketplace, you can write custom agents in Ruby, giving you comparable flexibility without any subscription cost.

### How do pricing models affect cost predictability?

Pay‑as‑you‑go platforms (Pipedream, Make) charge per operation/event, which can fluctuate with usage spikes. Fixed‑tier plans (Zapier, Tray.io) provide predictable monthly invoices but may include unused capacity. For steady workloads, a fixed tier is usually more cost‑effective.

### Which tool has the best AI‑assisted workflow creation?

Zapier introduced **Zapier AI** in 2025, allowing users to describe a workflow in plain English and receive an auto‑generated Zap. Make followed with **Make AI** in early 2026, offering similar functionality plus AI‑driven data mapping suggestions. Both are strong, but Zapier’s AI currently supports more natural‑language variations.
