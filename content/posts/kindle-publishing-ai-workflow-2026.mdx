The royalty decision is where most first-time Kindle publishers lose money, and it has nothing to do with writing. Amazon offers two royalty rates, and the higher one subtracts a delivery cost based on file size. A book padded with full-page images can lose a meaningful slice of every sale, and nobody mentions it in the publishing guides.

This workflow covers the writing side quickly, because AI has made that part cheap, and spends its time on the three decisions that actually determine whether a book earns: the niche, the file, and the price.

*Last tested: November 2026.*

## Step 1: Pick a question people pay to have answered

Nonfiction sells when it solves one specific problem for one specific reader. The test is whether you can name the reader and the sentence they would type into search. "Small landlords who need a lease termination letter" passes. "People interested in real estate" does not.

Look for questions you have answered professionally, or processes you have run. A 60-page book that answers one question completely outsells a 200-page overview, and it takes a fraction of the time.

## Step 2: Outline before you generate a word

Write the table of contents yourself, in one sitting, as a list of questions the reader has in order. Each chapter answers one. This outline is the entire quality control on the writing, because a model asked to produce chapters will fill space.

Then generate chapter by chapter, one prompt per chapter, with the outline pasted in. Ask for 800 to 1,200 words per chapter and a summary line at the end of each.

## Step 3: Replace every fact you did not verify

This is the step that separates a book from a liability. Models produce confident, wrong specifics: dates, laws, thresholds, prices. In nonfiction, one wrong number in a chapter about taxes or compliance is a refund and a review.

For every claim with a number, a date or a rule, either verify it against a primary source and add it to a source list, or cut the specific and write the principle instead. Keep that source list in the back matter. Readers of practical books check.

## Step 4: Edit for the reader who is skimming

Practical books are read in fragments. Three habits help:

- Open each chapter with the outcome, not the background.
- Use numbered steps where a process exists.
- Put the summary in bold at the end of every chapter.

Then cut 15 percent. Generated drafts repeat themselves in the transitions.

## Step 5: Format for file size, not for beauty

Amazon's 70 percent royalty option is 70 percent of the list price less delivery costs, which the royalty page describes as averaging $0.06 per unit and varying by file size. That last clause is the one to design around.

Practical rules: compress images before embedding, avoid full-page decorative art, and check the file size in the previewer. A novel is tiny; a heavily illustrated guide can be several megabytes, and the delivery cost comes out of your royalty on every copy.

## Step 6: Price using the two royalty options

Amazon's royalty page lays out two options: 35 percent of the list price without VAT, or 70 percent of the list price without VAT less delivery costs, for eligible sales in the 70 percent territories, with 35 percent outside them.

| List price | 35% royalty | 70% royalty (before delivery) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $0.99 | $0.35 | not eligible |
| $2.99 | $1.05 | $2.09 |
| $4.99 | $1.75 | $3.49 |
| $9.99 | $3.50 | $6.99 |

Those are arithmetic on the published rates, not projections. The practical lesson: a $0.99 book earns about a third of a dollar, so pricing low to "get reviews" costs you the royalty rate as well as the margin.

## Step 7: Set categories and keywords like a librarian

Pick three categories that describe the reader's problem, not the book's topic. Then use all seven keyword slots with phrases a buyer would type, including the problem wording. Do not repeat words across slots; the fields are matched as phrases.

## What can go wrong

**Unverified specifics.** The single biggest risk. Every number gets checked or removed.

**Illustrated files that eat the royalty.** Check the delivery cost estimate before you publish, not after ten sales.

**Thin "low-content" books.** Journals, planners and blank books have their own rules and a long review queue. A 40-page book of prompts is a different product from a 40-page book that answers a question.

**Disclosure requirements.** Amazon requires publishers to declare AI-generated content in the publishing workflow. Read the current policy before upload rather than after a takedown.

**Covers built at screen size.** Text that is readable on your monitor can be unreadable as a thumbnail. Check it at 200 pixels wide.

**One book, one launch, no follow-up.** A series of three short books on related questions sells more than one long book, because each one has its own search entry.

## The judgment call

Write the book you would have wanted when you had the problem, and price it like a product rather than a portfolio piece. AI makes the draft cheap, which means the scarce input is your verified knowledge. If you cannot add three things to a chapter that a model would not have known, that chapter is not worth publishing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do Amazon KDP royalties work?

Amazon offers 35 percent of the list price without VAT, or 70 percent of the list price without VAT less delivery costs for eligible sales in the 70 percent territories. Delivery costs average about $0.06 per unit and vary with file size.

### Is a $0.99 Kindle price a good idea?

At the 35 percent rate a $0.99 book earns about $0.35 per sale, and the 70 percent option is not available at that price. Pricing low costs you the royalty rate as well as the margin.

### Do I have to tell Amazon that AI helped write my book?

Amazon requires publishers to declare AI-generated content during the publishing workflow. Check the current policy in KDP before you upload rather than after a takedown.
