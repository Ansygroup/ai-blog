A $24 t-shirt on Etsy does not return $24. Before you see a penny, Etsy takes a listing fee, a transaction fee and a payment processing fee, and your print partner takes the production cost. The design is the fun part. The fee stack is the business.

This workflow covers both: generating designs that fit a print method, and pricing them so a $24 sale is worth making. It assumes print on demand, no inventory, and a shop with fewer than 50 listings.

*Last tested: November 2026.*

## The fee math, before anything else

Etsy's seller pages list a $0.20 listing fee, active for four months or until the item sells, a 6.5 percent transaction fee, and a 3 percent plus $0.25 payment processing fee in the United States, checked in November 2026.

On a $24 shirt with a $13 production cost:

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Sale price | $24.00 |
| Transaction fee (6.5%) | -$1.56 |
| Payment processing (3% + $0.25) | -$0.97 |
| Production cost | -$13.00 |
| Listing fee (amortised) | -$0.20 |
| Left before tax | $8.27 |

That is the number to plan around. It also explains why cheap products do not work on Etsy: at $14 the same fees leave under $2, and one return wipes out three sales.

## Step 1: Pick a niche with a buyer, not a vibe

The design does not create demand; it captures it. Choose an audience that already searches for identity or occasion: a profession, a hobby, a life event, a local place.

Then list ten phrases that audience would wear. If a phrase needs explaining, it is not a shirt.

## Step 2: Check every phrase for trademarks before you generate art

This is the step that ends shops. Band names, sports teams, film quotes, brand slogans and character names are trademarked, and print-on-demand sellers get caught constantly.

Before generating, search the phrase in the trademark database for your selling country, and search it on Etsy to see whether established sellers own the look. If either search is ambiguous, pick another phrase. A takedown costs more than a design.

## Step 3: Generate concepts as variations on one idea

Ask for layouts, not finished art:

```
For the phrase "[phrase]", describe 8 t-shirt layout concepts:
type-only, illustration, badge, hand-lettered, minimal line art.
For each: composition, placement on the shirt, colour count, and what
would make it fail at print size.
```

The last column is the useful one. Concepts that need fine detail or gradients fail on most print methods, and knowing that before you draw saves a wasted mockup.

## Step 4: Design for the print method

Two rules cover most of it. Keep line weights thick enough to survive a few hundred DPI at final size, and limit colours if your partner uses screen printing. Direct-to-garment tolerates more detail but shows halftones and gradients badly on dark fabric.

Then check the file at the size your partner asks for, and add a margin: prints that run to the edge get cropped by the shirt's seams.

## Step 5: Mockups decide the click

Buyers judge the shirt, not the art. Use a mockup with realistic fabric texture, natural light and the design at true scale. A flat PNG on a white background reads as a sticker.

Show one mockup as the primary image, one close-up of the print, and one on a person. Three images is enough for a first listing.

## Step 6: Write the listing like a search result

The title is the search entry: phrase plus product plus audience. The first two lines of the description should say what it is, who it is for, and the material. Then sizing, shipping and care.

Do not let a model write the sizing chart. Get the real numbers from your print partner.

## Step 7: Price at a number that survives one return

Work backwards: production cost plus fees plus a return allowance plus the margin you need. For most POD apparel that lands between $24 and $32. Discounting below that to win a sale is how shops end up busy and broke.

## What can go wrong

**Trademark and copyright.** The most common cause of shop suspension. Search before you design, every time.

**AI disclosure and originality rules.** Etsy's rules on what counts as your own work have been tightening around AI-assisted designs. Read the current policy, and be ready to describe your process honestly if asked.

**Print quality surprises.** Order one sample of your best design before you scale a listing. Photos of a bad print are worse than no listing.

**Duplicate listings.** Ten colourways of the same design compete with each other in search. One listing with variants performs better.

**Ignoring the fee stack.** A design that sells at $18 with a $14 production cost is a hobby.

## The judgment call

Treat the shop as a small retail business with a 30 percent cost structure, not as a design portfolio. The designs that sell are specific enough that a stranger feels seen, and the pricing has to survive a return. If the margin after fees is under a third of the price, change the product before you change the design.

## FAQ

### What AI tools can I use to create designs for Etsy print‑on‑demand products?
Popular AI design tools include Midjourney, DALL·E 3, Stable Diffusion, Canva’s AI Image Generator, and Adobe Firefly. They can generate unique graphics, patterns, and illustrations that you can upload to POD services like Printful or Printify for Etsy listings.

### Do I need a commercial license to sell AI‑generated artwork on Etsy?
Yes. Most AI platforms require a commercial‑use license for any images you sell. Check the terms of service for each tool—Midjourney, DALL·E 3, and Adobe Firefly all offer paid plans that grant commercial rights, while free tiers may restrict resale.

### How can I ensure my AI‑generated designs are unique and not flagged for copyright infringement?
Use prompts that combine original concepts, add manual edits, and run a reverse‑image search (e.g., Google Images or TinEye) to verify originality. Adding custom elements, adjusting colors, and applying post‑processing in Photoshop or GIMP further reduces the risk of duplicate content.

### What file formats and specifications do Etsy POD services require for AI‑generated artwork?
Most POD partners accept PNG (transparent background) or high‑resolution JPEG files at 300 dpi. Recommended dimensions are 12×12 inches for apparel and 8×10 inches for wall art, with a minimum size of 4500 × 5400 pixels for print quality. Always check the specific requirements of your chosen POD provider.
