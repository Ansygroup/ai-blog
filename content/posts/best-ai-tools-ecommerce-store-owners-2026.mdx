The margin on an online store is decided after the sale, not before it. A support ticket answered by a bot that misunderstands a return policy costs a refund and a review. A cart recovery email that fires twice costs an unsubscriber. The AI tools that matter to a store owner are the ones sitting on those two edges, and the ones that look impressive in a demo usually are not them.

Prices were read on vendor pricing pages on November 27, 2026. Usage-based pricing in this category means your bill depends on your ticket volume, so the numbers below are starting points, not forecasts.

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Store doing under 500 orders a month | Shopify plus Klaviyo | Platform plus one email tool covers most of it |
| Support volume above one person's capacity | Gorgias | Ticketing built around orders, not generic inboxes |
| Product copy at catalogue scale | Jasper or Copy.ai | Templates and brand voice for repetitive pages |
| Support automation with outcomes pricing | Intercom | Charges per resolved outcome rather than per seat |
| Ad and social creative | Canva | Fast variants, cheap seats |

## The pricing model that decides everything

Store tools have moved from seats to usage. Gorgias charges by ticket volume: its entry plan includes 50 tickets a month with $0.40 per-ticket overage, a mid plan includes 2,000 tickets at $0.36 overage, and AI Agent interactions are priced at $0.90 each on annual plans or $1.00 on monthly plans, with each interaction also counting as a ticket. Intercom prices at $0.99 per outcome on top of seat fees listed at $19 to $132 per seat a month depending on tier.

Read those two models side by side and the risk is obvious. A support automation that resolves 3,000 conversations a month at $0.90 each is $2,700 a month before anyone's salary. That can still be cheaper than hiring, but only if the automation actually resolves rather than escalates. Ask any vendor for the escalation rate on stores like yours, and treat a vague answer as a no.

## Shopify: the platform fee is the cheap part

Shopify lists Basic at $29 a month, Grow at $79, and Advanced at $299, with POS Pro at $89 a month per location. Those are monthly figures; annual billing is lower.

The subscription is not where the money goes. Apps, payment processing, and the transaction fees that apply when you use a payment provider other than Shopify Payments add up quickly. Every AI tool you add is another app subscription and another script on your storefront, and page speed is a conversion lever you can feel.

## Gorgias: support that knows the order

Gorgias is built around the order record, which is the difference that matters. An agent sees the order, the tracking, and the customer's history in one panel, and the AI can answer "where is my order" without a human.

Where it fails: anything unusual. Custom requests, damaged goods, and multi-item returns still need a person, and the AI's confident wrong answer about a return window is worse than no answer. Route by intent, and keep returns out of automation for the first month.

## Klaviyo: the email engine

Klaviyo's marketing plans are listed from $20 a month, with a $1 first-month offer for up to 5,000 active profiles. Email is still the highest-return channel for most stores, and the AI features that help are unglamorous: subject line variants, send-time optimization, and product recommendations that do not look like a template.

The trap is list growth for its own sake. More profiles means a higher tier, and a list full of people who never open is a cost, not an asset.

## Jasper and Copy.ai: catalogue copy

Jasper's Pro plan is $59 a month billed yearly or $69 monthly. Copy.ai's paid plans start at $29 a month. Both are aimed at teams producing product descriptions, ad variants, and landing pages at volume.

For a store with 40 products, a general model plus a good prompt is enough. For a store with 4,000 SKUs and three languages, the brand voice and template layer earns its fee, because consistency at that scale is a workflow problem rather than a writing problem.

Either way, check the output against the actual product. Descriptions that invent materials or dimensions generate returns, and returns are the most expensive kind of error in ecommerce.

## Canva: creative volume

The reason to pay for Canva is not design quality, it is the number of ad variants you can produce in an afternoon. Stores that test creative win on volume, and a tool that lets one person ship twenty variants beats a tool that produces one beautiful asset.

## Where store owners lose money with AI

Three patterns, all common.

Automation without an escape hatch. If a customer cannot reach a human in two clicks, the bot is costing you reviews.

AI copy that describes a product the customer did not buy. This is the returns problem, and it shows up in the P&L six weeks later.

Usage pricing during a sale. Black Friday traffic triples your ticket volume and your AI interaction count at the same time. Check whether your plan has overage protection or you will find out in January.

## Who should skip this list

Skip the AI support layer until you have a real ticket backlog and a documented policy for returns and shipping. Automation amplifies whatever your policy already says, including the parts that are vague. Skip Jasper or Copy.ai if you write your own product pages, and skip any tool that requires you to move your catalogue into a second system.

## What to buy first

Shopify or your platform of choice, Klaviyo for email, and one support tool with a human escape hatch. Add copy tooling only when the catalogue outgrows a person. Then measure one number: the share of support conversations resolved without a human and without a follow-up. If it is rising while refunds are flat, the automation is working. If refunds are climbing, it is not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does AI customer support cost per conversation?

Gorgias prices AI Agent interactions at $0.90 each on annual plans or $1.00 monthly, and each also counts as a ticket. Intercom charges $0.99 per outcome on top of seat fees, so volume decides whether automation saves money.

### Is Shopify's subscription the main cost of running a store?

No. Shopify lists Basic at $29, Grow at $79 and Advanced at $299 a month, but apps, payment processing and third-party transaction fees usually exceed that. Every AI tool adds another app fee.

### Should a small store use Jasper for product descriptions?

Only at catalogue scale. Jasper Pro is $59 a month on annual billing, which is hard to justify under a few hundred SKUs where a general model and a reusable prompt do the same job.
