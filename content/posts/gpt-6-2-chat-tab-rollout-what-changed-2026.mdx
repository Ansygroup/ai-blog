OpenAI's release notes say a version of GPT-6.2 began rolling out globally on 23 November 2026 to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise users in the Chat tab. This piece sticks to what OpenAI has published, and flags that its benchmark figures come from OpenAI's own evaluations, not independent tests. Source links are at the end.

## Quick answer

| Question | Answer, per OpenAI |
| --- | --- |
| Who gets it? | Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise, in the Chat tab |
| Which model powers it? | A version of GPT-6.2 Sol tuned for everyday conversation |
| Does it change Work and Codex? | No, those models are not changing in this release |
| Free and Go users? | The release note does not list them |
| Are the benchmarks independent? | No, OpenAI describes them as its own tests |

## How is this different from the earlier GPT-6.2 launch?

On 20 November OpenAI introduced GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol in ChatGPT Work and Codex as a preview. OpenAI describes Astra as its frontier model for complex work in research, software development and other professional fields, and Sol as combining strong reasoning with speed and efficiency for everyday tasks. Regular Chat kept its existing model choices at that point. The 23 November update is the move into the main chat tab, which is where most people use ChatGPT.

## What did OpenAI report improved?

OpenAI compares GPT-6.2 against GPT-6 in the Chat tab. The reported figures:

| Measure | GPT-6 | GPT-6.2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Image understanding | 47.4% | 71.7% |
| Following complex instructions (hardest prompts) | 62.5% | 78.1% |
| Expert-level tasks, lowest reasoning setting tested | 52% | 65% |
| Internal BrowseComp, Extra high reasoning | 54.4% | 64.5% |

OpenAI says GPT-6.2 beat GPT-6 on its internal BrowseComp version at all four reasoning settings, with the biggest gap, about 10 percentage points, at Extra high.

## What changes in everyday use?

OpenAI's examples are practical. It says the model is better at complex requests that involve detailed instructions or finding information online, such as weighing price and location when comparing hotels. It also says the model is better at knowing when to follow up, for instance offering to tell you when a price drops if you ask whether something is on sale.

If you already pick a model manually, OpenAI's help page says that if your model picker shows GPT-6.2, the Latest option uses GPT-6.2 for Instant, Medium, High and Extra High where available, and an explicit GPT-5.6 Sol selection stays as it is. Check your own picker, since availability depends on plan and, for Business and Enterprise, on workspace admin settings.

## What about safety?

OpenAI reports significantly improved performance on its teen safety evaluations, with the largest gains in emotional reliance, disordered eating, and age-restricted goods, services and dangerous activities. In its evaluation of policy compliance in challenging conversations, it says responses met its safety policies 91% to 94% of the time, up from 77% to 80% for GPT-6. OpenAI points to a system card for details, which is the place to look for how those tests were run.

## Why should you treat the numbers carefully?

Three reasons.

1. **Internal evaluations.** The scores are from OpenAI's tests. They show direction, not how the model will do on your work.
2. **Chat tab only.** The figures apply to the Chat tab version. The Work and Codex models are a separate release.
3. **Moving picker.** Which model you actually get depends on your plan, picker version and admin settings, so two people on paid plans can see different options.

## What should you do?

If you use ChatGPT for work, run five or ten of your own real prompts, especially ones with images or long instructions, and compare the answers with your previous model before switching defaults. If you manage a Business or Enterprise workspace, check which models your admin settings expose. And wait for independent benchmark results before treating the percentages as settled.
