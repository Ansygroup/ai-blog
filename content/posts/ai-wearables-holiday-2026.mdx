AI wearables split into two groups people tend to blur: glasses with a camera, speakers and an assistant, and small clip-on or pendant recorders that capture conversations and turn them into notes. They solve different problems and carry different privacy costs.

## Glasses or a recorder

Our own framing, not a vendor claim:

- **Glasses** suit someone who wants hands-free photos, audio and quick questions. The main costs are battery life, the camera on your face, and prescription lens fit.
- **Recorders and pendants** suit someone who sits in many meetings and wants notes. The main costs are the subscription and the legal and social side of recording other people.

If your real need is meeting notes, your phone and a transcription app may already do most of it. ## Five checks before you buy one as a gift

1. **Price of the plan, not just the device.** Many recorders sell hardware and transcription separately. Read both pages and ask what happens to your stored notes if you stop paying.
2. **Phone compatibility.** Check iPhone and Android support before ordering.
3. **Return window.** Gifts get returned. Find out who pays shipping.
4. **Recording rules.** The law on recording conversations varies by country and sometimes by region, and some places need everyone's consent. Check where the recipient lives and works.
5. **Company longevity.** AI hardware moves fast and some products have been shut down. A device that depends on a cloud service stops being useful if the service ends.

## What you will not find on a promotional page

A sale percentage tells you nothing without the price it is taken from, and a feature list tells you nothing about the people around the wearer. A camera or microphone worn all day records bystanders who never agreed to it. Put the rule in writing before you gift one: tell people when you are recording, and switch it off when they ask. Some workplaces, schools, gyms and clinics ban cameras outright, so a gift that cannot be worn where the recipient spends their day is a poor gift.

## A quick test before the return window closes

Wear the device for a full week in the places where you would really use it. Count how often you reach for your phone anyway, and read back a few recordings or summaries to see whether they caught what actually happened. If the answer is rarely useful, return it while you still can.

## What to watch

As of November 2026 these are open questions, not predictions: whether sale prices become the normal prices after the holidays, how retailers handle returns on opened devices, and whether venues post rules on camera glasses.

## Our judgment

If you want one AI wearable for someone who will actually use it, pick the one with the simplest job. Camera glasses are a reasonable gift for someone who already wears glasses or sunglasses and takes lots of photos, and the 25% offer is a real reason to buy now, provided you confirm the base price. For a recorder, buy only after you have read the plan terms and the local recording rules. If neither fits, a phone stand and a free transcription app will do most of the same job for nothing.
