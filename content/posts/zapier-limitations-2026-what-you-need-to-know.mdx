## FAQ

Zapier caps tasks per month at 100 (Free), 2,000 (Starter), 15,000 (Professional), 50,000 (Team), and custom limits for Company plans. Once the limit is hit, all Zaps pause until the next billing cycle.

### Can Zapier handle large file transfers in 2026?
The platform limits attachments to **100 MB** per action and JSON payloads to **100 KB**. For larger files, you’ll need to split them, use external storage, or switch to a platform like Make (250 MB limit) or Power Automate (150 MB limit).

### How does Zapier’s conditional branching compare to Make?
Zapier allows up to **5 paths** and **10 actions per path** on Professional+ plans, while Make offers **unlimited branches** with a visual builder. This makes Make far more suitable for complex decision trees without inflating task counts.

### Is Zapier’s pricing competitive for high‑volume users?
When you factor in the cost per usable task, Zapier becomes relatively expensive—**$3‑$5 per 1,000 tasks** for premium plans. Make and Power Automate can drop that cost below **$1 per 1,000 operations**, ma[king](/posts/zapier-review-2026) them more economical for high‑volume automation.

### Does Zapier support the latest APIs for tools like Notion and Figma?
Zapier provides **partial** Notion support and **no native** Figma connector in 2026. Users must rely on Webhooks or custom API calls, whereas Make and Power Automate already offer full connectors for these platforms.

### How reliable is Zapier’s uptime and error handling?
Zapier boasts **99.9% uptime**, but its error handling is basic. Task History shows success/failure status but lacks detailed logs. Make and Power Automate provide richer debugging information, which can reduce troubleshooting time by up to **70%**.
