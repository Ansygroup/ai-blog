At Microsoft Ignite on 17 November 2026, Microsoft announced a long list of Copilot changes. The useful question for a buyer is which ones you can use today. This article sorts the announcements by the availability Microsoft states in its own Copilot blog post, and flags the claims that come from Microsoft itself. Sources are listed at the end.

## Quick answer

| Item | Status per Microsoft (17 Nov 2026) |
| --- | --- |
| Home (Chat and Cowork together) | Generally available |
| Office in Copilot | Generally available |
| Copilot Managed Runtime (hosting apps) | Generally available |
| Autopilot (always-on personal agent) | Starting to roll out to Frontier customers |
| Workspaces (people and agents together) | Available in Frontier |
| Copilot Code | Generally available "in the coming weeks" |
| Today (command center inside Home) | Private preview |
| Agent 365 Business Analytics | Rolling out in Frontier Public |

## What is generally available now?

Microsoft says Home is now the starting point in Copilot, where Chat and Cowork come together. It has one composer powered by Auto, which Microsoft says picks the model and effort for each task and may suggest moving from Chat to Cowork for bigger jobs. Employees can also choose OpenAI or Anthropic models manually, subject to organization policy.

Office in Copilot brings Word, Excel and PowerPoint creation into the Copilot app. Microsoft says files inherit Microsoft 365 permissions, sensitivity labels and compliance controls, and that creating a deck or spreadsheet does not run a usage meter because it is included in the Copilot license. Copilot Managed Runtime, which hosts apps built with Copilot inside your tenant, is also generally available.

## What is still in preview or limited release?

The headline agent feature, Autopilot, is described as an always-on agent with its own Microsoft Entra Agent ID and a named manager, which IT can scope or block. It is rolling out to Frontier customers, which is Microsoft's early-access program, so most organizations will not have it yet. Workspaces, the successor to Copilot Notebooks, is also in Frontier. Today, a command center across mail, calendar and Teams, is in private preview in the Copilot app and Outlook. Copilot Code, which lets people describe an app or dashboard and have Copilot build it, is promised for the coming weeks.

## What did Microsoft add for sales, service, finance and legal?

Microsoft announced plugins and skills for these teams. It cites more than 100 prebuilt skills for Dynamics 365 in Copilot, including 44 for sales and 13 for customer service, a Salesforce plugin with more than 38 sales skills, 30 finance skills and more than 20 legal skills. These are counts from Microsoft's post, and the post does not say how many are available today versus announced.

## What are the cost claims?

Agent 365 gets cost controls. Microsoft describes Agent 365 Cost Management for tracking consumption across users, services, agents and models, with spending limits and alerts, and a Cowork spending limit per team. It also introduces Agent 365 Business Analytics, which measures investment in work completed rather than tokens.

The boldest claim is price. Microsoft says, citing a recent GigaOm report, that for comparable quality Copilot is 84% cheaper than Claude and 63% cheaper than ChatGPT for an everyday user, and 68% and 35% cheaper for an advanced user. We have not seen that report, and Microsoft does not publish the assumptions in its post. Separately, GeekWire reported a standalone Copilot offering priced at $21 per user per month for small businesses and $30 for enterprise customers before discounts, which it says Microsoft announced on its Partner Center site in October. Check Microsoft's current price list before you plan around either number.

## What should you do with this?

1. If you already pay for Copilot, check whether Home and Office in Copilot are visible in your tenant and what your admin has turned on.
2. Ask your Microsoft rep whether you qualify for Frontier before you plan around Autopilot or Workspaces.
3. Run your own cost comparison on your real tasks. A vendor-cited benchmark of "comparable quality" is not your workload.
4. If you plan to let agents act on their own, read the identity and audit controls first: Microsoft says each Autopilot is logged under its own name.
