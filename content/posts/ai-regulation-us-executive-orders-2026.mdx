This piece reads the orders themselves where we could fetch them: the December 2025 order on a national AI policy framework (White House text) and, for the September 2026 terminology order, a law firm summary by Wiley Rein published on Legal 500. It is an explainer, not legal advice.

Two executive orders get mixed up in conversations about US AI rules. One tries to limit state AI laws. The other changes what the federal government calls AI. Neither is a statute, and neither creates new duties for most businesses by itself.

## Order one: the national policy framework (11 December 2025)

The order, titled "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence," states a policy of "a minimally burdensome national policy framework for AI." It argues that a patchwork of state laws makes compliance harder, especially for start-ups, and it names a Colorado law on "algorithmic discrimination" as an example of what it objects to. Source: [White House text](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/).

The order does not declare any state law void. It tells agencies to do things. Here is each assignment, as written:

| Section | Who | What | Deadline in the order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Attorney General | Set up an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws inconsistent with the policy | 30 days |
| 4 | Secretary of Commerce | Publish an evaluation of state AI laws, identifying onerous ones | 90 days |
| 5 | Commerce (NTIA) and agencies | Make states with onerous AI laws ineligible for certain remaining BEAD broadband funds, and review conditioning other discretionary grants | 90 days |
| 6 | FCC Chairman | Start a proceeding on a federal reporting and disclosure standard that would preempt conflicting state laws | 90 days after the Section 4 identification |
| 7 | FTC Chairman | Issue a policy statement on when state laws requiring changes to truthful AI outputs are preempted by the FTC Act | 90 days |
| 8 | Two White House advisers | Prepare a legislative recommendation for a uniform federal framework that preempts conflicting state AI laws | No date given |

Section 8 carves out areas the recommendation should not preempt: child safety protections, AI compute and data center infrastructure (other than generally applicable permitting reforms), and state government procurement and use of AI. Section 9 says the order is implemented "consistent with applicable law" and creates no enforceable rights.

What this means in practice: a company operating in several states still has to follow each state's law that is in force. An executive order cannot, on its own, override a state statute. The order sets up litigation, funding pressure and a push for Congress to act. We did not verify in this session whether each deadline above was met, so check the agency pages before relying on any of them.

## Order two: "Super Intelligence" (29 September 2026)

Per Wiley Rein's summary, EO 14434, "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," directs agencies to use "Super Intelligence" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" in official communications, websites, reports and other non-statutory materials, to the maximum extent permitted by law. Source: [Legal 500, 4 October 2026](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/government-public-sector/executive-order-rebrands-ai-as-super-intelligence-and-signals-potential-federal-legislative-changes).

Key points from that summary:

- It changes terminology, not scope. For the order's purposes, the new term covers the existing statutory definition of AI in 15 U.S.C. 9401(3).
- Existing statutes, regulations, contracts and grants stay unchanged unless modified later.
- It starts a process to propose a federal definition and to evaluate amendments to existing statutes.
- It does not expressly address preemption of state AI laws, though the summary notes that preemption could arguably appear in the legislative package being prepared.
- NIST posted that it is updating its communications to use the new term.

The practical effect, per the same summary, is likely procedural and administrative in the near term. The friction would come if federal terms drift from those used in state laws, international standards and corporate compliance programs.

## What you will not find on a government page

The two orders point in different directions on state law. The 2025 order treats state AI laws as a problem to challenge. The 2026 order is silent on them. Anyone reading headlines may think the federal government has taken over AI regulation. It has not. The binding rules most companies face today still come from existing federal law and from state and foreign law, such as the EU AI Act, which we cover in [our EU AI Act explainer](/news/ai-regulation-2026-eu-ai-act). ## What to watch

As of November 2026, these are things to check, not forecasts:

- Whether Congress takes up the federal framework the December 2025 order asked for.
- Whether the AI Litigation Task Force files suits, and against which state laws.
- The proposed federal definition from the White House science adviser's office, and whether it changes any statute.
- Whether your own contracts, grants or compliance documents cite "artificial intelligence" in a way a new definition could change.

## Our judgment

Treat both orders as signals, not as rules you can comply with. If you run a small business or a content site, nothing in either order changes what you must do tomorrow. Keep following the state and foreign laws that apply to you, and set a reminder to re-read the agency pages when a bill is introduced.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do the executive orders override state AI laws?

No. An executive order cannot void a state statute on its own. The December 2025 order directs agencies to challenge state laws, tie some funding to them and push for federal legislation.

### What does EO 14434 change?

Per a Wiley Rein summary, it directs agencies to say Super Intelligence instead of AI in non-statutory materials and starts a review of the statutory definition. Existing statutes and contracts are unchanged.

### Does either order create new duties for small businesses?

Not directly. Both direct federal agencies. Check state and foreign laws that apply to you.
