Every team building on language models hits the same wall: the prompt works most of the time, and the failures cluster in one repeatable pattern. Someone in the room says "we should fine-tune." Sometimes that person is right. In 2026 the answer changed underneath us, because the biggest fine-tuning platform is shutting down while the alternatives got good. This guide gives you a working decision process, not a slogan.

Outcome: by the end you will know which of your failures a prompt can fix, which ones need fine-tuning, what each path costs, and how to run a cheap experiment before committing.

Prerequisites: a real task with real examples, about two hours, and access to the model you already use. No code required for the decision; light scripting helps for the experiment.

## Step 1: Name the failure precisely

Collect 30 to 50 recent outputs that disappointed you. Sort each into one of two buckets: the model did not know something (facts, catalog data, policy details), or it knew but did not behave (wrong format, wrong tone, inconsistent structure, skipped steps).

This sorting decides everything. Knowledge failures are never a fine-tuning problem: facts change, and retraining to update a fact is the most expensive filing system in computing. Knowledge failures go to retrieval, which our [RAG explainer](/posts/rag-explained-for-non-developers-2026) covers. Only behavior failures are fine-tuning candidates, and most of those still lose to a better prompt.

## Step 2: Push the prompt first

Take the behavior bucket and attack it with the three prompt levers, in order. Rewrite the instruction as a spec: exact output format, tone rules, and two or three explicit "never do this" lines. Add few-shot examples, three to five input-output pairs showing the behavior you want, which teaches format better than any paragraph of description. And split the task: one prompt to draft, a second to check against your rules, instead of one prompt doing everything. Our [ChatGPT prompting guide](/posts/chatgpt-prompting-guide-2026) and [Claude prompting guide](/posts/claude-prompting-guide-2026-master-anthropic-ai-tips) cover the mechanics.

Score the result against your 30 to 50 failing cases. If pass rate jumps above 90 percent, stop. You just saved yourself a training pipeline.

## Step 3: Estimate the fine-tuning path honestly

Fine-tuning earns its cost when the behavior is complex, consistent, and high-volume: a support agent that must always answer in your house style across thousands of daily conversations, a classifier that must output exact labels, a generator that must match a niche format no prompt holds steady.

Price it before romanticizing it. On OpenAI's platform, still the reference point, training gpt-4.1-mini costs $5 per million tokens and gpt-4.1 costs $25 per million, with each epoch billed as a full pass through your training file, as of November 2026. A 500-example file might run two million tokens per epoch, so a three-epoch gpt-4.1 job lands around $150 plus inference at roughly double the base model's per-token price. Cheap enough to experiment, expensive enough that you should not.

## Step 4: Check the platform risk, this is the 2026 twist

OpenAI is winding down its fine-tuning platform. It closed to new users, existing users can create training jobs only until January 6, 2027, and fine-tuned models die with their base models: gpt-4.1-nano fine-tunes already shut down on October 23, 2026, and gpt-4.1 and gpt-4.1-mini fine-tunes follow on January 14, 2027.

The lesson generalizes beyond one vendor: a fine-tune is a dependency on a specific model snapshot. Build one only with an exit plan. Open-weight models you host or rent, Llama, Mistral, Qwen families, remove the deprecation risk at the price of running infrastructure. If you go that route, our [guide to fine-tuning an LLM](/posts/how-to-fine-tune-an-llm-2026) walks the mechanics.

## Step 5: Run the tiebreaker experiment

When the prompt plateaus below your bar and volume justifies it, spend one afternoon on the smallest real test. Take 200 of your best input-output examples, hold out 50, fine-tune the smallest eligible model on the rest, and score both the tuned model and your best prompt on the held-out 50 with the same rubric. Decide on the numbers, not on the demo. A two-point quality gain that doubles inference cost and adds a training pipeline to maintain is usually a loss wearing a win's clothes.

## What can go wrong

Fine-tuning a knowledge problem: the model memorizes last quarter's catalog and states it confidently after the catalog changes. Training on messy data: inconsistent examples teach inconsistent behavior, and garbage in really does mean garbage out. Format overfit: the model performs your format and quietly gets worse at everything else. Platform drift: your tuned model retires on a vendor's schedule, as thousands of teams are learning right now. And epoch surprise: billing multiplies by epochs, so a "cheap" training file run five times costs five times.

## Time estimate

The decision process above takes an afternoon. A serious prompt pass takes a day or two. A first fine-tuning experiment takes a weekend including data cleanup. Productionizing a fine-tune with evals, monitoring, and a retraining cadence is a quarter of engineering time, which is why the bar for choosing it should be high.

## The decision in one line

Prompt for behavior until the prompt demonstrably plateaus, retrieve for knowledge always, and fine-tune only when a measured experiment beats both on a task with enough volume to pay the maintenance bill. In 2026, add one more clause: fine-tune only what you can afford to retrain on a new base model, because you will.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should I fine-tune instead of improving my prompt?

Only when the failure is behavioral (format, tone, structure), your best prompt has measurably plateaued, and volume is high enough to justify training and maintenance costs.

### How much does fine-tuning cost?

On OpenAI, as of November 2026, training gpt-4.1-mini costs $5 per million tokens and gpt-4.1 costs $25 per million, with each epoch billed as a full pass through the training file, plus higher inference prices afterward.

### Is OpenAI shutting down fine-tuning?

Yes. The platform is closed to new users, existing users can create jobs until January 6, 2027, and fine-tuned models retire with their base models, with gpt-4.1 family fine-tunes ending January 14, 2027.

### Can fine-tuning teach a model new facts?

Technically it can memorize them, but it is the wrong tool: facts change and require retraining. Use retrieval (RAG) for knowledge and fine-tuning for behavior.
