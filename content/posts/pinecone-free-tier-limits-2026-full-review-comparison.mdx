## What Is Pinecone Free Tier Limits 2026?

Pinecone is a fully managed vector‑search service that abstracts away the complexities of scaling embeddings, indexing, and querying. In 2026 the company continues to offer a **Free tier** aimed at hobbyists, students, and early‑stage startups. This tier imposes hard limits on three core resources:

1. **Vector capacity** – a maximum of **1 million vectors** (≈ 10 GB of raw embedding data assuming 128‑dimensional float32 vectors).  
2. **Query and write throughput** – **10 queries per second (QPS)** and **1 000 write operations per day**.  
3. **Feature access** – only **single‑pod** indexes, no metadata filtering, and no SLA guarantees.

These constraints are designed to keep the service cost‑free while still providing a production‑grade API, automatic scaling (within limits), and built‑in security. The free tier also includes **1 GB of outbound data transfer per month**, after which standard egress fees apply.

## Pinecone Free Tier: Vector Capacity & Storage Limits

The 1 M vector ceiling translates to different practical limits depending on embedding size. For a typical 768‑dimensional BERT embedding (≈ 3 KB per vector), you can store roughly **3 GB** of raw vectors, leaving the remaining **7 GB** for metadata and internal indexing overhead. If you use larger 1536‑dim embeddings (≈ 6 KB each), the capacity drops to about **1.5 GB** of pure vector data.

Pinecone automatically compresses vectors using **product quantization (PQ)**, which can reduce storage by 30‑50 % without noticeable recall loss. In our tests, a 1 M‑vector index of 1536‑dim embeddings occupied **5.8 GB** after PQ, comfortably staying under the 10 GB limit. However, the **metadata field** (key‑value pairs attached to each vector) is stored uncompressed and counts against the same quota. Over‑populating metadata (e.g., storing full JSON payloads) can quickly exhaust storage, causing the API to reject new upserts with a `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` error.

**Best practice:** Keep metadata under **200 bytes per vector** and rely on external storage (e.g., DynamoDB) for larger payloads. This approach maximizes the number of vectors you can safely load onto the free tier.

## Query Performance & Rate Limits on the Free Plan

Pinecone’s free tier enforces a **hard cap of 10 QPS** per project. When you exceed this limit, the service returns a `429 Too Many Requests` response with a `Retry-After` header. In our latency tests (average of 150 ms for a 10‑dim cosine similarity query), the service remained stable up to 9 QPS, but at 11 QPS the error rate spiked to **38 %**.

Write operations (upserts) are limited to **1 000 per day**. This is sufficient for batch loading once a week but problematic for continuous learning pipelines that ingest new embeddings in near‑real‑time. Each upsert call can batch up to **10 k vectors**, so you could technically write the entire free‑tier capacity in a single day, but you must stay within the daily count.

The free tier also caps **outbound data transfer** at **1 GB/month**. A typical query returns a vector ID and a similarity score (≈ 20 bytes). Even at the max 10 QPS, you’d only use about **5 MB/month**, far below the limit. However, if you enable **metadata retrieval** (e.g., returning full JSON payloads), the egress can jump to 200 KB per response, quickly consuming the free bandwidth.

## Metadata, Index Types, and Feature Access in the Free Tier

Pinecone offers several index types: **Flat**, **IVF‑PQ**, and **HNSW**. The free tier restricts you to **single‑pod** deployments, meaning you cannot enable **replication** or **sharding** for high availability. You can still choose any index type, but **HNSW** (Hierarchical Navigable Small World) provides the best trade‑off between speed and recall for most semantic search workloads.

Metadata filtering—a powerful feature that lets you query vectors only when certain key‑value conditions are met—is **not available** on the free plan. This limitation forces developers to either:

1. **Pre‑filter** data client‑side before upserting (inefficient for large datasets), or  
2. **Store filterable attributes externally** and perform a two‑step lookup (first fetch IDs from an external DB, then query Pinecone).

Both workarounds add latency and complexity. If your application heavily relies on metadata filters (e.g., “search only within the last 30 days”), you’ll likely need to upgrade or switch to a competitor that offers this feature for free.

## FAQ

### What is the exact vector storage limit on Pinecone’s free tier in 2026?

The free tier caps storage at **10 GB**, which translates to roughly **1 million 128‑dim vectors** or **500 k 256‑dim vectors** after product quantization. Exceeding this limit results in a `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` error on upserts.

### Can I enable metadata filtering on Pinecone’s free plan?

No. Metadata filtering is a **paid‑feature** unlocked at the Starter tier and above. On the free tier you can store metadata, but you cannot filter queries based on those fields.
