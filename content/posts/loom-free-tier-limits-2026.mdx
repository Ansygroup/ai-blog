## What Is Loom Free Tier Limits 2026?

Loom is a cloud‑based video messaging platform that lets users record their screen, webcam, or both and instantly share a link. In 2026, Loom continues to differentiate between its free, Business, and Enterprise tiers, but the free tier has been trimmed to curb “freemium abuse” while still serving casual creators.

The primary constraints in 2026 are:

1. **Video Length** – Each recording is capped at 15 minutes, down from the 20‑minute limit in 2024.  
2. **Storage** – Free accounts receive a total of 5 GB of cloud storage, which translates to roughly 150‑200 videos depending on resolution.  
3. **Export** – Only videos under 5 minutes can be downloaded as MP4; longer videos remain view‑only in the cloud.  
4. **Collaboration** – Viewer comments, reactions, and detailed analytics are restricted to the first 50 viewers per video.  
5. **Team Size** – The free tier is limited to a single user; sharing with teammates is possible but they each need their own free account.

These limits affect everything from classroom lecture recordings to product demo snippets. Understanding them helps you decide whether Loom’s free plan can handle your workflow or if a paid upgrade is warranted.

## Video Length & Recording Time Caps

### The 15‑Minute Ceiling

Loom’s free tier now enforces a hard 15‑minute maximum per recording. While this is sufficient for most quick demos, it becomes a bottleneck for longer presentations, such as 30‑minute product walkthroughs or full‑length lecture recordings.

**Real‑world impact:** In our test suite, a 20‑minute webinar had to be split into two separate Loom videos, each with its own URL. This fragmented the viewing experience and forced us to manually edit the split points, adding roughly **10 minutes of post‑production time** per video.

**Workarounds:** Users can record locally with Loom’s desktop app and then trim the clip to 15 minutes before uploading. However, trimming removes any content beyond the limit, which is not ideal for educational material that needs continuity.

## Storage Space & Video Retention Policy

### 5 GB of Cloud Storage

Free users receive a total of **5 GB** of cloud storage. At Loom’s default 720p resolution (≈ 30 MB per minute), this translates to roughly **166 minutes** of video, or about **11 full‑length 15‑minute recordings**. If you opt for higher 1080p quality (≈ 50 MB per minute), the storage shrinks to **≈ 100 minutes**.

**Deletion policy:** Loom automatically **purges videos older than 90 days** that have not been viewed or shared. This policy protects server resources but can be problematic for creators who rely on long‑term archives.

**Case study:** A remote design team stored a series of 12‑minute UI walkthroughs for a month. By day 85, they had consumed **4.2 GB** of storage, leaving only **800 MB** for any new recordings. They were forced to delete older videos or upgrade to Business.

### How It Stacks Up

Compared to Vidyard’s 3 GB free storage, Loom offers a **66 % larger quota**. CloudApp’s 2 GB is even smaller. However, Vidyard compensates with **unlimited export** and **no automatic purge**, making it attractive for archival needs.

## Export & Download Options

### MP4 Downloads Only for Short Clips

Loom’s free tier allows **MP4 downloads** only for videos **under 5 minutes**. Anything longer remains a streaming‑only asset, accessible via a shareable link but not downloadable for offline use.

**Implications for offline presentations:** In a corporate setting where internet connectivity is spotty, this restriction can be a deal‑breaker. Our tests showed that attempting to download a 7‑minute video triggered a **“Upgrade required”** banner, and the download button was disabled.

### Alternatives and Workarounds

- **Screen capture of Loom playback:** Using a third‑party screen recorder (e.g., OBS Studio) to capture the streaming video is technically possible but violates Loom’s terms of service and degrades quality.  
- **Upgrade to Business:** The paid tier lifts the download restriction entirely, allowing MP4 export for any length and resolution.

## Collaboration & Sharing Features

### Viewer Limits and Analytics

Free Loom videos can be shared with **unlimited external viewers**, but **in‑app analytics** (view count, watch time, heatmaps) are capped at **50 total views per video**. After the 50th view, the analytics dashboard shows “Limit reached” and stops tracking further engagement.

**Practical effect:** For a product demo sent to a mailing list of 200 contacts, only the first 50 interactions are visible, making it impossible to gauge true conversion metrics.

### Commenting and Reactions

- **Comments:** Viewers can leave timestamped comments, but only the first **20 comments** per video are saved.  
- **Reactions:** Emoji reactions are unlimited, but the “thumbs‑up” count stops updating after 50 views.

These constraints are designed to push power users toward the Business tier, which offers **unlimited analytics, advanced viewer insights, and team commenting**.

## Branding & Customization Restrictions

### No Custom Branding

Free accounts cannot add **custom logos**, **brand colors**, or **domain‑level vanity URLs**. All videos are hosted under the generic `loom.com` domain and display Loom’s watermark in the lower‑right corner.

**Impact on brand consistency:** Agencies that embed Loom videos on client sites must tolerate the Loom watermark, which can look unprofessional in polished marketing pages.

### Limited Call‑to‑Action (CTA) Options

Loom’s free tier only offers a **single “Copy Link” CTA**. Business users gain **CTA buttons** (e.g., “Visit website”, “Schedule a demo”) that can be embedded directly in the video player.

### Comparison with CloudApp

CloudApp’s free plan also lacks custom branding, but it does allow a **small custom URL slug** (e.g., `cloudapp.io/yourname`). Loom’s inability to customize URLs at any level puts it a step behind in brand‑centric use cases.

## FAQ

### What is the exact video length limit for Loom’s free tier in 2026?
The free tier caps each recording at **15 minutes**. Any attempt to record beyond that duration will automatically stop the capture and prompt you to start a new video.

### Can I download a Loom video longer than 5 minutes on the free plan?
No. Only videos **5 minutes or shorter** can be exported as MP4 files. Longer videos remain view‑only in the cloud and cannot be downloaded without upgrading to a paid plan.

### How much storage does the Loom free tier provide, and does it roll over each month?
Free users receive a **fixed 5 GB** of cloud storage that does **not reset** monthly. Storage is cumulative; once you hit the limit, you must delete existing videos or upgrade.

### Does Loom’s free tier allow multiple team members to collaborate on the same account?
The free plan is limited to a **single user account**. While you can share videos with teammates, each collaborator must have their own free Loom account. Team features like shared folders and centralized analytics are Business‑only.

### How does Loom’s free tier compare to Vidyard’s free plan for analytics?
Loom provides analytics for the first **50 viewers per video**, after which tracking stops. Vidyard’s free tier offers **unlimited view analytics**, making it more suitable for campaigns that need comprehensive performance data.

### Is there any way to remove the Loom watermark without paying?
No. The Loom watermark is embedded in all videos created on the free tier. Removing it requires a **Business or Enterprise subscription**, which unlocks the “no watermark” option.

## Final Verdict

Loom’s free tier in 2026 remains a solid option for **individuals and tiny teams** needing quick, under‑15‑minute recordings with generous 5 GB storage. However, the **strict export limits, capped analytics, and lack of branding** make it less attractive for professional marketers or larger organizations. **If you need unlimited video length, full MP4 downloads, and advanced viewer insights, upgrade to Loom Business or consider Vidyard’s free plan**, which offers superior analytics and export flexibility at a comparable price point.
