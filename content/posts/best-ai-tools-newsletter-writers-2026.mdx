A newsletter does not die from a lack of ideas. It dies in week nine, when the [writer](/posts/copy-ai-limitations-2026) is tired, the open rate is flat and the Sunday deadline feels [like](/posts/best-ai-tools-grammarly-for-accountants-2026) homework. AI tools are most useful for that week: they cut the hours spent on research [notes](/posts/best-ai-tools-copilot-for-podcasters-2026), subject lines and reformatting, so the writer's energy goes to the one paragraph only they can write.

Prices below were read on vendor pages on November 27, 2026. Last [tested](/posts/gamma-review-2026-ai-presentation-tool-tested-in-depth): November 27, 2026.

## beehiiv: the [platform](/posts/hugging-face-review-2026) with the clearest money [terms](/posts/best-ai-tools-automated-privacy-policy-generation-2026)

beehiiv's page lists a free [plan](/posts/cursor-pricing-breakdown-2026) at $0 and paid tiers at $49 and $95 per month. The detail worth noticing is that beehiiv states it takes 0% of paid subscription revenue. Payments run through Stripe, whose fee on the page read was 2.9% [plus](/posts/chatgpt-plus-vs-free-what-you-get-2026) $0.30 per charge. So on a $10 monthly subscription, Stripe takes $0.59 and you keep the rest before tax.

That clarity is rare. Many [platforms](/posts/best-ai-powered-push-notification-ab-testing-platforms-2026) take a percentage on top of payment processing, which compounds as you grow. Do the sum for 500 paid subscribers at $8 a month and a 10% platform cut would cost $400 monthly, which is more than either paid beehiiv tier.

Where it fails: the free plan has [limits](/posts/copilot-free-tier-limits-2026), and the page lists which features move to paid tiers. Check the subscriber cap and the features you rely on, such as [automations](/posts/best-n8n-workflow-examples-2026) and referral programs, before [building](/posts/ai-linkedin-connection-outreach-automation-tutorial-2026) your list there. Moving a list later is possible, but your archive and links rarely move cleanly.

## Claude for drafts you will rewrite

Claude Pro [costs](/posts/copy-ai-pricing-breakdown-2026) $17 per month billed annually or $20 monthly. The strongest use is not [writing](/posts/best-ai-tools-claude-for-journalists-2026) the issue. It is the work around it. Paste your rough notes and ask for the three weakest claims. Paste the finished issue and ask for ten subject lines that avoid the words you overuse. Ask it to cut 15% without losing a single fact.

Where it fails: [voice](/posts/ai-voice-search-seo-keyword-clustering-boost-rankings-2026). A newsletter is bought for a point of view, and a draft written from nothing reads like everyone else's. If a paragraph could appear in a competitor's issue unchanged, delete it.

## Grammarly for the last pass

Grammarly's paid plan was $12 per month on the page read. It catches typos, repeated words and long sentences. It will also flatten distinctive phrasing, so accept fixes for [errors](/posts/fixing-undefined-errors-ai-apps) and reject suggestions that change your voice.

## Buffer for distribution

Buffer listed $5 and $10 per channel. After each issue, schedule three posts that quote one idea each, linking to the archive. Distribution is the part most [writers](/posts/best-ai-tools-non-native-english-writers-2026) skip, and a few scheduled posts per issue is the lowest-effort fix.

## What the vendor pages do not say

Open rates are less reliable than they were. Mail [apps](/posts/best-notebooklm-tools-2026) that preload images inflate opens, so a subject line test based on opens alone can reward the wrong winner. Use clicks and replies as your measure instead. A newsletter with a 40% open rate and no replies is weaker than one with 20% and ten replies each week.

A second [hidden](/posts/kaggle-api-cost-comparison-2026) cost is time on the list. Growing a list beyond your first 1,000 readers usually comes from one source: recommendations from other newsletters or consistent posts on one social channel. Pick one and do it for 12 weeks before adding tools.

## A weekly [workflow](/posts/best-ai-copy-tools-for-translators-2026) that fits in three hours

Monday: collect links and notes in one document as you read, with a single sentence on why each matters. Wednesday: pick the one idea you have the strongest opinion on and write 400 words badly and fast. Thursday: ask Claude for the weak spots, rewrite by hand, and run the grammar pass. Friday: schedule the issue and the three social posts. The point is a fixed rhythm. Writers who publish on the same weekday for six months keep more readers than writers who publish brilliant issues at random intervals.

## Who should skip this list

If you write fewer than twice a month, any free tool works and optimizing is premature. If your newsletter is a company [update](/posts/ai-regulation-update-creators-november-2026) to existing customers, your CRM's email tool probably already does the job.

## What to buy first

Start on beehiiv's free plan and publish six issues before spending anything. Add Claude Pro only if [drafting](/posts/best-ai-powered-legal-contract-drafting-tools-2026) is the bottleneck. Move to a paid beehiiv tier when a specific feature you need is locked. If you plan paid subscriptions, compare the platform's cut against your forecast revenue before choosing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does beehiiv take a cut of paid subscriptions?

The page read on Nov 27, 2026 says it takes 0% of paid subscription revenue. Stripe's own fee of 2.9% plus $0.30 still applies.

### Can AI write my newsletter?

It can tighten and critique, but a draft from nothing reads like everyone else's. Keep the opinion and the [examples](/posts/best-kaggle-workflow-examples-2026) yours.

### Are open rates still a [good](/posts/best-ai-tools-notebooklm-for-nonprofits-2026-ultimate-guide) measure?

Less so, because mail apps preload images. Track clicks and replies alongside opens.
