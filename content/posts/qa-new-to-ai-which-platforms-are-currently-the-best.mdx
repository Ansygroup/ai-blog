## Picking Your First AI Platform: A Step‑by‑Step Checklist  

1. **Define the core outcome** – Are you drafting copy, extracting insights from a 100‑page report, or building a quick prototype? The post already groups ChatGPT with writing, Claude with long‑form reasoning and code, and Perplexity with citation‑heavy research. Align your primary goal with the platform that excels in that niche.  

2. **Map the input format** – ChatGPT handles conversational prompts and short snippets well; Claude tolerates larger context windows (up to 100 k tokens in 2026) making it ideal for PDFs or codebases; Perplexity expects a query plus optional source URLs. If your data is a mixture of PDFs and web links, Claude’s “document upload” feature may save you a conversion step.  

3. **Test the citation workflow** – For research‑oriented tasks, run a parallel query in Perplexity and ChatGPT’s “advanced research” mode. Compare the citation density (number of footnotes per answer) and source freshness (last‑updated timestamps). A quick 5‑minute side‑by‑side test will reveal which model gives you the most trustworthy references.  

4. **Evaluate cost vs. usage limits** – Free tiers of ChatGPT and Perplexy allow ~10 k tokens per month, while Claude’s “Pro” plan unlocks higher limits but adds a per‑token charge. Sketch a usage curve (e.g., 2 k tokens per draft, 5 k for research) and calculate monthly spend.  

5. **Watch for integration friction** – If you need the AI inside Notion, Slack, or a CI pipeline, check the official SDKs. Claude’s “Claude‑API” now supports direct GitHub Actions, while Perplexity offers a REST endpoint for “search‑as‑you‑type”.  

**Common pitfalls** –  
- Overloading ChatGPT with >4 k tokens in a single prompt (it will truncate).  
- Assuming Perplexity’s citations are peer‑reviewed; they are web‑sourced and need a sanity check.  
- Ignoring Claude’s “system prompt” limits, which can cause unexpected output style changes.  

Run a tiny pilot (one blog outline, one research query, one code snippet) across the three platforms, record the metrics above, and let the data decide your long‑term stack.

## Looking Ahead: The AI Platform Landscape in 2027‑2028  

By the time 2028 rolls around, the “practical trio” identified in the 2026 post is likely to be reshaped by three converging trends.  

1. **Unified multimodal cores** – Expect Claude‑style reasoning to be merged with ChatGPT‑style conversational fluency in a single model that natively ingests text, tables, and images without separate “upload” steps. Early demos from Anthropic’s “Claude‑5” already show 200 k token windows and on‑the‑fly diagram interpretation.  

2. **Citation‑by‑design engines** – Perplexity’s strength in source‑backed answers is pushing the industry toward built‑in citation graphs. The next wave will embed a provenance layer directly into the model’s output, allowing users to toggle “strict citation mode” that filters results to peer‑reviewed or government‑issued sources only.  

3. **Composable AI marketplaces** – Rather than picking a monolithic platform, developers will assemble “micro‑models” (e.g., a summarizer, a fact‑checker, a code generator) from a marketplace akin to npm. OpenAI’s “ChatGPT Plugins 2.0” and Claude’s “Skill Store” are early signs that the best tool for a task will be a pipeline you stitch together, not a single endpoint.  

These shifts will blur the lines between the three platforms highlighted today. The practical advice will evolve from “pick ChatGPT vs. Claude vs. Perplexity” to “choose the right composition of multimodal reasoning, citation fidelity, and integration hooks for your workflow.” Keeping an eye on the emerging marketplace APIs and the provenance features in upcoming releases will ensure you stay ahead of the curve without having to overhaul your entire AI stack each year.
