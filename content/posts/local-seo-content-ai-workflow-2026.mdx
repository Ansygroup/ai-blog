Local search is not won by publishing more blog posts. It is won by being unambiguous about what you do, where you do it, and what happens when someone calls. The businesses that outrank bigger competitors in a city are usually the ones with a complete profile, a page for every service, and reviews that mention the actual work.

AI helps with the writing that surrounds those fundamentals. It cannot fix a profile that says "we do everything", and it cannot invent the reviews you did not ask for.

This workflow covers the order that works: profile, then service pages, then the questions your phone already answers, then reviews, then posts. Two afternoons for the setup, an hour a month after that.

*Last tested: November 2026.*

## Who this is for

Single-location and small multi-location businesses: clinics, trades, salons, studios, professional practices. Skip it if you sell nationally online, where local intent is not the constraint.

## Step 1: Fix the profile before writing a word

Google's guidelines for representing a business on Google cover the profile itself, including content guidelines and a list of prohibited content and behavior that applies to all Business Profile content, according to the guidelines page checked in November 2026.

Practically, that means four fixes that take an hour:

- The business name is the real name, with no keyword stuffing.
- The primary category is the most specific one that fits, not the broadest.
- Hours, phone and address match your website exactly.
- Photos are recent and show the premises, the team and the work.

Do this first because everything else depends on it. A perfect service page pointing at a profile with the wrong hours is wasted effort.

## Step 2: One page per service, not one page per city

City pages that differ only by the place name are the oldest thin-content trick in local SEO, and they stopped working years ago. Service pages work because they answer a specific need.

If you serve five nearby towns, do not write five copies of the same page. Write one strong page per service, then mention the towns inside it where they genuinely matter: "we cover [town] and the surrounding area, with same-week appointments."

## Step 3: Use AI to turn your process into page sections

The raw material is what you already do on every job. Dictate it, then structure it:

```
Here is how we handle [service], step by step, including what we check
and what we charge for: [your notes]

Write page sections for: what's included, how long it takes, what it
costs (ranges only, from my notes), what can go wrong, and 6 FAQs a
customer would ask before booking.

Rules: use only my numbers. Do not add certifications, guarantees or
timelines I did not mention. No superlatives. Short paragraphs.
```

The "ranges only, from my notes" rule keeps you out of the trap where a model invents a price and a customer arrives expecting it.

## Step 4: Answer the twelve questions from your phone

Write down the last twelve questions customers asked on the phone. They are your FAQ, and they are better than any keyword tool because they are what people actually say. Put six on each service page, answered in two sentences.

Use the exact words customers use, not the industry terms. "How much does a boiler service cost" is a question. "Boiler maintenance solutions" is a brochure.

## Step 5: Ask every customer, the same way

Reviews are the strongest local signal you can influence, and the method is boring: ask every customer, at the same moment in the process, with the same short request.

Do not filter for happy customers before asking. Beyond the policy problems with selective solicitation, the arithmetic is against you: a steady flow of honest reviews with a few threes reads as real, while twelve perfect fives read as bought.

Reply to reviews, including the bad ones, in two sentences. A calm reply to a one-star review is the most persuasive page on your site.

## Step 6: Post updates that have a date

Weekly profile posts about real things: a new service, a seasonal reminder, a completed job with a photo, a holiday closing. Posts expire quickly, so the cadence matters more than the polish.

Ask the model for five post drafts from one photo and a sentence of context. Edit the date and the offer, publish one a week.

## Step 7: Track the three local actions

In your profile insights, watch calls, direction requests and website clicks. Those three map to revenue in a way impressions never will. Compare month to month, and note what changed in the same month: a new service page, a photo batch, a review push.

## What can go wrong

**Keyword-stuffed business names.** Google's guidelines prohibit misleading representation, and stuffed names get profiles suspended. Use the name on your signage.

**Generated local claims.** "Serving 40,000 customers since 2009" invented by a model is a lie on a page that ranks. Use only what you can prove.

**Duplicate pages.** Two pages targeting the same service compete with each other. Consolidate and redirect.

**Hours drift.** Holiday hours left unchanged generate one-star reviews about a locked door. Update them the week before, every time.

**Ignoring the phone.** Local SEO that sends calls to voicemail converts worse than ranking second. Answer, or add a receptionist workflow that does.

## The judgment call

If you only do two things this quarter, complete the profile and write one honest page per service with real prices. That combination beats a blog, a social calendar and a link campaign for a business whose customers are within a twenty-minute drive. AI makes the writing fast, which means the constraint is now how well you know your own process. Write that down first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I create a separate page for every city I serve?

No. Pages that differ only by place name are thin content. Write one strong page per service and mention nearby towns where they genuinely matter.

### What should I fix first for local SEO?

The Business Profile: real business name, the most specific primary category, hours and phone matching the website, and recent photos. Everything else depends on it.

### How many reviews do I need to rank locally?

There is no threshold. A steady flow of honest reviews with a few middling scores reads as real, and replying to every review, including the bad ones, matters more than chasing a perfect average.
