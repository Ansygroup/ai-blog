An online coach with 30 clients does not have a training problem. They have a Sunday-night problem: thirty check-ins to read, thirty programs to adjust, and a feeling that the clients who need the most attention are buried in the pile. The AI tools that help here do not write programs for you. They shorten the reading and the typing so the coaching time goes to the people who are slipping.

Prices below were read on vendor pages on November 27, 2026. Last tested: November 27, 2026.

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New coach with under 10 clients | Everfit | Priced by client count, from $19 monthly or $16 billed annually for 5 clients |
| Coach who wants a branded app and add-ons | Trainerize | Add-ons start at $10 per month, so you pay only for what you use |
| Coach who wants simple program delivery | TrueCoach | Three published tiers, $26.34 to $136.99 per month |
| Check-in replies, content and nutrition handouts | Claude Pro | Drafts in your voice, $17 annual or $20 monthly |

## Everfit: pay by the client

Everfit's page lists plans by client count: $19 for 5 clients, $29 for 10, $49 for 20, $75 for 35, $95 for 50 and $120 for 75 on the monthly view, with the per-client cost falling from $3.80 to $1.60 as the roster grows. Those figures were the monthly-billed numbers on the page read, and the starting price showed $16 when billed annually.

The structure suits a coach who is still growing. You never pay for a roster you do not have. It also makes the cost of an extra client visible, which helps when you are deciding what to charge.

Where it fails: client-count pricing punishes coaches who run many low-touch clients, such as a group program with 200 members. At that size, compare it against a flat-rate platform before committing.

## Trainerize: add what you need

Trainerize lists add-ons from $10 per month and a few at $25 and $45 per month, plus a one-time $169 fee on one offering and an AI add-on priced per action. The details of which plan includes what change often, so read the plan table on the day you buy.

The upside is a mature client app with workout logging and habit tracking. The risk is cost creep: three or four add-ons turn an entry price into something close to a higher tier. Add up the exact bundle you need before you compare.

## TrueCoach: simple delivery

TrueCoach showed three prices on its page: $26.34, $57.99 and $136.99 per month. It is built around programming and messaging rather than a long list of extras, which suits coaches who do not want to learn a platform. Check which tier fits your client count, since limits differ by plan.

## Claude for the reading and the typing

Claude Pro was $17 per month annual or $20 monthly. Paste a client's weekly check-in (sleep, steps, how training felt, one problem) and ask for a three-line summary and a suggested reply in your voice. Do that for thirty clients and the Sunday session drops by hours. Ask it for a one-page handout on a topic you explain often, such as protein distribution or deload weeks, then edit it with your own cues.

Where it fails: medical territory. If a client mentions chest pain, dizziness, an eating disorder or a new injury, the tool should not be advising. Write a rule for yourself: any message with those words gets your own reply or a referral, not a draft.

## What the vendor pages do not say

The metric that decides whether your coaching business survives is not the number of clients. It is client retention in months two to four, which is when motivation fades. The most valuable use of AI is to flag who has not logged a workout in five days so you can message them that day. Platforms with workout logging make this possible, but only if clients actually log. Before paying for a more expensive tier, check how many of your current clients log more than twice a week. If it is under half, your problem is onboarding, not software.

Also note that client data includes health details. Read the privacy terms before pasting names, weights or injuries into any general chat tool. Strip identifying details first.

## Who should skip this list

A coach with under five clients can use a shared spreadsheet and a messaging app. A coach who sells only a one-off PDF program needs a payment page, not a coaching platform.

## What to buy first

Take one platform's free trial and run your three most demanding clients through it for two weeks. Choose Everfit if you want to pay by roster size, TrueCoach if you want the fewest moving parts, and Trainerize if the app experience for clients is the selling point. Add Claude Pro only after you have measured how long check-ins take you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can AI write workout programs for clients?

It can draft ideas, but a coach must review every program. It does not know a client's injuries or history unless you tell it, and it should not advise on medical issues.

### Which coaching platform is cheapest to start?

Everfit prices by client count from $19 monthly for 5 clients on the page read, which suits a small roster. Compare again as you grow.

### Is it safe to paste client data into a chat tool?

Remove names and health identifiers first, and read the tool's privacy terms. Treat any medical mention as a case for your own reply.
