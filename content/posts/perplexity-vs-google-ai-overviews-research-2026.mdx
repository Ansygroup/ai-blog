Google now answers a huge share of searches with an AI-written paragraph before you ever see a link. Perplexity built a whole company on the same idea, then charged $20 a month for it. If your job involves checking facts rather than glancing at them, the difference between "an answer" and "an answer you can defend" is the entire comparison. As of late November 2026, both tools have changed enough this year that older comparisons are stale.

| Scenario | Winner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quick factual lookup | Google AI Overviews | Free, instant, already where you search |
| Research you must cite at work | Perplexity | Inline citations you can audit, premium data sources |
| Long, multi-source investigation | Perplexity | Deep Research and model selection beat a summary box |
| Questions about your own files and email | Google | AI Mode now reads Drive and Wallet with permission |
| Breaking news and niche forums | Perplexity | Better at surfacing fresh, specific sources |
| Zero budget | Google | Costs nothing; Perplexity's free tier is capped |

## What each one actually is in late 2026

AI Overviews is the AI summary at the top of Google Search, and AI Mode is its conversational big sibling. Google has spent 2026 fusing them into the main search experience: a November redesign replaced the classic homepage with an AI sidebar, Gemini 4 Hydrogen now powers the answers, and as of November 23, 2026, AI Mode can pull from your Drive and Wallet to personalize responses. It is free, and it is becoming the default front door to the web.

Perplexity is a standalone answer engine. The free tier covers casual use with citations in every answer. Pro costs $20 a month or $17 a month billed annually, and adds Deep Research, model selection, the Comet browser assistant, and premium data sources like PitchBook, Statista, and S&P Capital IQ. Max, at $200 a month or $2,000 a year, unlocks frontier models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5, plus the new Teammates agents.

## Accuracy and citations

This is where the tools split. Every Perplexity answer carries inline citations you can click and audit, and Pro lets you pick which model reasons over the sources. When it is wrong, you can usually see why, because the source it leaned on is right there.

AI Overviews has improved its sourcing, but the citation UX is still secondary to the answer, and the answer is still optimized for speed. Its known failure mode is confident compression: a technically-true summary that flattens an important caveat from the source. For a consumer question, fine. For a number going into a client report, you will end up opening the links anyway, which is the work AI Overviews was supposed to save you.

## A week of real use

In our testing across a week of work questions, the pattern was consistent. Google won the "what time does" and "how do I" queries outright, often with the answer visible before the page finished loading. Perplexity won every query that started with "compare," "why did," or "what changed," because its answers preserved the disagreement between sources instead of averaging it away. One concrete example: when we asked both tools about a recent pricing change at a software vendor, AI Overviews summarized the vendor's own blog post, while Perplexity surfaced the blog post, two independent write-ups, and a forum thread where users reported the change had rolled out unevenly. Only one of those answers would have survived a manager asking "are you sure?"

There is also a workflow difference that spec sheets miss. Perplexity keeps your research as threaded conversations you can return to and extend, which behaves like a research log. AI Overviews is disposable by design: each search is a fresh start unless you move into AI Mode's threads, which are newer and less proven.

## Where each one fails

AI Overviews fails at depth and at accountability. Ask a layered question and you get a layered-average answer. It also blurs a line researchers should watch: with Drive and Wallet access, its answers get better by getting more personal, which is a privacy tradeoff every user should make consciously rather than by default.

Perplexity fails at the free tier, which is limited enough that serious users hit walls fast, and at price clarity: Pro versus Max versus Enterprise tiers, plus Computer credits, take real study. It can also cite low-quality SEO content as confidently as primary sources, so the citations need auditing, not blind trust.

## Which should you pick

If research means "get me the answer in ten seconds," stay with Google; you already pay nothing and AI Mode keeps getting more capable. If research means "get me an answer I can put my name under," Perplexity Pro at $20 a month is the only one of the two built for that job, and it is cheap insurance against one bad citation. Heavy daily investigators should test Pro first and only touch Max if they genuinely need the frontier models.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Perplexity better than Google AI Overviews?

For auditable research, yes: Perplexity puts inline citations at the center and offers Deep Research on paid plans. For fast casual answers, Google's free AI Overviews is usually enough.

### How much does Perplexity cost in 2026?

As of November 2026, Pro is $20 a month or $17 a month billed annually, and Max is $200 a month or $2,000 a year. A limited free tier exists.

### Can Google AI Mode read my files?

Yes. Since November 23, 2026, AI Mode can connect to Google Drive and Wallet for personalized answers, with permission.

### Do AI Overviews citations get clicked?

Google's AI answers de-emphasize links compared with classic search, which is why researchers often must open sources manually to verify claims.
