This comparison was supposed to be Runway against Sora. Then OpenAI shut Sora down: the web and app went dark on April 26, 2026, and the API followed on September 24, 2026. The throne Sora vacated did not stay empty. Google's Veo 3.1, flowing through the Gemini app and the Flow studio, is now the obvious rival, and the contest has a clear shape: Runway is the professional's editing bay, Veo is the consumer-friendly generator with frighteningly good raw output. People who ship video for a living need to know where the seams are.

| Scenario | Winner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short cinematic clips from a prompt | Veo 3.1 | Native audio and strong physics out of the box |
| Iterating a shot until a client signs off | Runway | Editing tools, not just regeneration |
| Tight budget, occasional clips | Runway | Free tier with 125 one-time credits; Standard at $12 |
| Already paying for Google AI Pro | Veo 3.1 | Flow credits are in the subscription you have |
| Longer, multi-shot projects | Runway | Project workflow, assets, and upscaling in one place |
| Volume generation via API | Veo 3.1 | Google's API access scales; Runway is credit-plan centric |

## Raw generation quality

Veo 3.1's calling card is that clips arrive with synchronized native audio and physics that rarely produce the famous AI weirdness: hands hold objects, crowds move plausibly, water behaves. For a "describe the shot, get the shot" workflow, it is the best raw generator we have used this year. Google's own evaluation page shows human raters preferring it in side-by-side tests against rivals, with the caveat that those clips are eight seconds and comparisons ran without sound for the visual-only metrics.

Runway's Gen-4.5, released in November 2025, is close on visual quality and better at intent: it follows camera direction and style references more obediently, and it slots into a workspace where generation is step one, not the whole job.

## The editing story is the real divide

Runway is a suite. Beyond generation you get Aleph for editing existing footage, 4K upscaling, green screen, asset storage, parallel generations, and a credit system that spans image, video, and audio models (it even hosts third-party models like Kling 4.0 and Seedance 2.0). When a client says "same clip, but slower and without the car," Runway gives you tools to fix the clip. Veo's answer is usually "generate again."

Veo 3.1 lives inside Gemini and Flow. Flow is Google's creative studio, and it is genuinely good for assembling scenes, but it is not an editor in the Runway sense. Our [Google Flow and Veo 3.1 production guide](/news/google-flow-veo-3-1-2026-ai-video-production-native-audio) covers that workflow in detail.

## Pricing in plain terms

Runway, as of November 2026: Free with 125 one-time credits; Standard at $15 a month or $12 billed annually with 625 credits a month; Pro at $35 ($28 annually) with 2,250; Max at $95 ($76 annually). Gen-4.5 costs 12 credits per second of video, so a 5-second clip is 60 credits. On Standard that is about ten 5-second clips a month; heavy iteration burns plans fast.

Veo 3.1 access comes through Google's subscriptions: Google AI Pro at $19.99 a month with 1,000 Flow credits, or AI Ultra at $99.99 a month (5x limits) or $199.99 (20x) with 10,000 to 25,000 Flow credits. If you already pay for Pro for Gemini, Veo is effectively bundled.

## A production week with both

We gave both tools the same assignment: a 30-second product teaser built from six shots. The Veo path was fast at the front and slow at the back. Four of six shots came back usable within a few prompts each, with audio we kept. The other two needed eleven regenerations between them, because there is no way to nudge a nearly-right clip, only to roll again. Total time was dominated by the two stubborn shots, and we burned through a noticeable share of the month's Flow credits on attempts, not outputs.

The Runway path was slower to first drafts and faster to final. Raw generations needed more prompt detail to match Veo's look, but when a shot was 80 percent right, Aleph edits and targeted regeneration of just the offending beat closed the gap. The finished teaser shipped with two Veo clips (the opening establishing shot and a closing wide) and four Runway clips, which honestly describes how most working editors we know use these tools now: whichever engine gets a given shot right, that is the engine for that shot.

## Where each one fails

Runway fails on cost transparency and length: credits evaporate during iteration, and long-form work still means stitching short clips. Veo fails on control and predictability: regeneration roulette, limited fine editing, and an eight-second ceiling on most outputs. Both still struggle with consistent characters across many shots, though both are improving monthly. ## Which should you pick

If video is your product or your client's product, Runway's Pro plan is the working tool; Veo is your sketchbook. If video is an occasional garnish for content you already make in the Google ecosystem, Veo 3.1 inside AI Pro is the best deal in AI video right now. And if you were holding out for Sora to come back: stop holding out.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Sora still available in 2026?

No. OpenAI discontinued the Sora web and app experiences on April 26, 2026, and the Sora API on September 24, 2026.

### How much does Runway cost in 2026?

As of November 2026: a free tier with 125 one-time credits, Standard at $15 a month ($12 annually), Pro at $35 ($28 annually), and Max at $95 ($76 annually). Gen-4.5 uses 12 credits per second of video.

### How do I get access to Veo 3.1?

Through the Gemini app and Google's Flow studio. Google AI Pro at $19.99 a month includes 1,000 Flow credits; AI Ultra tiers at $99.99 and $199.99 a month raise limits substantially.

### Which is better for client work, Runway or Veo?

Runway, because its editing tools (Aleph, upscaling, asset management) let you revise a shot without regenerating from scratch. Veo 3.1 produces excellent raw clips but offers less control after generation.
