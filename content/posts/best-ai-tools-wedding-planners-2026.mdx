A wedding planner's week is a stack of small promises: a vendor who needs a final headcount, a couple who wants the seating chart redone, a venue asking for a certificate of insurance you sent twice. Almost none of that is creative work, and almost all of it is where the hours go. The tools worth paying for are the ones that hold the promises, not the ones that write a nice paragraph about love.

Prices below were read on vendor pricing pages on November 27, 2026. Check them again before you sign anything, because wedding-adjacent software changes plans faster than couples change their minds.

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo planner, 15 to 25 weddings a year | HoneyBook | Invoices, contracts and client portal in one place |
| Planner with an assistant and subcontractors | Dubsado | Workflows and forms that survive a team |
| Client-facing mood boards and signage | Canva | Fast, and the couple can be given edit rights |
| First drafts of emails and timelines | ChatGPT or Claude | Good at structure, bad at specifics |
| Guest lists, vendor databases, run sheets | Airtable | Relational views beat a spreadsheet |

## What AI is genuinely good at in this job

Three tasks, and only three, consistently save time. Drafting the routine email, meaning the third reminder about the same deadline, phrased so it does not sound like a third reminder. Turning a rambling call transcript into a first-pass timeline with gaps flagged. And rewriting a vendor brief so it says what you meant in fewer words.

Everything else, from seating charts to floor plans, still needs you in front of it. Spatial reasoning is where these models produce confident nonsense, and a seating chart that puts two estranged relatives at the same table is not a small error.

## HoneyBook: the operations layer

HoneyBook's plans are listed from $18 a month, with a free trial. Payment processing is the part that matters more than the subscription: card fees start at 2.7% plus 10 cents, and ACH bank transfers cost 1.5%.

That difference is not trivial at wedding budgets. On a $30,000 wedding paid by card, the processing fee is roughly $810. On ACH it is roughly $450. If you can move couples to bank transfer for the larger payments, the savings exceed a year of software, and the tool that makes that easy is worth more than any caption generator.

Where HoneyBook falls short: it is built for one operator with a tidy pipeline. Add three coordinators and a dozen subcontractors and the shared inbox starts to feel like a group chat with invoices.

## Dubsado: workflows for teams

Dubsado lists its Starter plan at $335 a year and Premier at $525 a year, with additional user pricing below that. It is less friendly on day one than HoneyBook and more capable by month three, especially for questionnaires, workflows, and the kind of conditional logic that keeps a 12-month planning process on rails.

The tradeoff is setup time. Budget a weekend to build your first workflow, or you will end up using a $525-a-year tool as a very expensive invoice sender.

## Canva: the client-facing layer

Canva does not need a price quoted here to make the list, because its value is not the subscription, it is the handoff. Giving a couple edit access to a mood board template removes an entire round of email. The same goes for signage, place cards, and the welcome sign that has to be reprinted at 11pm.

The failure mode is taste. Templates pull every wedding toward the same aesthetic, and couples notice when their stationery looks like their friend's. Use it for production files, not for the design direction.

## ChatGPT and Claude: drafting, with supervision

Both are good at the email you have written forty times and the timeline structure you rebuild for every wedding. Both are bad at your specific venue. Feed them the actual vendor list, the actual load-in times, and the actual family situation, then edit hard.

The failure that costs money is invented specifics: a vendor's policy, a delivery window, a contract clause. If a sentence in a client email contains a fact, that fact came from a document you read, not from a model's memory.

## Airtable and the guest list problem

Guest lists are the one place where a relational database beats both a spreadsheet and a chat thread. Airtable lets you hold guests, households, meal choices, and RSVP status in linked tables, then produce the seating view and the caterer's count from the same data.

No AI feature solves this. The win is structural: when the count changes on Thursday, you update one field instead of three documents.

## Where these tools quietly cost you

The first trap is per-seat pricing on tools your subcontractors need for one weekend. Add a day-of assistant to a planning platform and you are paying for twelve months of access to cover eight hours of work. Use shared links and exported PDFs instead.

The second is client data. Guest lists contain addresses, phone numbers, and sometimes dietary and accessibility details. Before pasting any of that into a chat window, check the tool's data terms, and prefer the tool that keeps the data inside your account.

The third is the AI-written client email that reads like it was written by a company. Couples hire planners for a person. If your weekly update sounds like a newsletter, you have traded your main differentiator for twenty minutes.

## Who should skip this list

Skip the planning platforms entirely if you run fewer than eight weddings a year and your invoicing already works. Skip AI drafting if your client communication is your strongest selling point and you would rather write it yourself. And skip any tool that wants a three-year contract, which in this industry is a bet on your own burnout.

## The stack that holds up

HoneyBook or Dubsado for money and paperwork, Airtable for the guest list, Canva for anything the couple will see, and one model subscription used for drafting and nothing else. That is roughly the cost of one small wedding's florals, spread across a year.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is HoneyBook or Dubsado better for a wedding planner?

HoneyBook is faster to set up and better for a solo planner, with plans from $18 a month. Dubsado starts at $335 a year and is worth the setup time once you have assistants and repeatable workflows.

### Can AI build a wedding seating chart?

Not reliably. Seating depends on family politics and spatial constraints that models handle badly. Use AI for the guest database and the printed cards, then build the chart yourself.

### How much do payment processing fees cost on a wedding invoice?

HoneyBook lists card processing at 2.7% plus 10 cents and ACH at 1.5%. On a $30,000 wedding that is roughly $810 by card versus $450 by bank transfer.
