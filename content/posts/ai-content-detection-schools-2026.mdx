Last checked: November 2026. Sources: Turnitin's AI writing detection FAQ (updated 3 November 2026), a memo from the Washington State University (WSU) Provost's office dated 11 February 2026, and Curtin University's announcement of 4 September 2025. All three are linked below.

The short version: the company that sells the most widely used AI writing detector says its score "should not be used as the sole basis for action." Some universities have gone further and turned the feature off.

## What Turnitin says its detector does

From [Turnitin's FAQ](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/28477544839821-Turnitin-s-AI-writing-detection-capabilities-FAQs):

- The model scores each sentence between 0 and 1. Sentences over a threshold are labeled AI, the rest Human.
- The document score is the percentage of evaluated sentences labeled AI. Lists, headers, references, tables and code are left out.
- Scores below 20% are not displayed as a number. A low result shows an asterisk instead, because Turnitin says false positives are possible at low levels.
- Depending on the language, documents with fewer than 300 words in evaluated sentences are not processed.
- Turnitin says it aims to keep the false positive rate under 1% for documents with over 20% AI writing, and tests on over 700,000 papers written before ChatGPT existed before each update.
- It also says the trade-off is missed AI text: a document flagged at 50% could contain as much as 65% AI writing.
- In October 2026 it updated the model architecture for English submissions.
- The AI indicator is a different tool from Authorship, which uses metadata and forensic language analysis.

Turnitin also states that it "does not make a determination of misconduct" and that the percentage should not be the sole basis for action or a definitive grade.

## What schools are doing

| Institution | Action | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curtin University (Australia) | Disables Turnitin's AI writing detection on all campuses from 1 January 2026; text-matching stays on | Announced 4 September 2025 | [Curtin](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/oasis-news/update-on-turnitin-ai-detection-tool/) |
| Washington State University | Cancelled its contract for Turnitin AI detection; plagiarism checking stays available | 11 February 2026 | [WSU Provost memo](https://provost.wsu.edu/documents/2026/02/cancellation-of-turnitin-ai-detection-software_memo-to-instructors_provost-office_spring-2026.pdf/) |

The WSU memo says the move aligns it with other research universities it lists as having banned AI detectors in academic integrity cases: UC Berkeley, Colorado State, Indiana University, Michigan State, Oregon State and the University of Washington. We have not checked each of those institutions' own policies, so treat that list as WSU's statement.

## The arithmetic WSU used

This is the part vendor pages skip. WSU's memo notes that in Fall 2024 Turnitin analysed 148,547 assessments at WSU. Even at a 1% false positive rate, that is almost 1,485 assessments likely flagged as AI when they were not. The memo also says Turnitin's own claim is disputed, that it cites a 1 to 2% rate, and that other studies put it higher, especially for neurodivergent students and students with English as a second language.

Two further WSU findings: between 2023 and 2025, 33% of Academic Integrity Hearing Board cases about alleged AI use led to a finding of no violation (the memo text we fetched is cut off after "no"), and the detector's output is shown to instructors, not students, so students cannot raise an issue early.

A rate that sounds small becomes a large number of people when it is applied to a whole campus. If you are a student, that is why you should keep drafts and version history. If you are a teacher, it is why a score alone cannot carry a case.

## Evasion tools

The WSU memo describes a "cat and mouse game" between detectors and tools that rewrite AI text to avoid detection, and says it does not endorse any AI detector. It also warns that submitting student work to third-party tools can raise intellectual property and privacy risks, naming FERPA. Turnitin's own FAQ says its indicator also covers text likely modified by an AI paraphraser, so the vendor and the university disagree about how well the detector keeps up.

## If you are flagged

1. Ask which tool produced the score and what the score means. A document percentage is not the same as the amount of text that is AI.
2. Collect drafts, notes, browser history and document version history showing how the work developed.
3. Ask whether your institution's policy allows a detector to be the only evidence. WSU's does not.
4. Ask for a conversation about the work itself, such as explaining your sources and choices.

Turnitin's FAQ names the kinds of text that tend to produce false positives: content without much structural variation, text that repeats itself, and text paraphrased without new ideas.

## What to watch

As of November 2026, open questions rather than predictions (for the wider rules picture, see [our regulation update for creators](/posts/ai-regulation-update-creators-november-2026)): whether more universities follow Curtin and WSU, whether vendors publish independent false positive data, and whether assessment design moves toward oral checks and in-class work, which the WSU memo recommends.

## Our judgment

Use a detector, if at all, as a prompt for a conversation, never as proof. The vendor says the same. For checking copied text, plagiarism matching is a different and better-understood job; our [plagiarism detection tools comparison](/posts/2024-ai-powered-plagiarism-detection-tools-comparison) covers that side. And if your work depends on how AI-assisted writing is judged, read your own institution's policy first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Turnitin say its AI score proves cheating?

No. Its FAQ says the percentage should not be used as the sole basis for action or as a definitive grading measure.

### Which universities disabled Turnitin's AI detection?

Curtin University disabled it from 1 January 2026, and Washington State University cancelled its contract in February 2026.

### Why does a 1% false positive rate still matter?

Across a large campus it is many students. WSU calculated that at 1%, about 1,485 of 148,547 Fall 2024 assessments could have been wrongly flagged.
