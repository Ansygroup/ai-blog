Gamma's pitch is that you should never open a blank slide again. Type a prompt, paste an outline, or drop in a document, and a designed deck appears in under a minute. After a year of heavy use across client work and internal reports, the honest summary is: Gamma makes the first draft nearly free, and charges you for everything after.

## What it is

Gamma is a web app that generates presentations, documents, one-page sites, and social graphics from text. The output is not a PowerPoint file with AI text pasted in. It is a web-native card layout that Gamma also exports to PPTX, PDF, PNG, and Google Slides. Under the hood it routes work to outside models, including Claude, ChatGPT with DALL-E, Gemini, Flux, Ideogram, and Luma, chosen per task, which is why its image quality jumped when those models improved.

## What it is genuinely good at

Speed to a credible draft. For internal decks, status updates, teaching material, and webinar slides, Gamma takes a rough outline and returns something with consistent typography, sensible image choices, and a real narrative order. The "paste a document, get a deck" flow is the killer feature: a two-page memo becomes ten slides with headers pulled from your structure, not invented.

The free tier is unusually usable: 400 one-time credits, up to 10 slides per prompt, and exports included. You can evaluate the whole product without a card. ## Where it fails

Brand fidelity. Gamma's designs look like Gamma: clean, gradient-friendly, instantly recognizable to anyone who has seen three Gamma decks. Custom branding on the Pro plan helps with colors and fonts, but the layout vocabulary stays the same. For a sales deck in a competitive room, "that is a Gamma deck" is a real reaction, and not a compliment.

The PPTX export is the second failure. It works, but exported decks often need a cleanup pass: text boxes shift, card layouts flatten into plain slides, and some image crops change. If your destination is a corporate PowerPoint template with legal-mandated footers, you will rebuild slides by hand and wonder what the export saved you.

Third, generation is slot-machine-like. The same prompt run twice produces meaningfully different decks, and regenerating one card can change its neighbors. Fine control, "move this sentence to slide four's subtitle," is weaker than in a real slide editor, so perfectionists burn credits on rerolls.

## Pricing and the hidden costs

As of November 2026, Gamma prices in euros on its official pricing page. Free: 400 credits at signup, no refill. Plus: 8 euros a month billed annually at 96 euros, with 1,500 monthly credits, 100 slides per prompt, branding removal, and advanced image models. Pro: 216 euros a year, with 4,000 monthly credits, premium image models, custom branding, analytics, and up to 10 custom domains. Ultra: 77.33 euros a month billed annually at 928 euros, with 20,000 monthly credits and access to the most advanced text, image, and video models. Business adds per-seat credits at 10,000 per seat monthly. Annual billing saves up to 25 percent over monthly.

The credit system is the hidden cost. Every generation, regeneration, and AI edit spends credits, and heavier models cost more. Unused credits roll over only up to twice your monthly allowance, so hoarding does not work. A heavy editing month on Plus can run dry mid-deck, and top-ups are priced to nudge you upward. The practical line: Plus suits a few decks a month; weekly producers land on Pro whether they planned to or not. ## Who should skip it

Skip Gamma if your decks must match a strict corporate template, because you will fight the layout system and lose. Skip it if your presentations are data-dense: dense tables, precise charts, and financial footnotes are manual work here. And skip it for anything confidential enough that routing content through third-party model APIs needs a legal review, though Gamma is SOC 2 Type II compliant, which covers most business cases.

## Alternatives

Beautiful.ai and Tome-style tools chase the same prompt-to-deck idea with different tradeoffs; Canva's AI features bolt generation onto a huge template library; and the incumbent path, Copilot in PowerPoint or Gemini in Slides, keeps everything inside the file format your company already uses. None match Gamma's draft speed. We compare the field in our roundup of [Gamma alternatives](/posts/gamma-alternatives-2026-best-ai-presentation-tools).

## The verdict

Use Gamma as a drafting engine, not a design department. Generate the structure and first pass in Gamma, then finish in your real slide tool when the audience matters. Stay on Free until you hit the branding or slide-count walls, move to Plus for regular internal work, and treat Pro as the tier for client-facing polish. The moment you catch yourself rerolling cards for an hour, switch to manual editing: that hour is what the subscription was supposed to save.
