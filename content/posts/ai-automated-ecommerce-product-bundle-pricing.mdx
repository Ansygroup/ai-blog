To truly](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-vs-gemini) appreciate how AI optimizes bundle pricing, we must look under the hood at the mathematical models it uses. Traditional pricing relies on simple linear demand curves. AI, however, uses non-linear multi-variable equations that factor in **Transaction Utility Theory** and **Joint Price Elasticity of Demand**.

Transaction Utility Theory, pioneered by economist Richard Thaler, suggests that consumers derive utility (satisfaction) from two sources: **acquisition utility** (the value](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-pricing-breakdown-2026)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-pricing-breakdown-2026)](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-pricing-breakdown](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/chatgpt-pricing-breakdown-2026)-2026) of the product relative to its price) and **transaction utility** (the perceived quality](https://ai-blog-ten-steel.vercel.app/posts/best-ai-software-testing-tools-2026) of the deal). AI models maximize transaction utility by highlighting the "savings" of the bundle, while simultaneously adjusting the base prices of the individual items to ensure the merchant’s acquisition [cost](/posts/cursor-ai-editor-review-2026) is fully covered.

The mathematical formula for joint price elasticity of a two-product bundle ($A$ and $B$) can be represented as:

$\epsilon_{AB} = \frac{\% \Delta Q_{(A+B)}}{\% \Delta P_{(A+B)}}$

Where:
* $\epsilon_{AB}$ is the price elasticity of the bundle.
* $\% \Delta Q_{(A+B)}$ is the percentage change in the quantity of bundles demanded.
* $\% \Delta P_{(A+B)}$ is the percentage change in the price of the bundle.
