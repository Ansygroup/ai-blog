Support is the one department where AI adoption is no longer optional theater: customers now expect an instant first answer at 2 a.m., and no team of humans can staff that. The question for 2026 is not whether to add an AI agent but which platform's pricing model and guardrails fit your ticket mix.

## Who this is for, and who should skip it

This is for support leads at companies with a real ticket queue, from a two-person SaaS team to a fifty-agent operation. Skip the AI-agent features if your tickets are mostly judgment calls (refund exceptions, enterprise escalations, regulated complaints). Automating the wrong queue produces a fast, confident, wrong first answer, which is worse than a slow right one.

## Intercom: the per-resolution bet

Intercom's Fin AI Agent is priced per outcome: $0.99 per resolution on all plans, with Fin Voice at $1.99 per voice outcome as of November 2026. Seat pricing starts at $19 per seat a month billed annually for Essential (marked down from $29), with Advanced at $85 and Expert at $132; Copilot for human agents is $29 per teammate a month. There are serious startup discounts for small early-stage companies.

The per-resolution model aligns cost with value in a way per-seat pricing never did: quiet month, small bill. The trap is the definition of "resolution." An AI that wears a customer down until they give up can look identical to one that solved the problem, unless you pair the metric with CSAT and recontact rates. Set a monthly spend cap, audit a sample of "resolved" conversations weekly, and treat containment rate and resolution rate as different numbers.

## Freshdesk: the freemium entry

Freshdesk's free plan covers a small team's basics, and paid tiers are $19, $55, and $89 per agent a month billed annually (Growth, Pro, Enterprise) as of November 2026. Freddy AI Copilot for agents runs $29 per agent a month, and additional AI agent sessions sell in blocks ($49 per 100 sessions). For a team graduating from a shared inbox, this is the cheapest credible path to AI-assisted support.

The failure mode: the free and cheap tiers get you in the door, and the AI features you actually want live one tier up. Price the tier with the features, not the tier with the headline price.

## Zendesk and Help Scout: the established and the human-first

Zendesk remains the default at scale, with Support Team at $19 per agent a month and Suite plans from $55 per agent a month as of November 2026; its AI features (answer bot, agent copilot, intent routing) are deep but accretive, spread across add-ons and tiers, so total cost needs a spreadsheet, not a glance. Help Scout is the pick for teams that want a human tone with AI assist rather than AI-first: a free plan for up to five users, Standard at $25 per user a month billed annually, and an AI-answers feature priced per resolution ($0.75) as of November 2026. ## The knowledge base decides everything

Every AI support agent is a search engine over your help center with a friendly voice. If your articles are stale, contradictory, or missing for your top twenty ticket reasons, the AI will confidently serve that staleness at scale. Before buying anything, fix the knowledge base: a month of article cleanup improves both your human and machine answers, and it is the only prep work every vendor's demo quietly assumes you did. Our walkthrough on [using AI for customer support](/posts/how-to-use-ai-for-customer-support-2026) includes that audit.

## What goes wrong in production

Three failure modes to plan for. First, invented policy: an AI agent with gaps in its sources will improvise a refund or warranty policy that does not exist, and customers screenshot it. Second, loop purgatory: a customer stuck repeating themselves to a bot that will not escalate churns publicly, on social media, with the transcript attached. Set a hard escalation trigger (frustration signals, second recontact, any legal or safety word) to a human. Third, silent drift: products change, the knowledge base lags, and answer quality decays a percent a week. Assign an owner to AI answer quality the way you assign one to uptime.

## How to decide

1. Want cost tied to deflected tickets? Intercom Fin at $0.99 per resolution, with a spend cap and weekly resolution audits.
2. Small team leaving a shared inbox? Freshdesk free, then Growth at $19 per agent.
3. Complex routing, SLAs, and scale? Zendesk Suite from $55 per agent, with a budgeted add-on map.
4. Brand voice is your moat? Help Scout with per-resolution AI answers.

Whichever you pick, launch the AI on your ten most-asked questions only, measure for a month, then widen. The goal is a smaller queue for your humans, not a bigger wall between you and your customers.

### What is the cheapest way to add AI to a small support team?

Freshdesk: a free plan for basics, then Growth at $19/agent/mo billed annually, with Freddy AI add-ons. Help Scout also has a free five-user plan with per-resolution AI answers at $0.75.

### Should AI answer all our tickets?

No. Launch on your ten most-asked questions, set a hard escalation trigger to humans for frustration signals or legal and safety words, and widen only after a month of measured results.
