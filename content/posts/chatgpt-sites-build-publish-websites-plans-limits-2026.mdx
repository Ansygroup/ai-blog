OpenAI's help center has a page called "Creating and using ChatGPT Sites". It explains a feature that lets you create, preview, publish and share interactive websites and lightweight apps from ChatGPT. This article summarizes that page, linked at the end. We have not used Sites, so everything here is OpenAI's description.

## What is ChatGPT Sites?

OpenAI says Sites lets you build things like issue dashboards and document finders, including Sites that use a visitor's own connected apps. Each visitor uses their own connected accounts and existing permissions. You can create Sites on ChatGPT web and in the desktop app. To start, select Work, describe the website you want, and either include the word "website" or mention @Sites.

## Who can use it?

Sites is available to ChatGPT Plus and Pro accounts, and to Business and Enterprise workspaces. In managed workspaces, settings and role permissions decide who can use it. In Enterprise, public publishing is off by default and an admin must turn it on.

## How does publishing work?

1. Describe the site and add the content, files, data and links ChatGPT should use.
2. Review the preview and ask for changes.
3. Deploy to generate a URL. New Sites are private by default.
4. Open the Site, check its full URL, then choose who can access it in Share.

Deploying changes to an existing Site updates the live version. OpenAI suggests saving a version first and deploying only after review. For personal accounts the default URL uses a random identifier, but if you make your profile public or change your username, the URL may include your username.

## What are the limits and costs?

- Building or editing a Site counts toward your Codex usage.
- Usage for running and hosting Sites, including traffic and storage, is separate.
- Charges for running and hosting are currently waived for Enterprise workspaces. Building still counts toward Codex usage, and usage and storage limits still apply.
- Plus and Pro have no fixed limit on the number of Sites, but shared usage and storage limits apply. Hitting a shared limit may stop you creating a new Site.
- Each Site's database can store up to 10 GB. File storage has no fixed limit per Site.

## What else does the page cover?

**Custom domains,** where available. You must already own the domain and be able to edit its DNS records. Sites does not register domains. You add the DNS records Sites gives you, including a verification TXT record, and wait for the status to update.

**Tunnels,** which are rolling out to eligible workspaces. They let a Site reach a service on a private network, such as an internal inventory API, without making it public. Your IT team runs OpenAI's tunnel client, and the service's own access controls still apply.

**Takedowns.** OpenAI says it may remove or restrict a Site if there is a risk it violates policies or platform safety requirements, and offers an appeal route through the email notification or an intake form.

## Our take

The useful details are in the limits. Hosting is metered apart from the Codex usage that building consumes, so a popular Site could cost more than the build did, outside Enterprise where charges are currently waived. The page also warns to use content you have permission to share and to pick an audience that matches it. The private-by-default setting helps, but the Share step is where mistakes happen, so check the audience before you publish.
