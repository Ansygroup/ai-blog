## FAQ

### How did AI agents manage to bypass MFA in 2026?

AI agents used LLM‑generated social engineering to harvest one‑time passwords from compromised email threads. By automating real‑time credential harvesting, they achieved a 42 % bypass rate against SMS‑based MFA, prompting a shift toward hardware tokens and AI‑enhanced anomaly detection.

### Are there any free tools that can detect AI‑generated phishing?

Open‑source projects like **PhishDetect** now incorporate a lightweight LLM (based on Hugging Face’s DistilGPT) to score email content. While it catches about 68 % of AI‑phishing attempts, it lacks the real‑time quarantine capabilities of enterprise solutions.

### What is the difference between “AI‑as‑a‑Service” (AaaS) and “Agent‑as‑a‑Service” (AaaS) used by attackers?

“AI‑as‑a‑Service” refers to legitimate cloud‑based AI APIs (e.g., OpenAI, Azure AI). “Agent‑as‑a‑Service,” in the malicious context, is a rented autonomous hacking bot that leverages those APIs to generate exploits on demand. The latter is sold on dark‑web marketplaces for $0.02 per generated payload.

### Can existing firewalls block AI‑generated attacks?

Traditional firewalls can block known IPs and ports but cannot inspect the semantic content of AI‑generated payloads. Next‑gen firewalls (NGFW) with integrated AI inspection, such as **Palo Alto Networks’ Cortex XDR**, can flag suspicious traffic patterns, but full protection requires endpoint‑level AI detection.

### How often should organizations update their AI‑defense models?

Vendor‑provided models are updated weekly, but organizations should retrain custom baselines every 30 days to incorporate new telemetry. In fast‑moving threat environments like 2026, a 2‑week retraining cycle can shave off up to 1.2 days of dwell time.

### Is there any regulation in Canada or the US addressing AI‑driven cyber attacks?

Both countries introduced the **AI Cybersecurity Act** (US, 2025) and **Canadian AI Security Framework** (2026), mandating risk assessments for AI‑enabled systems in critical sectors. Non‑compliance can result in fines up to $5 million for U.S. entities and CAD 4 million for Canadian firms.
