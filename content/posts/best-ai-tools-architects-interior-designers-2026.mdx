The gap between a render and a buildable drawing is where architecture practices lose money. A client approves a moody image, the contractor prices it, and the number comes back double. AI image tools make that gap wider, because they produce beautiful rooms that cannot be built. The tools worth buying are the ones that close it, or at least keep the client anchored to something real.

Prices were read on vendor pricing pages on November 27, 2026. Seat-based design software changes its bundle structure often, so confirm against the vendor before quoting a project fee that depends on it.

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concept models you will actually build from | SketchUp | Fast massing and a model that survives into documentation |
| Residential interiors with client-facing renders | Planner 5D | Photorealistic output and a price a small studio can carry |
| Presentation and marketing pages | Figma | Layout control for portfolios and proposals |
| Early mood and material exploration | Midjourney | Fast visual range, never a deliverable |
| Specification writing and schedules | ChatGPT or Claude | Drafts you edit, not documents you send |

## What AI changes in a design practice

It changes three things and leaves the rest alone. Concept iteration gets faster, because you can produce twenty directions before lunch. Client communication gets better, because a rendered image explains a decision faster than a plan. And the boring documentation layer, meaning schedules, specifications, and meeting notes, gets cheaper to produce.

What it does not change is coordination. Clash detection, code compliance, and buildability are still the work, and no image model understands a structural span.

## SketchUp: still the fastest way to think in 3D

SketchUp's volume pricing is published per seat: $50 per user a year for teams of 10 to 50, $45 for 51 to 200, and $40 for 201 to 500, with network lab licensing at $30 per user a year. Single-seat pricing sits below those tiers on the same page.

The reason it stays in the stack is speed of iteration. Massing a volume and walking a client through it takes minutes, and the model can be taken into documentation rather than thrown away. The limitation is that it is not a documentation tool, and practices that try to make it one spend their savings on fixing drawings.

## Planner 5D: renders a small studio can afford

Planner 5D lists a free plan at $0, Premium at $19.99 a month or $59.99 billed yearly, and Professional at $49.99 a month or $399.99 billed yearly. The Professional tier is the one aimed at designers and studios: unlimited watermark-free 4K renders, CAD export, custom model uploads, a specification and documents organizer, and 360 panoramas.

For residential interior work, that is a remarkable amount of output for the price. The catch is fidelity of the underlying geometry. It is a visualization environment first, so anything that needs to be dimensionally exact should be modeled in your documentation tool and rendered from there.

## Figma: the proposal layer

Figma's seat prices start at $3 a month for a collaboration seat and $16 for a full design seat, rising to $55 and $90 at the organization and enterprise tiers. Practices use it for the thing clients actually judge: the proposal, the portfolio page, and the presentation that wins the job.

It is not a CAD tool and should not be treated as one. Its value is that a studio's documents finally look like they came from the same practice.

## Midjourney and the render trap

Image models are excellent at material and mood, and terrible at architecture. They invent structural elements, move windows, and produce staircases that go nowhere. Used as an early exploration tool with a designer who knows what is real, they are useful. Used as a client deliverable, they create expectations nobody can build.

The workflow that holds up: generate for direction, then rebuild the chosen direction in a model, then render from the model. Never send the generated image as the promise.

## ChatGPT and Claude: specifications and meeting notes

Drafting a specification section, rewriting a contractor email so it is unambiguous, and turning a site meeting recording into a list of actions are all reasonable uses. The failure mode is specificity: a model will produce a product reference, a fire rating, or a code citation that reads correctly and is wrong.

Treat any number in a specification as unverified until someone opens the manufacturer's document. This is the same discipline as checking a dimension on site, applied to text.

## Where practices get this wrong

The first mistake is buying image tools before fixing the model. A practice with sloppy drawings does not become precise by rendering.

The second is presenting AI images as design intent. Clients remember the image, not the caveat, and the conversation about cost happens later when nobody is in the room.

The third is seat sprawl. Design software priced per seat quietly multiplies when you add contractors, and a five-person studio with eight licenses is paying for three people who log in twice a month.

## Who should skip this list

Skip the render tools if your work is commercial and documentation-heavy, where the value is in coordination rather than presentation. Skip image models entirely if your clients are contractors rather than homeowners. And skip any tool that cannot export geometry you can use downstream, because a beautiful picture that stops at the picture is a cost, not a capability.

## What to buy first

SketchUp for the model, Planner 5D or your renderer of choice for the client-facing image, Figma for the proposal, and one model subscription for text. That combination covers concept, presentation, and paperwork at a cost a two-person studio can justify on a single kitchen remodel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Planner 5D good enough for professional interior design work?

For residential visualization, yes. Its Professional plan at $49.99 a month or $399.99 a year includes unlimited 4K renders, CAD export and a specifications organizer. Anything dimensionally critical should still be modeled in your documentation tool.

### How much does SketchUp cost for a studio?

SketchUp publishes volume pricing at $50 per user a year for 10 to 50 seats, $45 for 51 to 200 seats and $40 for 201 to 500 seats, with network lab licensing at $30 per user a year.

### Can architects use AI renders as client deliverables?

Not directly. Image models invent structural elements and produce rooms that cannot be built, so use them for direction, then rebuild the chosen option in a model and render from that.
