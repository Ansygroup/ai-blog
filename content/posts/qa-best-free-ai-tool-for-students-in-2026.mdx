## How to Combine Free AI Tools for One Effective Study Session  

1. **Define the learning goal** – Write a one‑sentence objective (e.g., “Explain the causes of the French Revolution and draft a 500‑word essay with sources”).  
2. **Start with ChatGPT (free tier) for quick explanations** – Prompt it with “Explain the main causes of the French Revolution in simple terms.” Copy the bullet‑point summary into a new Google Docs or NotebookLM page.  
3. **Validate facts with Perplexity** – Highlight each bullet and ask Perplexity, “What primary sources support this cause?” Perplexity returns a list of citations and short excerpts; paste the URLs next to the corresponding bullet.  
4. **Draft the essay using Claude** – Feed Claude the outline you just built: “Write a 500‑word essay on the French Revolution using the points above and embed the cited sources as footnotes.” Claude’s output includes a polished narrative and correctly formatted footnotes.  
5. **Organize everything in NotebookLM** – Import the ChatGPT summary, Perplexity citations, and Claude essay into a single NotebookLM notebook. Use the built‑in “Highlight → Create Flashcard” feature on key dates and terms, then tag the notebook “History‑2026”.  
6. **Review and edit** – Run a quick check with ChatGPT: “Proofread the essay for logical flow and suggest any missing citations.” Incorporate the suggestions, then export the final version as PDF for submission.  

**Common pitfalls to avoid**  
- Relying on a single tool for all tasks; each free tier has usage caps and different strengths.  
- Ignoring the citation URLs from Perplexity; they are essential for academic honesty.  
- Over‑prompting Claude with overly detailed instructions; keep prompts concise to stay within the free token limit.  

By chaining these free services deliberately, a student can produce a research‑ready piece without spending a dime or hitting any one tool’s limits.
