## 5‑Step Workflow for a High‑CTR Thumbnail (Practical)

1. **Define the visual hook** – Write a concise prompt that isolates the core moment you want viewers to notice (e.g., “dramatic close‑up of a surprised gamer holding a glowing controller”). Keep the description under 30 words to avoid dilution.  
2. **Set the YouTube‑ready canvas** – Append `--ar 16:9` to force the 1280 × 720‑friendly aspect ratio. If you plan to reuse the asset across a series, add `--cref <character‑id>` and `--sref <style‑id>` so Midjourney keeps the same avatar and colour palette.  
3. **Generate background variations** – Run the prompt three times with different seeds (`--seed 1234`, `--seed 5678`, etc.). Pick the version that offers the cleanest negative space for text; a clutter‑free background raises click‑through rates.  
4. **Export and refine** – Download the PNG, open it in Canva (or Photoshop). Overlay bold, high‑contrast typography using the “Impact” or “Montserrat ExtraBold” fonts. Stick to a maximum of two words for the headline; the AI‑generated image already tells most of the story.  
5. **Test and iterate** – Use YouTube’s “Thumbnail A/B test” (available to verified channels) to compare the AI‑crafted version against a manually designed baseline. Record the CTR over a 48‑hour window; if the AI version lags, revisit step 2 and adjust the prompt’s lighting or colour temperature.

**Common pitfalls** –  
- Forgetting `--ar 16:9` results in awkward cropping on mobile.  
- Relying on Midjourney to render readable text; always add copy in a dedicated design tool.  
- Over‑specifying style tags, which can cause the model to “lock” into a stale look and reduce novelty.
