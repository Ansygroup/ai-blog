NotebookLM has a new name. Google called it NotebookLM until 16 July 2026 and now calls it Gemini Notebook, according to Fello AI. The slug of this article keeps the old name because people still search for it. As of November 2026, the comparison with ChatGPT's deep research comes down to one question: do you want answers from documents you already have, or a cited report built from the open web? Last tested: 27 November 2026. Limits and prices below come from third party reviews that read Google's and OpenAI's help and pricing pages, namely [Fello AI](https://felloai.com/notebooklm-vs-chatgpt/) (rechecked 23 November 2026) and [Promptline](https://promptline.net/guides/chatgpt-deep-research-limits) (checked 9 November 2026). OpenAI's own pricing page loaded for us without dollar amounts.

## The verdict first

| Scenario | Winner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Questions about your own PDFs and notes | Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM) | Free holds 50 sources per notebook, against 5 files in a free ChatGPT Project |
| A cited report from the open web | ChatGPT deep research | One task is a research run with an editable plan and a cited report |
| Free plan for document work | Gemini Notebook | 50 sources and 100 notebooks at $0 |
| Knowing your exact allowance | Neither | Google uses multipliers and OpenAI prints words, not counts |
| Adding web sources to your documents | Gemini Notebook | Its Deep Research feature finds web sources to add to a notebook |
| Heavy research volume | ChatGPT Pro | OpenAI lists maximum deep research on Pro |

## What each one limits

| Tier | Gemini Notebook | ChatGPT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | 50 sources per notebook, 100 notebooks | 5 files per project, limited deep research |
| Entry paid | Google AI Plus $4.99 a month: 100 sources, 200 notebooks, 2x usage | Go $8 a month: 25 files per project, limited deep research |
| Mid paid | Google AI Pro $19.99 a month: 300 sources, 500 notebooks, 4x usage | Plus $20 a month: 25 files per project, expanded deep research |
| Top paid | Ultra $99.99 or $199.99: 500 or 600 sources | Pro from $100: 40 files per project, maximum deep research |

Since 2 September 2026, Fello AI reports, Google counts usage as a compute allowance shared by chats and studio features that refreshes every 5 hours up to a weekly limit. Older guides that quote 50 chats a day are out of date.

OpenAI no longer prints a task count for deep research on Free, Go, Plus or Pro, per Promptline. Older articles quoting 25 tasks a month on Plus use a figure that no longer appears on OpenAI's current pages. The counter inside ChatGPT is the only reliable number, and allowances reset 30 days after your first use.

## How they work

Gemini Notebook answers from the sources you put in. Its Deep Research feature can search the web for new sources to add to a notebook, and the chat can run code over a spreadsheet you added. ChatGPT's deep research takes a goal, proposes a plan you can edit and returns a cited report. Work plans can go past their limits with credits at about 50 credits per task.

## Sharing, uploads and notes

Fello AI reports that a free ChatGPT Project can be shared with 5 collaborators, rising to 10 on Go or Plus and 100 on Pro, and that a single ChatGPT upload is capped at 10 files at a time. Linked Google Drive files and Slack channels count against a separate project limit that OpenAI gives no number for. On the Google side, notes you save as sources have their own cap of 500 per notebook, or 600 on Ultra, and do not count toward the source totals above. Studio outputs such as video overviews and slide decks draw on the same compute allowance as chat, and when it runs out the web version lets you queue an item to generate later.

## Cost per useful answer

Plans buy capacity more than new features for document work. A Google AI Pro subscription at $19.99 a month includes 300 source notebooks, and a ChatGPT Plus subscription at $20 includes 25 file projects, plus expanded deep research. If your work is a pile of papers, the source cap is the number to compare. If it is ad hoc research, compare how often you hit the deep research counter.

## Where each one fails

Gemini Notebook fails when you have no documents. It is a tool for sources you supply, and a notebook is capped by source count, so a huge library needs several notebooks. ChatGPT deep research fails on transparency: the allowance is described only as limited, expanded or maximum, and availability varies by country. Its Projects also cap files at 25 on Plus, so a big document set will not fit.

## Who should skip which

Skip Gemini Notebook if your question needs fresh web research with no documents of your own. Skip ChatGPT deep research if you want to ask follow up questions of 40 PDFs. ## Test it yourself

1. Put five PDFs you know well into a free Gemini Notebook and ask three questions with known answers.
2. Run a deep research task in ChatGPT on a topic you know and check every cited link.
3. Count wrong or missing citations in each.
4. Check the usage counter after each run.
5. Ask both a question the sources do not answer and see whether they say so.

## Which should you pick

Use Gemini Notebook for your own documents and ChatGPT deep research for new topics. If you pay for only one, pick by your most common task, not by the headline. Check the live limits before you pay, since Google said its limits are subject to change.
