The week of November 23 delivered the strangest AI-business story of the year, a genuine model release pile-up, and a string of enterprise moves that matter more than their headlines. Seven stories, each verified and dated, with why they matter beyond the announcement.

## 1. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO after White House input

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on November 23 to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO, and director, eleven days after he resigned amid scrutiny over his personal relationships and an ongoing investigation into whether a foreign intelligence service targeted him. Reuters reported that President Trump and Vice President Vance personally intervened, viewing Huang as essential to the US position in AI, and Nvidia says its board investigation continues. Nvidia shares surged 31 percent on the news, their biggest gain since 2003. Whatever you think of the governance, the episode shows how tightly AI chip supply and national policy are now fused. Sources: [AI Affairs](https://www.aiaffairs.com/audio/2026-11-24/) and [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-november-24-2026/), November 24, 2026.

## 2. OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, plus a sales suite

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17, positioning Astra for demanding projects and Sol as the efficient everyday model, alongside ChatGPT for Sales with a Sales plugin and more than 30 partner integrations. The release lands days before GPT-6 Sol and Luna on November 22, a rapid cadence even by this year's standards. For builders, the practical read: model selection now needs a quarterly review, not an annual one. Source: [Pras Intelligence](https://prasintelligence.com/en/articles/update-gemini-claude-openai-november-2026), November 21, 2026.

## 3. Google launches Gemini 4 Neon

Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, a frontier model it ranks third overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, with a one-million-token context window and pricing at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output. Neon rolls out across the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Antigravity, and the Gemini app for paid subscribers. Google's family strategy, Carbon, Hydrogen, Neon in one month, mirrors OpenAI's tiering and confirms the market has settled on good-better-best model ladders. Source: [Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/), November 18, 2026.

## 4. Z.ai's GLM-5.4 reruns its layers instead of growing them

Chinese lab Z.ai released GLM-5.4 on November 24, its first flagship built on a Loop Transformer that runs the same 28-layer block twice per token, reusing weights rather than adding parameters. Adapted from a pre-trained GLM-5 at 750 billion parameters, Z.ai claims it approaches models of two to three trillion parameters, though the published scores come from a smaller 30-billion test model. Open weights are planned after safety hardening. If the loop approach holds up at scale, it pressures the assumption that capability requires parameter count, which matters for everyone's compute bills. Source: [AI Affairs](https://www.aiaffairs.com/audio/2026-11-24/), November 24, 2026.

## 5. Amazon buys Decart's optimization team in a reported $7.5 billion deal

Amazon agreed to acquire Decart's AI optimization technology and research team, a deal reported at $7.5 billion, aimed at running models more efficiently on Amazon's Trainium processors. Efficiency acquisitions at this price tell you where the bottleneck is: not training bigger models, but serving them cheaply enough to compete with Nvidia-based rivals. Source: [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-november-24-2026/), November 24, 2026.

## 6. Anthropic lets enterprises keep their own compliance records

Anthropic plans to let Mythos and Fable enterprise customers store their required 30-day prompt-and-response records in their own cloud environments, with customers controlling the encryption keys, Bloomberg reported. Anthropic's software would scan for signs of sophisticated cyberattacks every 15 minutes without accessing conversation content. This is the compliance architecture regulated industries asked for, and it removes one of the last structural objections to deploying frontier models in finance and health. Separately on November 24, Anthropic launched Claude Ship for GitHub, with code review, PR stewardship, and rule-based approvals for Team and Enterprise plans. Source: [AI Affairs](https://www.aiaffairs.com/audio/2026-11-24/), November 24, 2026.

## 7. Cisco and OpenAI open-source Shirudo, an eBPF shield for unpatched services

Cisco and OpenAI launched Shirudo on November 24, an open-source project that places temporary, targeted shields around running Linux services while patches are prepared, using eBPF programs that security teams approve and cryptographically verify. In a controlled test, OpenAI's Codex agent generated a shield that blocked a Java4Shell exploit attempt against a live Elasticsearch service. AI agents writing mitigations for vulnerabilities, with humans approving deployment, is the defensive use case security teams have been promised for two years. Source: [AI Affairs](https://www.aiaffairs.com/audio/2026-11-24/), November 24, 2026.
