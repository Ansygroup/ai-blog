Both surveys are self-reported, and JetBrains reweights its sample to represent the global developer population.

AI coding tools are no longer an experiment that a few engineers run on the side. The [open](/posts/top-hugging-face-alternatives-2026) question for a company is no longer whether developers use them. It is which tools, under what [policy](/posts/best-ai-tools-automated-privacy-policy-generation-2026), and at what cost.

The two surveys ask different questions of different samples, so the 90% and the 66% are not in conflict. JetBrains counts weekly use of agents among professional developers. Stack Overflow counts a broader group of technologists and a broader answer set. Quote one or the other, with its definition, never an average of both.

## The governance gap

Here is what no vendor page tells you. Stack Overflow found that 30% of developers say AI use at work is optional and left to the individual, while only 24% say their organisation publishes approved tools or an official AI policy. Usage has outrun policy. If you manage engineers, the chances are [good](/posts/best-ai-tools-notebooklm-for-nonprofits-2026-ultimate-guide) that some of them are already using a tool you have not approved.

Trust is conditional. The same survey says nearly half of respondents trust AI output when they can verify it, and only 6.6% are prepared to trust it for important decisions. Developers who switched tools mostly did so because the output was better (25%), and 11% because their organisation required it.

## What [the paid](/posts/copy-ai-vs-free-version) [plans](/posts/copy-ai-pricing-breakdown-2026) look [like](/posts/best-ai-tools-grammarly-for-accountants-2026)

Pricing changes often, so confirm on the vendor page. As of our November 2026 check of the public pages:

- **GitHub Copilot:** Free at $0, Pro at $10 per month, Pro+ at $39, Max at $100. Each plan includes a monthly allowance of GitHub AI Credits, where one credit is $0.01. Code completions do not use credits, while chat and agent work do. On Business and Enterprise, admins set usage [limits](/posts/copilot-free-tier-limits-2026) and decide whether extra paid usage is allowed. We did not capture the Business and Enterprise prices.
- **Cursor:** Free, then individual tiers from $8 to $200 per month, Teams from $30 per [user](/posts/chatgpt-best-settings), and Enterprise with [custom](/posts/auto-ml-tutorial) pricing.

The shift to credit-based billing matters for budgeting. A flat seat price no longer fixes the bill when agent sessions run long. Ask your vendor how overage is capped before you roll out to a [team](/posts/descript-for-teams-2026-review). If you are also weighing chat products for the wider company, see [ChatGPT Team vs Enterprise](/posts/chatgpt-team-vs-enterprise-which-openai-plan-is-right-for-yo).

## A rollout [checklist](/posts/top-ai-tools-gdpr-checklist-2026)

1. Publish an approved tool list and a one-page policy. Only 24% of organisations do, so this puts you ahead.
2. Set a spending cap per developer, and an alert threshold.
3. Require review of agent-written changes by a person, in [line](/posts/tutorial-ai-driven-ab-testing-email-subject-lines-2026) with the verify-first attitude developers already report.
4. Decide what code and [data](/posts/ai-for-generating-schema-markup-for-seo-2026-tutorial) may be sent to which vendor.
5. Measure something other than usage: review time, defects found after release, and time to merge.

## Our judgment

The adoption question is settled, and the [management](/posts/hugging-face-for-teams-2026-review-best-collaborative-ai) question is not. If your engineers are among the 90% using agents weekly, a tool ban is unlikely to hold, and an approved list with spending limits will serve you better. Pick one or two tools, pilot them for a month with real metrics, and treat survey share as a reason to include a tool in the pilot, not as proof it is [the best](/posts/best-ai-tools-accountants-bookkeeping-2026) fit for your codebase.

### How can enterprises ensure [compliance](/posts/best-ai-tools-automated-privacy-policy-generation-2026) when using AI coding assistants?
Enterprises should adopt a compliance [framework](/posts/langchain-review-2026-complete-ai-framework-analysis) that includes: (1) vetting the vendor’s data‑[privacy](/posts/best-ai-tools-automated-privacy-policy-generation-2026) policies, (2) configuring the tool to run in a private or on‑premise environment, (3) establishing code‑review gates that flag AI‑generated snippets, (4) maintaining an audit trail of AI suggestions, and (5) updating internal policies to cover licensing, copyright, and security implications of AI‑generated code.

### What are the common challenges when scaling AI coding tools across a large development team?
Scaling challenges include inconsistent adoption across teams, managing API‑rate limits, ensuring the AI [model](/posts/auto-ml-tutorial) respects corporate coding standards, handling data residency requirements, and preventing “code drift” where AI suggestions introduce hidden dependencies. Address these by providing unified onboarding, setting up centralized policy enforcement, monitoring usage metrics, and regularly updating the [model with](/posts/auto-ml-tutorial) internal codebases.

### How do policies [need](/posts/copy-ai-free-tier-limits-2026) to change to address the risks of AI‑generated code in the enterprise?
Policies should be updated to: (a) define acceptable [use cases](/posts/langchain-vs-chatgpt-2026) for AI‑generated code, (b) require mandatory peer review of AI suggestions, (c) outline data‑handling rules for code [prompts](/posts/copy-ai-prompting-guide-2026), (d) specify licensing checks for third‑party snippets, (e) incorporate security scanning of AI‑produced code, and (f) establish incident‑response procedures for any AI‑related vulnerabilities or compliance breaches.
