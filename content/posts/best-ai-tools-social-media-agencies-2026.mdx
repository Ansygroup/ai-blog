The moment a social media manager picks up a fourth client account, the job stops being about posting and becomes about context switching. Five brands, five voices, five approval chains, and a client who wants to see next month's calendar by Friday. The tools below are ranked by how much of that switching they remove, not by how clever their AI demo looks.

Prices were checked on vendor pricing pages on November 27, 2026. Seat and channel counts change often, so check the current plan grid before you commit a team to one.

## The short answer

| Scenario | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo manager with 3 to 6 profiles | Buffer | Cheap per channel, clean queue |
| Agency with approvals and several seats | Hootsuite | Review and routing are built in |
| Heavy short-form video output | OpusClip | Turns long recordings into clips fast |
| Visual consistency across clients | Canva | Brand kits per client in one account |
| Caption drafts and reply tone | Claude or ChatGPT | Fast first drafts in a set voice |

## Why per-seat and per-channel pricing decides this

Two pricing models dominate, and they fail in opposite directions. Buffer charges per channel, listed at $5 a month for one channel and $10 for the next tier. Hootsuite charges per user, with Standard at $99 per user per month on annual billing, Professional at $199, and Advanced at $399. Month-to-month rates on the same page run higher, $149, $299, and $499.

A solo manager with eight client profiles pays Buffer for eight channels and pays Hootsuite for one seat. A five-person agency with two profiles each pays Hootsuite five times and Buffer twice. Neither is wrong. They are just built for different shapes of business, and the wrong shape costs several hundred dollars a month for nothing.

## Buffer: the one that stays out of the way

Buffer's pitch is restraint. You queue posts, it publishes them, and the AI sits in the corner suggesting a caption rewrite rather than taking over the composer. For a manager running several small accounts, that is the correct amount of help.

The limitation is collaboration. Approvals, client review links, and comment threads are thinner than what an agency with a demanding client will want. If your workflow includes a client who marks up a calendar in three colors, Buffer will make you export a spreadsheet.

## Hootsuite: approvals, at a price

Hootsuite's Advanced tier is where the approval workflow lives: review and approve content, assign and route messages, and measure team throughput. That is the feature an agency actually needs, and it is why Hootsuite keeps winning accounts it should lose on price.

The trap is the seat model. Every freelancer you add to review one client's calendar is another $399 a month at the Advanced tier on annual billing. Agencies routinely discover this after they have onboarded a contractor for a single account. Budget for the seats before you promise the workflow.

## Later: the visual planner

Later's strength is the grid view, which is still the fastest way to explain a visual strategy to a client who does not read captions. Its add-on pricing is granular: extra users at $5 a month, extra social sets at $11.25 a month, and extra AI credits at $3.75 for 100 credits a month. Granular pricing is honest, but it means the sticker price is rarely the invoice.

## OpusClip: the video multiplier

OpusClip is listed at $15 a month for the entry paid tier and $29 for the next one up. It takes a long recording and returns short clips with captions, reframed for vertical. For managers whose clients want Reels, Shorts, and TikToks from one podcast or webinar, it removes the most tedious hour of the week.

Two things go wrong. Credit limits arrive faster than expected once you start exporting variants, and the auto-selected clip boundaries are frequently slightly off the joke or the point. Plan on a manual trim pass, and treat the tool as a first cut rather than a finished edit.

## Canva: the brand kit layer

Canva's real value to a social manager is not image generation. It is that each client's fonts, colors, and logo sit in a separate brand kit, so a junior teammate cannot accidentally post in the wrong palette. For teams, the per-seat cost is the thing to model, since every designer and every account coordinator needs a login.

## Claude and ChatGPT: caption drafting

Both are good at the part of the job nobody bills for: turning a client's rambling voice note into six caption options in the right register. The workflow that holds up is a short style brief per client, saved as a reusable prompt, with two examples of posts the client liked.

The failure mode is sameness. Ask either model for captions for five clients in one sitting and you will get five variations of the same voice. Draft one client at a time, and rewrite the opening line yourself.

## What these tools still cannot do

None of them fix the two structural problems of the job. The first is that platform APIs and posting limits change, and a scheduler that misses a scheduled post is worse than no scheduler. Keep a manual check on the first post of any new campaign.

The second is approval lag. The bottleneck in agency social work is a client who replies on Thursday to a Monday draft. No amount of AI shortens that, though a shared calendar link with a visible deadline does more than most features.

## Who should skip the expensive tiers

Skip Hootsuite Advanced if you are a solo manager. Skip Buffer if you need formal approvals. Skip OpusClip if your clients do not want video. And skip any annual plan in your first month with a new client, because losing that client should not lock you into a tool for a year.

## The stack I would run

For a solo manager: Buffer for publishing, Canva for assets, OpusClip for video, and one model subscription for captions. For a small agency: Hootsuite Advanced for the accounts that need approvals, Buffer for the ones that do not, and a shared brand-voice document that every model prompt points back to.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Buffer or Hootsuite cheaper for a small agency?

It depends on shape. Buffer charges per channel at $5 to $10 a month, so eight client profiles cost far less than five Hootsuite seats at $99 to $399 per user per month. Hootsuite wins when you need formal approvals and message routing across a team.

### Do social media managers still need a scheduler in 2026?

Yes. Native scheduling tools are improving, but they do not give you a cross-client calendar or an approval trail. A scheduler is the audit record that protects you when a client asks who approved a post.

### How accurate is OpusClip at picking clip moments?

Useful as a first cut, not a final edit. It reliably finds speaker segments, but the boundaries often land slightly before or after the point, so plan a manual trim pass before anything publishes.
