Enforcement of the EU AI Act did not start on one day. It has been switching on in stages since 2025, and 2026 added two more steps: the Commission's power to fine providers of general-purpose AI models and the transparency duties of Article 50, both from 2 August 2026. At the same time, a law called the Digital Omnibus on AI pushed the high-risk rules back to December 2027 and August 2028. This explainer sets out which parts apply today, which are delayed and what lands on 2 December 2026. It is general information, not legal advice. Last updated: 27 November 2026.

## What the Digital Omnibus changed

The Omnibus was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026. It amends the AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. It is widely called a postponement, and for high-risk systems that is right. Logan Partners points out the description is incomplete: much of the Act already applies, and the Omnibus adds new prohibitions and stronger enforcement.

The high-risk dates moved from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems, which cover areas such as recruitment, credit scoring, education, biometrics and law enforcement. Products under Annex I, such as machinery, toys and medical devices, moved from 2 August 2027 to 2 August 2028. Logan Partners notes the legislator chose fixed dates and did not tie them to standards, so businesses should not plan on a further extension.

## What is already enforceable

Providers of general-purpose AI models have been under Chapter V since 2 August 2025. They must keep technical documentation, have a copyright policy and publish a summary of training content. The Commission's power to fine these providers began on 2 August 2026.

Article 50 transparency applies from 2 August 2026. Users must be told when they are talking to an AI system, synthetic content must be marked in a machine readable format and deepfakes must be disclosed. Under new Article 111(4), generative systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to comply with the marking duty.

## What starts on 2 December 2026

The Omnibus adds two prohibitions to Article 5 from 2 December 2026. One covers AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic intimate or sexually explicit images of an identifiable person without consent. The other covers AI generated child sexual abuse material. Under new Article 5(1a), a provider may be caught even if the system was not designed for this purpose.

## Stronger enforcement

Logan Partners says breaches of new value chain obligations in Article 25 may be fined up to 15 million euros or 3% of turnover. The AI Office gets exclusive competence over certain systems, including those built on a general-purpose model where the same provider makes both, and systems that are part of very large online platforms or search engines. It also gets inspection powers and may impose periodic penalty payments.

## What could go wrong

The common mistake is reading "delay" as "nothing applies". Transparency, GPAI duties and the prohibitions are live. Another mistake is treating 2 December 2026 as a general deadline: it is specific to the marking grace period and the two new prohibitions.

## Who should skip it

Skip this if you only use AI tools casually and never publish AI generated content to the public. Deployers and providers should read the full texts. Dazr's advice for most organizations is an inventory of AI tools in use, a prohibitions check, a recorded AI literacy briefing and Article 50 checks.

## Test it yourself

1. List every AI tool your team uses.
2. Mark each one that talks to the public or generates images, audio, video or text you publish.
3. Check whether that output is labeled in a machine readable way.
4. Check your use cases against the Article 5 prohibitions.
5. Read Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 on EUR-Lex for the exact wording.

## Verdict

Enforcement has begun, but only part of it. Transparency and GPAI rules apply now, and the high-risk regime waits until 2027 and 2028. Check your marking duty before 2 December 2026. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Did the EU delay the AI Act?

Only the high-risk rules. Annex III systems now apply from 2 December 2027 and Annex I products from 2 August 2028. Transparency and general-purpose AI duties still apply.

### What starts on 2 December 2026?

Two new Article 5 prohibitions and the end of the grace period for marking duties on generative systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026.

### Can the Commission fine AI model providers?

Yes. Its power to fine providers of general-purpose AI models has applied since 2 August 2026.
