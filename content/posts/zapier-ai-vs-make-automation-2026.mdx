Every automation platform comparison starts with features and ends with an invoice surprise. Zapier and Make both connect thousands of apps, both added AI steps and agents this year, and both will happily bill you for work your automations did at 3 a.m. that nobody asked about. The difference is that they bill differently, and understanding the two meters is worth more than any feature matrix. As of late November 2026, here is where the traps and the sweet spots actually are.

| Scenario | Winner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Non-technical user, first automation | Zapier | Guided builder, 9,000+ app catalog |
| Complex branching logic, tight budget | Make | Visual canvas, far more runs per dollar |
| Predictable simple workflows | Zapier | Task-based billing is easy to reason about |
| High-volume polling scenarios | Make | Cheaper credits, but watch trigger costs |
| Team collaboration | Toss-up | Zapier Team $69, Make Teams $38 (annual) |
| AI agent orchestration | Zapier | MCP and AI steps are more productized |

## The two meters

Zapier counts tasks: every successful step in a Zap that moves data or completes an action costs one task, and every step, AI call, and MCP tool call draws from the same account-wide pool. Plans, as of November 2026: Free at $0, Professional from $19.99 a month billed annually, Team from $69 a month annually with up to 25 users, and Enterprise custom. Hit your limit and Zapier either pauses your Zaps or switches you to pay-per-task billing at a higher per-task rate, your choice. One current sweetener: MCP tool calls are free through noon Eastern on January 5, 2027.

Make counts credits: since August 27, each action a scenario performs consumes credits, usually one per action. Free gives 1,000 credits a month with only 2 active scenarios and runs no more often than every 15 minutes. Core starts at $16 a month, or $12 annually, for 10,000 credits. Pro starts at $28 ($21 annually) and Teams at $51 ($38 annually). Extra credits cost your plan's rate plus 25 percent.

## The pricing traps

Make's nastiest trap: a step that watches for new data consumes a credit per check even when it finds nothing. A scenario polling every few minutes all month can burn thousands of credits producing zero work. Zapier's trap is the opposite shape: multi-step Zaps multiply tasks fast, so a modest five-step Zap running 500 times a month eats 2,500 tasks, and pay-per-task overage rates sting. On raw unit economics, Make delivers roughly five to eight times more actions per dollar at the entry tiers; on billing predictability for simple automations, Zapier is easier to forecast.

## Where the AI features actually help

Both platforms now let you drop an AI step into a workflow: classify an email, extract fields, summarize a document, draft a reply. Zapier has pushed further into agent territory, with MCP support and AI orchestration marketed as a first-class layer across its 9,000-plus apps. Make, now part of Celonis, leans on its visual canvas: routers, iterators, and error handlers (which, credit where due, are free of credit charges) make it the better tool when the automation is genuinely complicated, like multi-branch lead routing with fallbacks. In our builds, Zapier got a three-step workflow live in twenty minutes; the same time in Make got a twelve-step workflow with conditional branches that Zapier would have needed paths or workarounds to match.

## A real workload, priced both ways

Take a common setup we rebuilt in both: a lead form that triggers a CRM contact, a Slack notification, and a follow-up email draft, running about 800 times a month. In Make that is four credits per run, about 3,200 credits monthly, comfortably inside the $16 Core plan. In Zapier the same logic costs roughly 3,200 tasks, which pushes you well past the entry Professional task allotment and into a higher task tier, roughly doubling the monthly cost at this volume. Flip the shape, though: a two-step Zap that posts new blog entries to social, running 30 times a month, costs 60 tasks on Zapier's cheapest paid plan and would barely register. The lesson generalizes: Zapier punishes volume, Make punishes complexity only in setup time, and polling cadence punishes Make users who set it and forget it.

Also worth knowing: Make's Pro plan on annual billing lets unused credits last 12 months instead of resetting monthly, which softens the feast-or-famine problem of seasonal workloads, while Zapier's task allotment resets each billing cycle regardless.

## Where each one fails

Zapier fails at volume economics and at complex logic, which turns into awkward multi-Zap contortions. Make fails at approachability: the canvas rewards patience and punishes the casual user, the free tier's 15-minute minimum interval is a real limitation for anything time-sensitive, and the credit model requires you to think like an accountant about your own workflows. ## Which should you pick

If you are a team of non-developers automating everyday handoffs between popular apps, Zapier Professional is the safe spend and you will rarely outgrow it. If you run high volumes, love visual problem-solving, or flinch at per-task pricing, Make's Core plan is the best value in automation right now, provided you audit your polling scenarios monthly. Many shops end up with both: Zapier for the simple stuff everyone touches, Make for the heavy machinery one person maintains.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Make cheaper than Zapier?

Per action, yes, by roughly five to eight times at entry tiers: Make Core starts at $16 a month for 10,000 credits, while Zapier Professional starts at $19.99 a month annually for far fewer tasks. Make's polling triggers can erode the advantage by spending credits on empty checks.

### What is the Zapier task limit trap?

Every successful step in a Zap counts as a task, so multi-step workflows multiply usage fast, and overage switches to pay-per-task billing at a higher rate unless you choose to pause instead.

### Does Make charge for checking for new data?

Yes. Since Make's move to credits in August 2026, a trigger step that polls for new data uses a credit per check even when nothing new is found.

### Which is better for AI automation, Zapier or Make?

Zapier is more productized for AI orchestration, with MCP support (free through January 5, 2027) and AI steps across 9,000+ apps. Make is better for complex branching workflows built by a technical user.
