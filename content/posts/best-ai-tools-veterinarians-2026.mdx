Most vets finish the last appointment at five and then spend two hours writing notes. That unpaid evening is the cost that drives people out of clinical practice, and it is the single task where AI tools have the clearest case. An ambient scribe listens to the consult and drafts the record. The vet reads it, corrects it and signs it. The question is which tools do that safely, and what they cost.

Prices below were read on vendor pages on November 27, 2026. Last tested: November 27, 2026.

## Scribenote: the veterinary-specific scribe

Scribenote's page title reads "Free Plan or $79/mo". The free plan was $0 with unlimited note usage on standard AI models and standard transcription quality, unlimited care team members, and PIMS integration listed. A Pro plan and an enterprise tier for multi-clinic groups sit above it, and every sign-up gets a 2-week Pro trial with no credit card. The page shows a SOC 2 badge and lists an opt-out of data sharing for model improvements among its features, so check which plan includes it.

That last point matters. Read what happens to consult audio and text on the free plan before you record a client conversation. Ask whether data is used to train models, whether you can delete recordings, and where the data is stored.

Where it fails: names, doses and species. A scribe can mishear a drug name or a weight. Treat every dose and every number in a draft as unverified until you have checked it against the patient record and your formulary.

## Claude for client-facing writing

Claude Pro was $17 per month annual or $20 monthly. The best use is turning your clinical shorthand into a plain discharge sheet: what was done, what to watch for at home, when to call. Paste only the clinical facts, with no owner name or phone number. Ask for a reading level around grade 7.

It also helps with the awkward messages: a reminder for an overdue booster, a polite note about an unpaid balance, a condolence message after a loss. For the last one, write the first sentence yourself.

Where it fails: medical advice. A chat tool can produce plausible but wrong guidance on dosing or toxicity. Do not use it to decide treatment, and never let a client-facing message state a diagnosis you have not made. ## Calendly for recheck bookings

Calendly listed paid tiers at $10 and $16 per seat. A booking link in a discharge message lets owners schedule the recheck at once, which improves follow-through on wound checks and dental cleanings. Set separate appointment types by length, so a recheck does not take a 40-minute slot.

## What the vendor pages do not say

The risk is not that the note is wrong. It is that the note is plausible. A draft that reads fluently invites a quick skim and a signature. Build a habit: check three things in every note before signing, which are the weight, the drug and dose, and the plan. Those are the lines that cause harm when wrong.

Consent is the second point. Tell clients you use a recording tool, and put it on a sign at the front desk and in your intake form. Rules on recording differ by location, so check yours rather than assuming a sign is enough.

## A two-week test that gives a real answer

Pick ten routine appointments, five written by hand and five with the scribe. Record the minutes from the end of the consult to a signed note. Then have a colleague read both sets and mark errors without knowing which is which. You want two numbers: time saved per note, and errors per ten notes. If the errors rise, the time saved is false economy. If both improve, you have an argument for the practice owner that rests on your own clinic's data, not a vendor claim.

## Who should skip this list

A clinic with a records system that already includes a scribe should test that first, since a built-in tool avoids a second data flow. Very low-volume practices may find the setup effort larger than the time saved.

## What to buy first

Use Scribenote's 2-week Pro trial on one vet and one service type, such as routine wellness visits. Time the note-writing before and after. If the evening hour drops to 20 minutes, extend it to the team. Keep Claude Pro for discharge sheets and reminders, and add Calendly only when recheck no-shows become a measurable problem.
